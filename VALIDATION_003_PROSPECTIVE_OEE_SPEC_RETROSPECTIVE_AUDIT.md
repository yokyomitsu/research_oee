## 4. Retrospective compatibility versus compliance

The retrospective question is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Can the required information be reconstructed from the surviving}
\atop
\text{publication/archive, and if not, does the absence materially limit today's claim audit?}
}
$$

It is not whether a historical paper complied with a 2026 checklist. A semantic description distributed across a paper, source release, supplement, and later archive can be scientifically sufficient even when no manifest existed.

## 5. Historical fairness principles

1. Scientific identity is semantic before it is technological. A rule table, initial configuration, and adequate methods can identify an old system without Git, Docker, JSON, hashes, or a DOI dataset.
2. Missing convenience infrastructure is not a scientific defect unless its absence prevents reconstruction of a claim-relevant relation.
3. Publication validity and present exemplar auditability are separate. `PARTIALLY AUDITABLE` never means the original publication was invalid.
4. Claim proportionality governs retention. Full state histories are unnecessary when a smaller record audits the claim.
5. Historical impossibility is used sparingly. Scientifically essential ancestry, intervention, or selection information is not excused merely because it was inconvenient to preserve.

## 6. Missing-information taxonomy

Every audited field uses exactly one of:

- **AVAILABLE** — directly present;
- **RECONSTRUCTABLE** — reliably recoverable from publication, code, or data;
- **PARTIALLY RECONSTRUCTABLE** — important parts recoverable but ambiguity remains;
- **MISSING** — absent;
- **NOT APPLICABLE** — irrelevant to the claim/system; or
- **HISTORICALLY IMPRACTICAL** — unreasonable to retain at the historical time or scale.

`HISTORICALLY IMPRACTICAL` applies mainly to event-complete high-volume state retention, not to compact semantic provenance.

## 7. Scientific-consequence taxonomy

Each partial or missing field receives one of:

- **NO MATERIAL IMPACT** — the relevant result remains auditable;
- **REDUCED AUDITABILITY** — independent reconstruction is harder while the claim remains interpretable;
- **MATERIAL CLAIM LIMITATION** — an important claim component cannot be strongly validated; or
- **CLAIM-BLOCKING** — the present exemplar-level claim cannot be established from surviving evidence.

Consequences concern the retrospective exemplar claim, not original publication validity.

## 14. Measure-semantics audit

EX2 identifies the main interpretation burden in every relevant control:

- **EVO-D:** genotype, reproduction, and competitive persistence support Darwinian evolution, not OEE.
- **STR-AFN:** generic activity locates change; reaction semantics plus N/F/H/A/C support finite AFN. Activity alone is insufficient.
- **AVI-C:** environmental genomic information is not genome length and remains bounded by the declared genome/environment.
- **AVI-EQU:** EQU is a complex rewarded computation inside a supplied task family, not continuing functional-class creation.
- **AEV-C:** active functional-proteome accumulation and retention are not an indefinitely advancing frontier. `Complexity ratchet` does not imply S3/S4 or I.
- **GEB-I:** distinct active developmental genes are not neural allowance, behavior count, or one-run infinite growth. I4 is a finite, qualified resource-path result.
- **GEN-A:** positive new evolutionary activity is not automatically AFN, complexity growth, or broad OEE.
- **FL-E:** visual morphology, parameter diversity, and activity do not by themselves establish organismal complexity or AFN.
- **OUT-HR:** causal multiscale self-replication is not heritable adaptive novelty or complexity OEE.

**Interpretation.** Measure semantics is not bureaucratic metadata. In GEN-A, FL-E, AEV-C, and GEB-I it is the principal defense against claim inflation.

## 32. Required / Recommended / Optional calibration

**Required.** Most REQUIRED information corresponds to an actual historical pain point. History identity, interventions, measure semantics, claim trace, censoring, and run selection are repeatedly consequential. The exception is a universally mandatory formal discovery/confirmation label: historical exploratory studies are often transparently exploratory without such a label, and its absence did not usually weaken the narrow result.

**Recommended.** Open code, executable artifacts, persistent IDs, hashes, machine-readable metadata, independent reruns, and validation packets substantially reduce burden when present but do not define scientific validity. Stringmol and Outlier demonstrate high value; Evoloops demonstrates non-necessity.

**Optional.** No optional universal tree, ontology, metric, format, container, vendor, reliability threshold, or blockchain repeatedly becomes claim-blocking. When ancestry is essential, the conditional continuity requirement—not a universal tree—does the scientific work.

Flags:

- Discovery/confirmation label for transparently exploratory component science: **POSSIBLY OVERCLASSIFIED AS REQUIRED**.
- Machine-readable metadata: no upgrade pressure; its scientific status remains **RECOMMENDED**.
- High-volume raw state: no universal upgrade pressure; retain **RECOMMENDED / CONDITIONAL**.
- AI provenance in pre-AI studies: **NOT APPLICABLE**, not missing.

## 33. Historical-era robustness

**Decision: ROBUST WITH MINOR ERA-SENSITIVE RECONSTRUCTION BURDEN.**

- Early CA/digital evolution: semantic rules, initial conditions, figures, and lineage descriptions can support scientific adequacy; raw-run and selection reconstruction is often weaker.
- Mature digital evolution: methods and replicate designs are strong, but exact run/data/analysis binding often remains distributed.
- Modern open-data ALife: persistent archives can make end-to-end audit low-burden, as OUT-HR demonstrates; modernity alone does not fix metric semantics, as FL-E demonstrates.

EX2 benefits from newer infrastructure without declaring older rigorous science unusable.

## 35. Provenance-technology neutrality

**Validated.** EX2 accepts paper tables, rule descriptions, source archives, supplementary documentation, datasets, and causal graphs as alternative semantic provenance carriers. Git, Docker, Zenodo, JSON, hashes, and containers can strengthen identity but do not define scientific validity.

Machine readability is scientifically useful for scale, cross-package comparison, and automated trace checking. Historical cases do not show it to be scientifically required. The frozen `RECOMMENDED` classification is correct.

AI provenance is **NOT APPLICABLE** for the historical primary studies. Future AI-specific reporting activates only when AI materially enters evidence extraction, classification, or interpretation.
