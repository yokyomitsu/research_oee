# OEE Frontier Research Workspace

## Mission

This repository is a persistent scientific workspace for investigating Open-Ended Evolution (OEE), with particular emphasis on why artificial evolutionary systems tend to enter bounded, recurrent, saturated, or quasi-stable regimes while natural evolution appears capable of repeatedly generating new adaptive possibilities.

The repository has completed a substantial literature-, theory-, and methodology-first phase.

The current objective is no longer to accumulate additional literature or invent another general OEE mechanism from existing observations.

The current objective is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{established boundary}
\rightarrow
\text{discriminating question}
\rightarrow
\text{matched empirical design}
\rightarrow
\text{original evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{attack}
\rightarrow
\text{falsifiable causal inference}
}
$$

The repository should now move toward **original frontier research** when, and only when, a bounded experiment can generate evidence that changes what can scientifically be inferred.

The ultimate motivating question remains:

> Why do artificial evolutionary systems usually converge or close, while natural evolution appears capable of continually generating new evolutionary possibilities?

Do not assume that one universal mechanism answers this question.

OEE should currently be treated as a family of related dynamical hallmarks whose causal relationships remain to be established.

---

## Current research stance

The literature/theory/methodology phase produced a durable result:

$$
\boxed{
\text{the current repository mechanism decomposition is exhausted}
\neq
\text{the OEE field is exhausted}.
}
$$

Further subdivision of already-closed theoretical families is not progress.

The highest-value unresolved work now concerns **creating evidence that existing literature does not contain**.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{reading is support for research}
\neq
\text{the research target itself}.
}
$$

A literature search is justified only when it resolves a decision needed by an active frontier, such as:

* whether a proposed experiment has already been performed;
* whether a measurement is scientifically valid;
* whether a model exposes the required intervention;
* whether a candidate claim is prior art;
* whether a historical baseline can be reconstructed;
* whether a confound has already been characterized.

Do not open a new literature-review branch merely because more papers exist.

## Fundamental distinctions

Never conflate:

* replication;
* self-reproduction;
* heritable variation;
* Darwinian evolution;
* adaptive evolution;
* evolutionary activity;
* diversity;
* novelty;
* adaptive novelty;
* functional novelty;
* complexity;
* complexity growth;
* resource scalability;
* major transitions;
* Open-Ended Evolution.

In particular:

$$
\text{Darwinian evolution}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{OEE}
$$

$$
\text{evolutionary activity}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{adaptive novelty}
$$

$$
\text{diversity}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{novelty}
$$

$$
\text{large state space}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{large evolutionary accessibility}
$$

$$
\text{formal constructibility}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{evolutionary accessibility}
$$

$$
\text{finite adaptive novelty}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{ongoing adaptive novelty}
$$

$$
\text{realized complexity growth}
\neq
\text{resource scalability}
$$

$$
\text{component exemplar}
\neq
\text{broad OEE exemplar}.
$$

Finite observations are always censored:

$$
\text{no event observed by }T
\not\Rightarrow
\text{event impossible after }T,
$$

and:

$$
\text{growth through }T
\not\Rightarrow
\text{unbounded growth}.
$$

## Closed-frontier firewall

Do not reopen a closed theoretical branch by renaming it.

The following are not active general-mechanism hypotheses unless new discriminating evidence materially changes the canonical state:

* universal recurrent-transition mechanism;
* unconditional broad-scope evolvability;
* generic endogenous organizational-type creation;
* generic ecological construction of selection structure;
* self-maintaining plasticity as a universal OEE mechanism;
* universal continuity invariant;
* formal possibility-space expansion by itself;
* generic self-reference or meta-evolution by itself.

Earlier provisional representations such as:

$$
\mathcal S_t=(X_t,\mathcal O_t)
$$

and:

$$
\mathcal O_t=(V_t,R_t,H_t,S_t)
$$

remain historically useful analytical representations.

They are **not the current central hypothesis**.

Do not return to:

$$
\text{OEE}
\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{endogenous transformation of the effective evolutionary possibility space}
$$

as the default research program.

Any future return to a related concept must be triggered by new evidence and must survive the prior-art and counterexample firewalls again.

## Original-frontier principle

The next phase should preferentially pursue work capable of producing an **original empirical or formal result**, including a well-supported negative result.

Original does not mean unprecedented-looking.

A candidate contribution must be checked against prior art before being described as novel.

A worthwhile frontier should have a plausible path to:

$$
\boxed{
\text{one observation that was not already present in the literature}
}
$$

and that observation should alter the set of scientifically admissible explanations.

The objective is not to maximize:

$$
P(\text{positive result}).
$$

The objective is to maximize:

$$
\boxed{
P(\text{interpretable state-changing result}).
}
$$

A negative or null result may therefore be more valuable than a visually impressive positive result.

---

## Frontier-selection rule

When multiple original-research directions are available, compare them explicitly.

Prefer directions with high:

* expected information gain;
* causal interpretability;
* measurement maturity;
* feasibility;
* prior-art headroom;
* probability of producing an auditable result;
* value of a null result;
* independence from unavailable external resources;
* ability to support a deeper follow-up experiment.

Prefer low:

* uncontrolled confounding;
* observer dependence;
* right-censoring dominance;
* archaeological reconstruction burden;
* implementation complexity unrelated to the scientific question;
* dependence on a hoped-for rare emergence.

Do not reduce these dimensions to a decorative scalar score.

Use them to eliminate weak candidates and select exactly one frontier.

---

## Same-system contrast preference

For causal empirical work, prefer:

$$
\boxed{
\text{same system, matched regimes}
>
\text{closely matched variants}
>
\text{cross-system comparison}.
}
$$

This is a preference, not a universal rule.

Cross-system comparisons remain useful for identifying candidate dimensions, but differences between systems usually confound causal inference.

The ideal experiment changes one interpretable factor while preserving the scientific identity of:

* substrate;
* evolutionary process where possible;
* measurement object;
* observation protocol;
* history definition.

Same-system experiments are not automatically clean. Every intervention must still be audited for hidden changes.

---

## Existing-system preference

When two designs can answer the same scientific question, prefer:

$$
\boxed{
\text{existing reproducible system}
>
\text{minimal controlled modification}
>
\text{new experimental architecture}.
}
$$

A new artificial-life architecture is justified only when existing systems cannot instantiate a sufficiently discriminating experiment.

Do not select Evoloops, Lenia, Flow-Lenia, Stringmol, Aevol, Geb, Avida, or any other substrate because of historical attachment, implementation convenience, aesthetic richness, or programming-language preference.

The scientific question chooses the substrate.

---

## Experiment-design gate

No experimental implementation may begin until a prior design pass establishes all or nearly all of the following:

1. one bounded scientific question;
2. one explicitly declared inference target;
3. a scientifically meaningful control or matched comparison;
4. an interpretable intervention or controlled difference;
5. a frozen primary observable;
6. a stable meaning of that observable across regimes;
7. positive-result interpretation;
8. null-result interpretation;
9. unexpected/reversal-result interpretation;
10. strongest known confounds;
11. explicit censoring limits;
12. a bounded run/resource plan;
13. a provenance and evidence-retention plan;
14. an exact-match prior-art search;
15. a statement of what the experiment would still not establish.

A design fails if every plausible outcome can be retrospectively interpreted as supporting the same explanation.

Every design must answer:

> What would we infer differently if the result went the other way?

If the answer is essentially nothing, the experiment is non-discriminating.

---

## Implementation policy

Implementation is no longer permanently outside scope.

Implementation is a **scientific instrument** that becomes permitted only after the experiment-design gate is passed.

When implementation becomes justified:

* build only what the selected experiment requires;
* avoid unnecessary architecture;
* preserve deterministic/reproducible configuration where possible;
* separate model code from measurement and analysis;
* retain exact version and configuration provenance;
* implement controls before interpreting effects;
* validate measurement before using it scientifically;
* run a pilot before the full experiment when instrumentation uncertainty exists.

Do not let software progress substitute for research progress.

A successful implementation with a non-discriminating scientific design is not a successful research result.

---

## Pilot rule

A pilot tests whether the experiment can be executed and measured as designed.

It does not test the main scientific claim.

A pilot may validate:

* model reproduction;
* intervention application;
* measurement extraction;
* metric identity;
* cross-regime comparability;
* history/provenance retention;
* computational feasibility.

Pilot observations must not be promoted into final scientific evidence unless the protocol explicitly established in advance that they qualify.

---

## Intervention rule

Where feasible, prefer controlled interventions over post hoc correlations.

However:

$$
\text{ablation dependence}
\neq
\text{general causal mechanism}.
$$

An intervention can establish a local causal relation under declared conditions.

It does not automatically establish:

* historical origin;
* necessity outside the tested regime;
* sufficiency;
* universality;
* a general OEE mechanism.

All causal claims must remain scoped to the tested system, regime, intervention, observable, and observation window.

---

## Measurement rule

Freeze the primary measurement before examining decisive outcomes.

Do not create or alter a metric in response to the desired result.

Always distinguish:

$$
\text{measurement}
\neq
\text{objective optimized by evolution}.
$$

Optimizing novelty, complexity, diversity, evolutionary activity, or another metric may answer a legitimate question, but:

$$
\boxed{
\text{optimization of metric }M
\not\Rightarrow
\text{spontaneous emergence of phenomenon }M.
}
$$

When available, reuse previously stabilized repository methods rather than inventing new metrics.

### Adaptive Functional Novelty

The existing AFN evidence structure remains:

$$
\boxed{N\land F\land H\land A\land C}.
$$

The AFN methodological branch itself remains paused where external independent validation is required, but the measurement framework may be used prospectively.

### Complexity

Keep separate:

$$
S=\text{realized temporal sustainedness}
$$

and:

$$
I=\text{resource-scalability evidence}.
$$

Do not infer one from the other.

A cross-regime complexity experiment requires the same defensible complexity object and semantics in all compared regimes.

---

## Provenance rule

Original evidence must be auditable.

For prospective experiments, preserve as appropriate:

* model/source identity;
* exact configuration;
* intervention assignment;
* seed/run identity;
* total runs;
* failed and excluded runs;
* run-selection rules;
* initial-state provenance;
* history/ancestry required by the claim;
* raw observations;
* processing transformations;
* metric implementation;
* analysis version;
* observation horizon;
* termination/censoring status.

The desired trace is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{system/history}
\rightarrow
\text{raw evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{processed evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{measurement}
\rightarrow
\text{claim}.
}
$$

Never retain only the best-looking trajectory without preserving how it was selected.

---

## Existence, typicality, and scalability

Every experiment must state whether its primary target is:

* existence;
* typicality / robustness;
* scalability;
* causal dependence.

Do not let a best-of-\(N\) run answer a typicality question.

Do not let longer runtime answer a resource-scalability question.

Do not let external resource scaling count as realized temporal growth.

---

## Novelty and prior-art rule

Already established phenomena are evidence, not discoveries.

Before retaining an original contribution, search the closest primary prior art.

Classify the proposed contribution cautiously:

* ESTABLISHED;
* PARTIALLY EXPLORED;
* UNDEREXPLORED;
* POTENTIALLY NOVEL;
* UNKNOWN.

Do not claim:

* novel;
* first;
* unexplored;
* new mechanism;

without an explicit prior-art audit.

A parameter variation of an existing experiment is not automatically a new scientific contribution.

---

## Hypothesis firewall

`HYPOTHESES.md` is not a brainstorming file.

A candidate causal hypothesis enters it only when it has survived:

1. prior-art review;
2. terminology/repackaging review;
3. counterexample attack;
4. stable phenomenon definition;
5. credible empirical evidence;
6. meaningful negative/control comparison;
7. causal discriminability;
8. a falsifiable prediction;
9. an explicit falsification condition.

Until then, record questions and designs in `FRONTIER.md` or dedicated research artifacts.

The current empty `HYPOTHESES.md` is a legitimate scientific state.

Do not fill it merely because empirical experimentation has begun.

---

## Mechanism gate

Do not infer a general mechanism from one successful experiment.

Mechanism work becomes justified only after sufficient evidence exists for approximately:

$$
\boxed{
\text{stable phenomenon}
+
\text{credible positive result}
+
\text{meaningful matched negative/control}
+
\text{causal discriminability}.
}
$$

A local mechanism may become supportable before a general OEE mechanism.

Always state scope.

For example:

$$
X
\text{ alters measured complexity in system }S
$$

does not imply:

$$
X
\text{ causes OEE generally}.
$$

---

## Negative-result rule

A hypothesis, design, or intervention that fails under a well-formed test is a successful research result if the failure eliminates a plausible explanation.

Use:

$$
\text{generate}
\rightarrow
\text{attack}
\rightarrow
\text{eliminate}
\rightarrow
\text{refine}.
$$

Do not rescue favored ideas by changing:

* measurement;
* observation window;
* target phenomenon;
* intervention;
* success criterion;

after seeing an unfavorable result without explicitly opening a new experiment.

### `STATUS.md`

Minimal sufficient description of the current research state.

It should answer:

* What question is active?
* What is established?
* What experiment/design is active?
* What is blocked?
* What is the exact next operation?

Keep it short.

### `KNOWN.md`

Canonical compressed boundary of durable established knowledge.

Do not store temporary plans, expected outcomes, or speculative mechanisms here.

### `HYPOTHESES.md`

Only hypotheses that have passed the hypothesis firewall.

### `FRONTIER.md`

Contains active, paused, blocked, and high-value unresolved research questions.

There must normally be at most one active frontier operation.

### Dedicated artifacts

Use dedicated artifacts for:

* synthesis;
* frontier selection;
* design;
* feasibility;
* pilot;
* experiment protocol;
* results;
* analysis;
* validation.

Do not overload canonical files with full research transcripts.

## Research modes

A research operation belongs to one primary mode:

### SYNTHESIS

Compresses established research state.

### FRONTIER SELECTION

Chooses the highest-information unresolved direction.

### DESIGN

Constructs and attacks a discriminating experiment or formal test.

### FEASIBILITY / PILOT

Determines whether the frozen design can actually be instrumented.

### IMPLEMENTATION

Builds the minimum scientific apparatus authorized by a passed design gate.

### EXECUTION

Produces original observations under a frozen protocol.

### ANALYSIS

Tests the predeclared scientific inference against generated evidence.

### HYPOTHESIS / MECHANISM

Admitted only after the evidence gate is passed.

Do not silently perform several of these modes in one pass.

---

## One-operation rule

Each Codex research pass must have exactly one primary operation.

A pass should:

$$
S_t
\rightarrow
Q_t
\rightarrow
R_t
\rightarrow
\Delta S
\rightarrow
S_{t+1}
$$

where \(R_t\) may be literature evidence, design analysis, pilot evidence, experimental evidence, or formal reasoning depending on the active mode.

Do not automatically continue into the next operation.

At the end of every pass:

1. state the decision;
2. state what changed scientifically;
3. state what did not change;
4. update durable repository state;
5. name exactly one next operation;
6. stop.

---

## Reading-only stop rule

Do not continue reading merely because an unresolved topic exists.

A literature/source pass must answer a decision-relevant question.

Terminate literature exploration when additional sources are unlikely to change:

* candidate selection;
* measurement validity;
* intervention validity;
* prior-art status;
* feasibility;
* hypothesis status.

The repository has already reached a literature-only empirical boundary once.

Do not reproduce that loop under new terminology.

---

## Success criteria

A research pass is valuable if it does at least one of the following:

* eliminates a plausible causal explanation;
* identifies one original discriminating experiment;
* demonstrates that a proposed experiment is non-discriminating;
* validates or invalidates a measurement needed for original evidence;
* establishes that an intervention is or is not causally interpretable;
* produces an original positive or negative empirical observation;
* closes a frontier through new evidence;
* justifies a falsifiable causal hypothesis;
* invalidates a previously plausible hypothesis;
* identifies that no feasible experiment currently has sufficient information value.

The strongest form of progress is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{fewer scientifically admissible explanations after the pass}
}
$$

not more files, more code, more terminology, or more observed activity.
