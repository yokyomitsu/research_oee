# EVIDENCE MAP 003 — COMPLEXITY STRONG-S / STRONG-I SEARCH

**Operation:** CG5 — EXPAND POSITIVE-CASE EVIDENCE MAP  
**Research date:** 2026-09-09  
**Scope:** Published and publicly accessible artificial-evolution evidence, historically through 2026  
**Method:** Frozen [Methodology 003](METHODOLOGY_003_COMPLEXITY_SUSTAINEDNESS_SCALABILITY.md)  
**No new computation:** No artificial-life system, deposited code, simulation, or raw dataset was run or reanalyzed.

## 1. Why CG5 was selected

**Fact.** [Application 002](APPLICATION_002_COMPLEXITY_SUSTAINEDNESS_SCALABILITY_CROSS_SYSTEM.md) classified the original E1 corpus as:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Avida}&=(S2,I0),\\
\text{Geb}&=(S1,I4),\\
\text{Tierra}&=(S0,I0),\\
\text{Stringmol}&=(S1,I0),\\
\text{Flow-Lenia.png}&=(S1,I0),
\end{aligned}}
\]

with Hintze stopped at the metric-validity firewall.

**Interpretation.** CG3A established **B — METHOD VALIDATED, POSITIVE CASE MISSING**. Its strongest realized-growth and scalability records occurred in different systems. CG5 was selected to test whether that split was an artifact of E1's system selection rather than a property of the broader published record.

## 2. Exact search target

The target was:

\[
\boxed{\exists X:\ S(X)\geq S_{\mathrm{strong}}\ \land\ I(X)\geq I_{\mathrm{strong}}}
\]

where, under the frozen method:

- strong sustainedness means **S3** or **S4**;
- strong scalability means **I3** or qualified **I4**; and
- both records must concern the same defensible complexity object in the same coherent system family under compatible evidential regimes.

No numerical threshold and no scalar combination such as \(S+I\) was introduced.

## 3. Frozen methodology

CG5 made no change to the sequence:

\[
\boxed{X\rightarrow M\rightarrow E\rightarrow (S,I)}.
\]

- **X — object specification:** the bearer, complexity object, metric, population summary, and observation protocol must be declared.
- **M — metric validity:** raw size, unused capacity, arbitrary accumulation, visual intricacy, and theoretical state-space size do not by themselves count as complexity.
- **E — evolutionary grounding:** the measured change must be produced by the reported evolutionary history; direct optimization of the metric is disclosed and cannot by itself establish spontaneous evolutionary complexity growth.
- **S — within-regime sustainedness:** `C_R(t)` is classified as S0–S4 using the frozen categorical vocabulary.
- **I — resource-scaled attained frontier:** `K_Π(R)` is classified as I0–I4 using the frozen categorical vocabulary.

The observation/scaling distinction remained:

\[
C_R(t)\neq K_\Pi(R).
\]

Cross-substrate transfer, more available genotype space, and indefinitely extensible architecture were not treated as resource-scaled evolved-complexity evidence.

## 4. Search strategy

The search was bounded in three stages.

### Stage 1 — high-priority candidates

Primary literature was inspected for Polyworld, Aevol, Avida extensions, other digital-organism and genome-architecture models, artificial chemistries, Ackley-influenced scalable systems, and recent OEE systems.

### Stage 2 — citation expansion

The search followed the major OEE syntheses and citation neighborhoods around Adami-style complexity, Geb scalability, Polyworld neural complexity, and the Aevol complexity-ratchet study. The search target included `complexity growth`, `evolving complexity`, `neural complexity`, `genome complexity`, `complexity ratchet`, `unbounded complexity`, `indefinite scalability`, and `increasing maximum complexity`, not only `open-ended evolution`.[^1]

### Stage 3 — current literature

Primary and preprint literature from 2024–2026 was screened, including Genelife, Outlier, Flow-Lenia developments, Hash Chemistry, particle and continuous-CA work, Evolvoid, fitness-free evolving agents, and MSPD.

**Interpretation.** Five new system/model families passed Gate S sufficiently to receive full S/I profiles: Polyworld, Aevol, the competition-driven phenotype-dimension model, the adaptive molecular-ratchet model, and MSPD. Other candidates were screened but stopped at X, M, E, or both S/I inclusion gates.

## 5. Candidate inclusion gate

A candidate entered the detailed map only when published evidence supported at least one of:

- **Gate S:** a temporal evolutionary increase in a declared, defensible complexity object; or
- **Gate I:** a resource-scaling experiment on an evolved complexity frontier.

Candidates were excluded from detailed S/I assignment when they supplied only visual complexity, richness, behavior without a complexity metric, genome/entity size without active-organization control, metric or architecture extensibility, or state-space capacity.

**Fact.** Fitness, adaptation, diversity, novelty, replication, and ecological coexistence were not accepted as substitutes for complexity growth. Hintze remained the adversarial metric control.[^2]

## 7. Polyworld

### X/M/E

**Fact.** Polyworld's bearer is an evolving neural agent. The central object is information-theoretic neural complexity: simultaneous integration and differentiation in recorded neural dynamics. Yaeger compared selection-driven evolution with passive/null evolution, and also compared natural selection with a separate steady-state genetic algorithm directly optimizing the complexity metric.[^5]

- **X:** **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- **M:** **STRONGLY SUPPORTED**. The metric is not neuron count or genome length and has explicit low-complexity limiting organizations.
- **E:** **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED** for the driven-versus-passive comparison.

### S audit

**Fact.** Selection-driven complexity increases early and exceeds passive evolution. The reported behavioral adaptation approaches a stable pattern on approximately the same timescale. Neural complexity then peaks or stabilizes rather than maintaining an advancing frontier. Direct metric optimization reaches values substantially above the selection-driven runs, showing remaining accessible metric capacity but not continued natural-selection-driven growth. Yaeger describes richer ecospace as a future conjecture, not as a result.[^5]

**Fact.** A 2017 Polyworld study again reports neural dynamics moving toward a viable regime and then stabilizing after the population can sustain itself; it is not a renewed higher frontier or resource-scaling experiment.[^6]

- `C_R(t)`: population neural-complexity distribution/summary.
- Temporal evidence: early selection-driven increase followed by stabilization.
- Duration: order of thousands to roughly 10,000 Polyworld time steps in the anchor experiments.
- Plateau/saturation: present in the observed regime.
- Reversibility: not a decisive reported test.
- Right-censoring: retained, but the terminal record is not continuing upward.
- **S class:** **S1 — FINITE COMPLEXITY INCREASE**.
- Evidence strength: **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.

### I audit

No Polyworld paper located in the reviewed search space scales a declared limiting-resource path and measures an attained neural-complexity frontier over several levels. Comparisons among imposed environmental conditions are not resource scaling, and the later dynamic study is not a frontier experiment.

- **I class:** **I0 — NO SCALABILITY EVIDENCE**.
- Evidence strength: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

### Destructive conclusion

**Interpretation.** Polyworld is a finite selection-driven complexity-gain case, not strong S. Model richness and remaining optimization headroom do not override the observed stabilization. It is not a Q4 candidate.

## 8. Aevol

### X/M/E

**Fact.** Liard et al. use two quantitative objects:

1. genomic complexity \(C_G\): the number of essential base pairs whose mutation changes phenotype; and
2. proteome/functional complexity \(C_P\): the number of distinct functional protein parameters represented in the proteome, with tolerance controls that avoid counting functionally duplicate proteins separately.

They also classify organisms as functionally simple or complex from proteome organization rather than genome size. The simple environment can be fit by a single functional protein, so complicated solutions are not required by target geometry.[^7]

- **X:** **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- **M:** **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** for \(C_P\); **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** for \(C_G\), which improves strongly on genome size but remains a mutation-sensitivity information proxy.
- **E:** **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**. The metrics are reconstructed along evolutionary lineages.

### S audit

**Fact.** The long experiment evolves populations for 270,000 generations and analyzes lineages through generation 250,000. Across 300 simple-environment runs, most terminal organisms are in the paper's complex functional class; complex lineages rarely return to the simple class. \(C_P\) continues accumulating over the analyzed long lineages, whereas \(C_G\) rises and then rapidly approaches a plateau. The result therefore establishes longitudinal functional organization, not merely larger genomes.[^7]

**Fact.** The paper explicitly examines whether the ratchet can continue indefinitely and states that terminal complexity appears bounded. It identifies remaining finite-capacity explanations, including coding-capacity constraints at the mutation regime and increasing waiting times. This is direct evidence against reclassifying the right-censored record as S3/S4. Irreversibility after complexity arises is not the same as a continually advancing frontier.

**Fact.** Later Aevol work starts from five lineages evolved for ten million generations with stable genome structures, then varies population size and mutation rate for two million generations. It measures equilibrium coding/noncoding genome structure and streamlining, not \(C_P\) frontier growth. Larger populations mainly remove noncoding sequence; higher mutation rates reduce both coding and noncoding sequence.[^8]

- `C_R(t)`: lineage \(C_P(t)\), with \(C_G(t)\) as a secondary object and simple/complex class retention.
- Temporal statistic: longitudinal lineage trajectories and terminal class counts across replicated runs.
- Duration: 250,000 analyzed generations; later related-platform evidence extends genome-architecture observation but not the same \(C_P\) record.
- Plateau/saturation: \(C_G\) plateaus; \(C_P\) continues through the finite window, but the authors report an apparent bound.
- Reversibility: rare under the focal regime; complexity can collapse under stronger robustness constraint/high mutation regime.
- Right-censoring: explicit.
- **S class:** **S2 — EXTENDED REALIZED GROWTH**.
- Evidence strength: **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED** for S2; S3/S4 are **EVIDENCE AGAINST** in the focal publication.

### I audit

No Aevol study located scales the limiting resources of the same \(C_P\) object along an ordered path and tests whether its attained frontier rises. The 2024 population-size experiment is a strong control against a loose platform-level combination: it studies a different object, different starting state, and equilibrium streamlining rather than expanding functional complexity.

- **I class:** **I0 — NO SCALABILITY EVIDENCE**.
- Evidence strength: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.
- Cross-experiment relation: **RELATED PLATFORM ONLY**; for a joint S/I claim the evidence is **INCOMPATIBLE EVIDENCE**.

### Destructive conclusion

**Interpretation.** Aevol is the strongest new realized-growth case, but remains S2/I0. Its ratchet supports persistence and extended accumulation; it does not establish unsaturated frontier advance or resource scalability.

## 9. Other digital-evolution candidates

### Competition-driven phenotype-dimension model

**Fact.** Ispolatov, Alekseeva, and Doebeli define complexity as the number of active phenotypic dimensions/capabilities. Adaptive-dynamics and individual-based simulations show sequential occupation of dimensions one through five; the higher-dimensional result is averaged over ten adaptive-dynamics runs. Each dimension is an active phenotype coordinate with a physiological cost, so this is more than population richness.[^9]

- **X:** **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- **M:** **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**. Acquired dimensions are active capabilities, but all five axes and their interpretation are supplied in advance.
- **E:** **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- **S class:** **S2 — EXTENDED REALIZED GROWTH**; evidence strength **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- **I class:** **I0 — NO SCALABILITY EVIDENCE**; evidence strength **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

**Interpretation.** The sequential series is scientifically informative but terminates at the model's five supplied dimensions. No observed unsaturated frontier or resource path supports S3/I3.

### Adaptive molecular-ratchet model

**Fact.** Röschinger et al. model evolving molecular recognition codes. The declared complexity object combines code length and coding density under explicit recognition function and a per-unit complexity cost. Time-dependent targets lead to longer codes than static targets, but the analysis is organized around stationary distributions and finite most-likely code lengths; simulated paths fluctuate around these states.[^10]

- **X:** **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- **M:** **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**. Length is functionally coupled and penalized, but remains the principal extensive quantity.
- **E:** **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED** within the population-genetic model.
- **S class:** **S1 — FINITE COMPLEXITY INCREASE**; evidence strength **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- **I class:** **I0 — NO SCALABILITY EVIDENCE**; evidence strength **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

**Interpretation.** A parameter-dependent shift to a higher stationary complexity state is not sustained frontier expansion and is not resource-scaled complexity.

### Avida population-size extension

The LaBar–Adami population-size experiment is retained only as a baseline-family incompatibility check. It has four primary population-size levels plus intermediate sizes and long replicated runs, but the complexity object is the number of acquired tasks in a fixed finite repertoire and does not monotonically increase with population size.[^4]

- Same platform: yes.
- Same evidential system/object as E1's physical complexity: no.
- Classification: **RELATED PLATFORM ONLY / INCOMPATIBLE EVIDENCE**.
- I upgrade: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

## 10. Scalable-substrate candidates

**Fact.** Ackley's indefinite-scalability program concerns indefinitely extensible computing media and local architectures designed not to require global reengineering as scale increases.[^11] That is substrate scalability, not a measured `K_Π(R)` for evolved complexity.

**Fact.** The reviewed Ackley-family and DISHTINY publications establish architectural execution, local interaction, and in some cases evolutionary or multicellular phenomena. They do not report several ordered resource levels with rising attained frontiers for a declared nontrivial complexity object.

**Interpretation.** Architecture-only systems fail Gate I for CG5. An indefinitely scalable substrate is evidence about available computation; it is not evidence that evolution uses added resources to attain greater organized complexity.

**Fact.** Geb remains the sole reviewed system in which co-limiting resource dimensions are explicitly identified, scaled separately and jointly, and linked to an established active-component complexity frontier. World length/population capacity and maximum neurons each saturate alone; joint scaling raises maxima across multiple levels approximately logarithmically in the lower limiting resource.[^12]

## 11. Recent 2024–2026 candidates

The current-literature screen produced no new Q4 candidate.

### Hash Chemistry family

**Fact.** Non-spatial and Structural Cellular Hash Chemistry papers describe continuing or unbounded complexity growth, but operationalize complexity primarily as the size/active-cell count of self-replicating components or patterns. The 2025 structural paper reports spatial interactions and replication; the 2026 synthesis treats spatial domain size as a control parameter for transitions between compact and runaway size-dominance regimes.[^13]

**Interpretation.** Under the frozen firewall, entity size alone does not establish organized functional complexity. This family is stopped at **M**, before S/I assignment. Spatial-domain effects also alter the ecological regime and do not supply a same-object, multi-level evolved-complexity frontier.

### Evolvoid

**Fact.** Evolvoid is a genetic algorithm with a designed fitness function that seeks a single optimum over six fixed morphological genes. Its reported `H(g)` is a sum of Shannon entropy/variability of morphological-gene values within a generation; the authors themselves describe the result as increasing variability. The best fitness reaches its global maximum around generation 227 and runs stop under optimization criteria.[^14]

**Interpretation.** Population variability over fixed genes is not a demonstrated complexity object. Evolvoid is stopped at **M**; its software's description as modular or scalable is architectural, not I evidence.

### Fitness-free self-organizing agents

**Fact.** The 2026 preprint reports a hard phenotypic-complexity ceiling in its first two experimental cycles and structural complexification after introducing a CPPN/NEAT-style encoding in the third.[^15]

**Interpretation.** The available record does not supply resource-scaled frontier evidence, and the representation change separates the third cycle from the first two as a different evidential regime. It does not reach Q4.

### Genelife, Outlier, Flow-Lenia, Particle Life, and continuous CA

**Fact.** Genelife reports continuing genetic/spatial novelty and activity rather than longitudinal complexity growth.[^16] Outlier reports self-replicating hierarchical structures and later causal analyses of distributed selfhood, but not a tracked complexity frontier.[^17] Recent Flow-Lenia/continuous-CA work reports emergent evolution or searches complex regimes; Particle Life exhibits rich collective dynamics; none supplies the required paired S/I records.

**Interpretation.** Replication, activity, novelty, hierarchical appearance, or discovered complex regimes do not pass Gate S without a declared longitudinal complexity object.

## 12. MSPD 2026

**Publication status:** **PREPRINT**, arXiv v2, 3 August 2026.[^18]

### What is established

**Fact.** MSPD is a population/path-level scalar measuring temporal multiscale organization of heterogeneity in local transition laws. It is a windowed, finite-resolution estimator. The paper includes synthetic low-score controls for absent or temporally unorganized heterogeneity and states sensitivity to window/lag choice and approximate within-window stationarity.[^18]

**Fact.** The metric plays two roles: a post-hoc analytical lens and an explicit optimization objective. Life-like CA rules are selected/ranked by MSPD; Flow-Lenia rule parameters are optimized over fixed pathwise contexts; Particle Life++ neural interaction weights are optimized by Sep-CMA-ES with MSPD plus a mean-heterogeneity term. Matched random controls generally score lower.[^18]

- Complexity measurement: **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- Optimization toward higher MSPD: **DIRECTLY SUPPORTED**.
- Metric validity beyond its stated dynamical object: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**.

### What is not established

The optimizer's generation/checkpoint sequence is not an organismal evolutionary history demonstrating continually increasing realized complexity. The result shows that search can find high-MSPD regions. It does not show spontaneous evolutionary histories repeatedly setting higher complexity records.

- Temporal evolutionary complexity growth: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** only as finite external metric optimization.
- Sustainedness: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.
- Resource scaling: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.
- Indefinite scalability: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

The transfers across Flow-Lenia, Life-like CA, and Particle Life++ test whether the definition and reported contrasts can be applied on different substrates. They do not order resource configurations of one model family. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{cross-substrate transfer}\neq I.}
\]

The paper also reports an incomplete claim-by-substrate matrix, fixed-context rather than fresh-context Flow-Lenia replay, and underpowered directional C5 effects. These limitations reinforce, but are not needed for, the S/I firewall.[^18]

- **S class:** **S1 — FINITE COMPLEXITY INCREASE**, conditional on treating direct external optimization consistently with Flow-Lenia.png.
- **I class:** **I0 — NO SCALABILITY EVIDENCE**.
- Same evidence regime for S and I: **NOT APPLICABLE**; no I record exists.

**Interpretation.** MSPD validates a measurable and optimizable dynamical-complexity target more than it validates evolutionary sustainedness. It is not a Q4 case.

## 20. Negative/latest-evidence audit

The strongest current evidence state, rather than the earliest positive report, was used.

- **Aevol:** long \(C_P\) accumulation is retained together with the authors' apparent-bound assessment and the later stable/equilibrium genome-architecture study.[^7][^8]

- **Recent fitness-free agents:** the reported structural increase is retained together with the preceding plateaus and representation change.[^15]

**Interpretation.** Later evidence weakens, rather than strengthens, a Q4 reading of the leading S candidates.

## 21. Theoretical versus empirical systems

**Fact.** The Chaitin/algorithmic-OEE family and later formalizations provide theorem-level constructions in which program or state complexity is unbounded under stated computational assumptions. Hernández-Orozco, Hernández-Quiroz, and Zenil define strong OEE with nontrivial algorithmic measures and analyze a Chaitin-derived example.[^19]

**Interpretation.** These results are not empirical resource-scaling records comparable to Geb. They use idealized, resource-unbounded computation or asymptotic constructions rather than several finite resource levels of an artificial evolutionary system with an observed established frontier. They therefore receive the separate label:

> **THEORETICAL POSITIVE, NOT EMPIRICAL Q4 CASE**.

They do not replace simulation/experimental evidence in the CG5 conjunction search.

4. additional candidates repeatedly failed at M (size, variability, compressibility), S (finite gain, plateau, stationary state, direct optimization), or I (no ordered resource-frontier experiment); and

**Interpretation.** Search completeness is adequate for this bounded pass. It does not support a global absence claim. A second-stage positive map has low expected information gain under the current published record.
