# Q014 — Evidence for Organizational-Type Creation

One theoretical research pass, 2026-09-08. This pass evaluates the evidential standard proposed in [Q013](Q013_ENDOGENOUS_ORGANIZATIONAL_TYPE_CREATION.md); it does not search for another candidate system or propose an OEE mechanism.

Epistemic convention: descriptions of cited definitions, models, and observations are **Fact**. Cross-framework sufficiency judgments and repository decisions are **Interpretation**. No scientific **Hypothesis** or unsupported **Speculation** is introduced.

## 1. Claim under examination

“Evolution endogenously created a new heritable/selectable organizational type” is a composite claim. It can mean at least six increasingly demanding things:

| Category | Claim | What would be a category error |
|---|---|---|
| **A. New morphology** | A spatial or structural phenotype not present in the comparison state appears. | Inferring heredity, selection, or individuality from shape alone. |
| **B. New collective trait** | A causally measurable property belongs to a group or interaction system rather than to any component considered alone. | Inferring that the group reproduces or is a unit of selection. |
| **C. New heritable collective organization** | A collective organization is reliably transmitted or developmentally reconstructed across collective parent–offspring cycles. | Requiring a distinct collective genome, or accepting environmental recurrence without collective ancestry. |
| **D. New selectable organization** | Heritable variation in the collective organization causally affects reproductive success under a stated population structure. | Equating collective benefit, cell-lineage success, or mere covariance with selection at the collective level. |
| **E. New evolutionary individual** | A newly delimited entity possesses Darwinian properties in its own right, supported by individuating mechanisms and a defensible reproductive life cycle. | Treating every selected group trait as a completed transition in individuality. |
| **F. New organizational type** | Relative to a declared equivalence, evolution produces a new causal organization of reproduction, inheritance, reconstruction, or individuality rather than a new value of an already specified organizational variable. | Treating any new phenotype, function, or parameter value as a new type. |

**Fact:** categories A–E have strong established literatures. Lewontin’s variation–fitness–heredity conditions, multilevel-selection theory, Darwinian-population accounts, evolutionary transitions in individuality (ETIs), and life-cycle closure address C–E. Evolutionary-novelty and character-identity work addresses whether a derived feature is a new character rather than another state of an existing one. [Lewontin 1970](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.01.110170.000245), [Godfrey-Smith 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:osobl/9780199552047.001.0001), [Clarke 2013](https://doi.org/10.5840/jphil2013110817), [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162), [Müller & Wagner 1991](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.22.110191.001305)

**Interpretation:** F is not an established additional grade of individuality. If it means “a new Darwinian individual,” ETI theory already supplies the appropriate vocabulary. If it includes non-individual changes in developmental, reproductive, or inheritance organization, it must be decomposed into a specific comparative claim. “Organizational-type creation” should therefore remain only a workspace shorthand, not a purported natural kind or new theory.

For this pass, the narrow testable meaning of F is:

> Relative to stated entities, environments, timescales, and a causal equivalence over reproduction and inheritance, descendants can recurrently construct a reproduction/inheritance organization that matched ancestors cannot construct, and that organization supports heritable fitness-relevant variation at the claimed level because of evolved individuating mechanisms rather than solely observer bookkeeping or an imposed collective cycle.

This definition does not require the selection pressure to be unplanned, a physically new primitive, or an observer-free ontology.

## 2. Closest established concepts

### Darwinian individuality and ETIs

Lewontin’s conditions establish when a population of proposed entities can evolve by natural selection: phenotypic variation, differential fitness, and parent–offspring resemblance. Godfrey-Smith treats reproduction, heredity, and fitness dependence as graded dimensions of Darwinian populations rather than universal binary gates. These frameworks establish evolutionary capacity at a proposed level, not novelty of that level. [Lewontin 1970](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.01.110170.000245), [Godfrey-Smith 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:osobl/9780199552047.001.0001)

ETI theory adds the diachronic claim that particles which were evolutionary individuals form collectives that acquire Darwinian individuality. Fitness reorganization, conflict mediation, bottlenecks, division of labor, integration, and loss of lower-level autonomy are mechanisms or indicators of transition progression; no one is a universally necessary signature at every stage or for both fraternal and egalitarian transitions. [Michod 2007](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0701489104), [West et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421402112), [Godfrey-Smith 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421378112)

Clarke’s account adds policing and demarcation mechanisms that cause a proposed entity, rather than an arbitrary collection, to satisfy the conditions for selection. Bourrat distinguishes pseudo-Darwinian properties produced by observer choice, Darwinian-like properties produced by external ecological mechanisms, and Darwinian properties produced by internal individuating mechanisms with some perturbation resilience. This is the closest existing answer to “endogenous.” [Clarke 2013](https://doi.org/10.5840/jphil2013110817), [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1086/719118)

Life-cycle closure adds a temporal criterion: stages of a proposed collective must form a closed, multiplicative cycle that reliably re-establishes them. It is especially useful for distinguishing collective reproduction from population growth or recurrent aggregation, but remains conditional on the proposed stage coarse-graining. [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162)

### Selection at multiple levels

MLS1 asks how group context affects change among particles; MLS2 treats collectives as entities that differentially found or replace collectives. Price partitions, contextual analysis, and counterfactual-fitness analyses answer different causal or bookkeeping questions, so no single nonzero statistic universally proves higher-level individuality. The ancestry mapping, trait, episode, and meaning of fitness must be declared. [Damuth & Heisler 1988](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00647962), [Shelton & Michod 2020](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2019.0364)

### Evolutionary novelty and character identity

Novelty theory separates the generation of a character from its fixation, adaptive refinement, and later ecological success. Structural novelty is often assessed through homology and character identity, not through selection strength. Character Identity Mechanisms ground diachronic sameness in a recognizable causal profile despite variation in lower-level implementation. These ideas provide a model for a causal identity comparison but do not automatically identify collective reproducers. [Müller & Wagner 1991](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.22.110191.001305), [Erwin 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12643), [DiFrisco, Love & Wagner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-020-09762-2)

### Organizational closure and causal emergence

Closure of constraints identifies mutually dependent constraints that maintain one another at specified timescales. It can help delimit constitutive organization, but organizational closure alone neither establishes reproduction nor heritable variation in fitness. Causal-emergence and information-theoretic-individuality frameworks can show that a macrodescription has causal or predictive advantages relative to a coarse-graining and intervention distribution; they do not uniquely select a biological individual or establish that the macrotype is evolutionarily new. [Montévil & Mossio 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029), [Hoel, Albantakis & Tononi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1314922110), [Krakauer et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12064-020-00313-7)

**Prior-art decision:** no single framework supplies a universal standard for F, but the scientifically meaningful parts are already available. A defensible test should compose ETI/individuality, novelty/identity, and intervention evidence without presenting their conjunction as a new theory.

## 3. External selection versus endogenous solution

Externally specified selection is not disqualifying. All laboratory evolution specifies or controls some environment, transfer rule, or survival condition. What matters is what the protocol encodes.

Selection for fast settling specifies an outcome. It does not specify failed mother–daughter separation, branched clonal growth, fracture-based propagules, apoptosis-mediated breakpoints, or a multicellular life cycle. Snowflake yeast is consequently described in the primary literature as a de novo transition and a new level of biological organization despite directed settling selection. [Ratcliff et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1115323109), [Ratcliff et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7102)

Likewise, Hammerschmidt and colleagues imposed a selective regime rewarding collective persistence, yet the evolved developmental switch and fitness decoupling were treated as key events in the evolution of Darwinian individuality. The imposed two-phase cycle limits what the experiment establishes about autonomous origin; it does not make the evolved switch designer-specified. [Hammerschmidt et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13884)

The valid distinction is therefore:

\[
\text{selection on an outcome }F
\neq
\text{enumeration or implementation of organizational solution }M.
\]

**Fact:** evolutionary novelty literature separates origination from fixation and refinement. Selection changes the frequency of available variation; it need not specify the developmental mechanism that generated it. [Müller & Wagner 1991](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.22.110191.001305), [Erwin 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12643)

**Interpretation:** Q013’s “no novelty target” clause was too strong. A protocol should be penalized when it directly supplies the claimed entity boundary, reproductive episode, inheritance channel, or organizational alternative—not merely because it imposes a fitness consequence. An explicit outcome target reduces the strength of claims about open-ended search, but it does not negate endogenous construction of an unforeseen means.

## 4. Heritability

The organization need not be copied as a ready-made object or encoded in a separate collective genome. Developmental-system and ETI accounts allow organization to be reconstructed from inherited lower-level resources, cell interactions, environmental inputs, and a repeatable life cycle. What must recur is the causally relevant collective organization, not every material component. [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162), [Godfrey-Smith 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421378112)

For category C, three propositions must be separated:

1. **Recurrence:** descendants repeatedly express a collective organization.
2. **Parent–offspring transmission or reconstruction:** resemblance follows a defensible collective ancestry relation rather than independent responses to a shared persistent environment.
3. **Heritable variation:** differences among parent collectives predict differences among offspring collectives under a declared environment and population.

Genetic transmission of component traits can be sufficient to generate high collective-trait heritability in clonal collectives; collective heredity may arise “for free” once the developmental and ancestry structure exists. This does not make collective heritability unreal, but it prevents treating a new inheritance molecule as necessary. [Zamani-Dahaj et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-018-0612-6), [Zamani-Dahaj et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes14081635)

Environmental recurrence alone is insufficient. If unrelated groups independently acquire the same phenotype because all encounter the same environment, there is no demonstrated parent–offspring inheritance of collective organization. Ecological inheritance can contribute when ancestors causally modify the conditions inherited by descendants, but the causal pathway and ancestry relation must then be included rather than treated as noise.

**Decision:** positive parent–offspring heredity of type-relevant collective organization is necessary for the phrase “heritable organizational type.” Reliable reconstruction through lower-level inheritance is sufficient in principle; a separate higher-level code is not. Heritability is population-, environment-, and trait-relative and is never sufficient for a new type or new individual.

Inheritance of the type must not be conflated with heritable variation within the type. An ETI claim requires the proposed collective population to support heritable variation in fitness-relevant traits; it does not require persistent polymorphism in the binary label “collective” after that organization fixes. A stronger claim that the organizational feature itself was selected does require historical variation in that feature, parent–offspring transmission, and differential reproductive consequences.

## 5. Selection

A covariance between a collective trait and collective reproductive success is evidence for a selection differential under the proposed grouping. It is not automatically evidence that the collective trait causes success, nor that collectives reproduce in their own right. Contextual and counterfactual analyses can test whether group properties alter particle or collective fitness; MLS2 bookkeeping can track differential collective founding or extinction. These analyses answer complementary questions. [Damuth & Heisler 1988](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00647962), [Shelton & Michod 2020](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2019.0364)

For category D, the minimum is not a preferred statistic but a causal claim:

> Under a specified collective ancestry and life cycle, variation in type-relevant collective organization changes expected collective reproductive contribution, and enough of that variation is transmitted for a response to selection to be possible.

This can be supported by convergent evidence: collective parentage, a multilevel partition, controlled manipulation or natural variation of the group property, response to selection, and exclusion of particle-only explanations. A nonzero between-group Price term alone is not universally decisive because partitions can be mathematically equivalent while supporting different causal interpretations.

Collective-level selection is not necessary for the first origin of A, B, C, or F. A structure may arise by mutation, developmental side effect, drift, or selection on a correlated lower-level trait and only later acquire fitness consequences. Bottom-up models in which cell-level selection generates collective behavior are direct counterexamples to the inference that an emergent collective phenotype required collective selection. [Colizzi, Vroomans & Merks 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.56349)

Actual collective-level selection is necessary to claim collective adaptation or realized MLS2 in an episode. Heritable fitness-relevant variation is necessary for a strong claim that the new organization is selectable. A completed evolutionary-individuality claim additionally needs evidence that the collective’s Darwinian properties arise from individuating mechanisms rather than an arbitrary partition.

## 6. Origin versus stabilization

There is no single category-independent instant of “creation.” The evidential threshold changes with the claim:

1. **Phenotypic origin:** the first occurrence of A or B.
2. **Developmental establishment:** the first reliable reconstruction of C across a collective cycle.
3. **Selective establishment:** D becomes heritable and causally fitness-relevant; it may then spread or be refined.
4. **Transition completion:** under a chosen ETI account, the collective attains a closed life cycle and internally generated Darwinian properties sufficient for E.

Novelty research explicitly distinguishes formation from adaptive refinement and ecological success. ETI research likewise treats transitions as graded and temporally extended: group formation, maintenance, transformation, life-cycle closure, and fitness reorganization need not coincide. [Erwin 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12643), [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112), [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162)

Therefore, if organization first appears neutrally and later becomes heritable/selectable, its morphological origin occurred first, its heritable organizational establishment occurred later, and its selective stabilization later still. Calling the earliest event an evolutionary individual would be premature; calling the later selected state the moment of phenotypic origin would be retrospective distortion.

## 7. Ablation and rescue

For a component or relation \(c\), observing

\[
\operatorname{do}(c=0) \Rightarrow \neg Y
\]

supports the claim that \(c\) is necessary for outcome \(Y\) in the tested descendant background and environment. The evidential content depends entirely on \(Y\): loss of morphology, life-cycle closure, parent–offspring resemblance, collective fitness, or an individuality classification are different results.

Ablation does **not** by itself establish:

- that \(c\) historically originated the organization;
- that the organization was absent ancestrally;
- that the affected group is a reproducer or evolutionary individual;
- that selection acted at the collective level;
- that \(c\) is constitutive rather than an upstream enabling condition;
- that another mechanism could not realize the same organization.

Rescue strengthens specificity. If restoring \(c\) in the ablated background restores the same type-relevant relation, it reduces concerns about off-target damage, pleiotropy, and general inviability. It establishes neither historical novelty nor a new evolutionary level. It also does not prove that \(c\) alone is sufficient outside the rescued background, because the rest of the descendant organization remains present.

The constitutive/causal distinction is not solved by a single intervention. In mechanistic philosophy, mutual manipulability has been proposed to identify constitutive relevance, but intervention on part–whole relations faces simultaneity and “fat-handedness” problems: changing a component may necessarily alter several features of the whole. Indirect mechanistic decomposition, localization, multiple selective interventions, and reconstruction are therefore stronger than one knockout. [Baumgartner & Gebharter 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axv003)

For an organizational claim, the strongest intervention targets a relation—such as state-dependent adhesion or a developmental transition—and shows specific loss of the proposed closed parent–offspring organization while matched lower-level reproduction and environmental opportunity remain. Rescue is desirable but not necessary: comparative, historical, and non-manipulative evidence routinely supports ETI and novelty claims where intervention is infeasible.

**Correction to Q013:** ablation and rescue are neither individually nor jointly necessary for organizational-type creation, and they are not sufficient. They are high-value evidence for the mechanism that realizes an independently defined organization.

## 8. Latent capacity versus newly constructed capacity

The operational comparison is between ancestral and descendant reaction norms or construction capacities, not simply their observed phenotypes in one environment.

Let \(O(g,e)\) mean that genotype or inherited system \(g\), under environment \(e\), constructs the declared organization. Then:

- **environmental revelation of latent capacity:** \(O(g_A,e_0)=0\) but \(O(g_A,e_1)=1\);
- **evolutionary construction or accommodation within the tested domain:** \(O(g_A,e)=0\) and \(O(g_D,e)=1\) for matched \(e\), with the difference traced to inherited descendant changes;
- **stabilization of ancestral plasticity:** both ancestor and descendant can express \(O\), but the descendant’s probability, threshold, robustness, or environmental range changes.

Plasticity-first research already uses ancestral or ancestral-proxy lineages and common-garden experiments across ancestral and derived environments to distinguish environmentally induced ancestral phenotypes from later genetic accommodation. The same logic applies to organizational capacity. [Levis & Pfennig 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2016.03.012)

Genotype novelty does not settle the issue: a new mutation can merely shift the threshold of an ancestral reaction norm, whereas a rearranged developmental relation can construct a phenotype using old genes. Conversely, co-option of an ancestral component can create a new organization; “all parts are old” does not imply “the causal organization was latent.” Character identity is relational and mechanistic, not a count of new molecules. [DiFrisco, Love & Wagner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-020-09762-2)

No finite environmental assay proves that an ancestor could never instantiate the organization. The defensible conclusion is bounded: the ancestral capacity was not detected across a specified, theoretically motivated intervention domain, while descendants acquired a reproducible capacity within it.

## 9. Pre-enabled substrate versus predefined organizational type

Every artificial system supplies physical or computational primitives, boundary conditions, mutation possibilities, and inductive biases. “No relevant structure built in” is impossible and should be rejected.

A useful audit distinguishes two kinds of enablement:

### Generic enabling substrate

- local rules apply without consulting the later collective label;
- primitives such as contact, adhesion, division, diffusion, or communication are usable in multiple organizations;
- no fixed field enumerates the candidate collective’s membership, reproductive phase, or fitness;
- the same substrate permits alternative scales, boundaries, and life cycles;
- candidate organization is identified after evolution and validated independently.

### Designer-enumerated organizational alternative

- group IDs or nested levels determine interactions, inheritance, or resource accounting;
- a collective reproduction operator, fixed boundary, or life-cycle phase is invoked by the dynamics;
- fitness is assigned to an explicitly represented candidate collective as such;
- the genome selects among predeclared organizational modes whose semantics are supplied by the evaluator.

This is a graded model-audit distinction, not a metaphysical line. Universal construction and formal expressibility show that a substrate can realize a structure; they do not show that evolution can discover or sustain it. Conversely, generic affordances are not disqualifying merely because a designer knows they can support collectives.

Absence of predefined labels is necessary only for the strong artificial-life claim that evolution discovered the effective level rather than selected a supplied level. It is not necessary for a biological claim that a novel mechanism or ETI occurred, and it is never sufficient: analyst-defined post hoc groups can still be pseudo-individuals.

## 10. Observer and causal dependence

Any type claim requires a map \(\phi:X\rightarrow Z\) that identifies entities, stages, traits, and equivalence classes. A new value under \(\phi\) is not automatically a new causal organization, while a coarse \(\phi\) can erase a real difference.

Observer freedom cannot be eliminated because investigators must choose:

- spatial and temporal scales;
- candidate boundaries and stage classes;
- parent–offspring mappings;
- traits and fitness measures;
- intervention families and environmental controls;
- which causal consequences an equivalence must preserve.

These choices can nevertheless be constrained. A proposed type distinction is stronger when it is preserved under alternative recodings and coarse-grainings that retain the same parentage, reconstruction, fitness, and intervention relations; predicts interventions not used to construct the classification; and fails on ancestral and shuffled/null histories.

“No simple redescription makes A and B equivalent” is not an operational universal criterion because simplicity depends on a description language. Causal emergence can compare macro- and microdescriptions relative to an intervention distribution, while informational individuality can compare temporal integrity of candidate partitions. Neither chooses the uniquely correct evolutionary ontology. [Hoel, Albantakis & Tononi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1314922110), [Krakauer et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12064-020-00313-7)

The appropriate standard is therefore conditional causal objectivity: declare the equivalence and intervention domain, then demand that the distinction track stable differences in reproduction, inheritance, or individuation rather than visual salience or naming convention.

## 11. Necessary / sufficient evidence table

No row is universally sufficient. “Necessary” below is indexed to the strongest stated claim: a **new endogenous heritable/selectable collective organization**. Different answers apply to A–E.

| Evidence | Necessary? | Sufficient? | What it actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Novel morphology | No | No | A structural phenotype differs under a chosen descriptor. |
| New collective trait | Yes for a collective claim; not necessarily morphological | No | A group-level observable is causally meaningful; not that the group reproduces. |
| Heritable collective phenotype | Yes for C–E and the stated F | No | Parent–offspring resemblance/variance under specified ancestry and environment. |
| Collective-level selection | No for origin; yes for realized collective adaptation/MLS2 | No | Differential collective contribution for a specified trait and partition. |
| Untargeted emergence | No | No | Reduces one form of experimenter anticipation; does not establish individuality. |
| External outcome selection with an unspecified solution | Compatible | No | A fitness criterion was imposed while the mechanism remained evolutionarily supplied. |
| Ablation | No | No | Necessity of the targeted component/relation for the measured outcome in one background. |
| Rescue | No | No | Specific and reversible dependence in the rescued background; not historical origin. |
| Scaffold independence | Conditional: required for a claim of internalized/autonomous Darwinian properties, not for origin | No | Persistence or reconstruction without a specified external individuating process. |
| Closed collective life cycle | Yes for a new collective reproducer/individual | No | Proposed stages recurrently and multiplicatively re-establish one another. |
| New reproductive relation | Yes for a new kind of reproducing collective; not for every organizational novelty | No | Parent–offspring organization differs from the ancestral comparison. |
| New inheritance relation | A separate carrier: no. Reliable inheritance/reconstruction: yes | No | Type-relevant organization is transmitted or rebuilt; carrier novelty is not implied. |
| Internally generated individuating mechanisms | Yes for “endogenous” Darwinian individuality under the adopted account | No | Darwinian properties are not solely observer- or scaffold-produced. |
| Ancestral/descendant capacity contrast | Yes for “newly constructed” within a declared domain | No | Descendant reaction norm supports organization where matched ancestor does not. |
| Absence of predefined organizational labels | Conditional: yes for artificial level-discovery claims | No | Dynamics do not directly consult the post hoc collective identity. |
| Declared causal type-equivalence | Yes for a type claim | No | Makes “new type” testable rather than a visual or verbal judgment. |
| Robustness across causally adequate representations | Defeasibly necessary for non-arbitrariness | No | The result is not an artifact of one convenient encoding or threshold. |

A **conditionally sufficient evidential package** for the stated F is:

1. a declared candidate entity, life cycle, ancestry relation, environment, and equivalence over type-relevant causal relations;
2. evidence that descendants, but not matched ancestors across a bounded reaction-norm assay, construct the proposed reproductive/inheritance organization;
3. reliable reconstruction of the type-defining organization plus heritable variation in collective traits; if the organization itself is claimed to have been selected, historical heritable variation in a type-relevant organizational feature;
4. causal fitness relevance at the claimed level if “selectable” is asserted, or realized level-specific selection if adaptation is asserted;
5. individuating mechanisms that generate these properties with appropriate perturbation resilience rather than only observer grouping or imposed bookkeeping;
6. robustness to plausible causally adequate recodings and negative controls.

Intervention on the mechanism is one strong way to support items 2 and 5, but neither ablation nor rescue is mandatory. This package is defeasible and domain-relative, not a universal logical proof.

## 12. Counterexamples

### A — Explicit selection can yield an endogenous solution

Snowflake yeast evolved a clonal multicellular life cycle under explicit settling selection. The outcome was imposed; the `ACE2` route, persistent mother–daughter attachment, branching geometry, and fracture-based reproduction were not supplied as the organizational solution. The literature treats the result as a transition toward a new level of biological organization. Therefore “untargeted selection” is not necessary. [Ratcliff et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1115323109), [Ratcliff et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7102)

### B — Untargeted organization can be plastic without inherited construction

An ancestral reaction norm can produce aggregation or a palmelloid form only in a triggering environment. Repeated appearance across generations under the same trigger does not by itself establish collective parentage or evolved reconstruction. Untargeted appearance is therefore insufficient.

### C — Collective phenotype can arise under lower-level selection

Cell-level ecological selection can generate adhesion and collective resource sensing. The collective trait is causal and adaptive for cells, yet no collective-level fitness term is required. This separates B from D and E. [Colizzi, Vroomans & Merks 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.56349)

### D — Ablation/rescue can prove dependence without individuality

A gene knockout and complementation can remove and restore any ordinary cellular trait. If pigmentation, metabolism, or motility is heritable and selected, the full genetic intervention still does not create a new reproducing entity. The same inference limit applies to `ACE2`: complementation establishes the cell-separation mechanism, while collective reproduction, heredity, and individuality require independent evidence.

### E — Accepted transition evidence can be substantially scaffolded

Hammerschmidt et al. supplied collective transfer phases yet demonstrated an evolved developmental switch and fitness decoupling that the literature treats as key ETI events. Ecological-scaffolding theory explicitly allows external mechanisms to initiate Darwinian-like collective properties and asks whether they become endogenized later. Therefore initial pre-enablement does not invalidate every transition claim; it limits claims of autonomous completion. [Hammerschmidt et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13884), [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1086/719118)

### F — H–S–C–A can describe a new value of an old type

Consider a population of already established multicellular individuals in which a heritable change increases body size, body size affects reproduction, the causal growth pathway can be ablated and rescued, and no fitness objective names that pathway. Heredity, selection, causal intervention, and non-predefinition are all satisfied, yet the result can remain a new value of the existing character “body size.” Without a causal identity/equivalence criterion and an ancestral organization contrast, the Q013 conjunction is too weak to establish F.

### G — No universal checklist matches accepted ETIs

Fraternal and egalitarian transitions differ. Bottlenecks and germ–soma separation help individuate multicellular and eusocial collectives but are not essential in the same way for endosymbiotic collectives. Individuality and reproduction are graded, and different mechanisms can realize demarcation and policing. Requiring one fixed list would reject accepted intermediate or non-fraternal cases. [Godfrey-Smith 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421378112), [Clarke 2013](https://doi.org/10.5840/jphil2013110817)

### Falsification structure

Within a declared domain, the composite interpretation is refuted if any indispensable part of the particular claim fails. Examples are: matched ancestors express the same causal organization across the stated reaction-norm assay; no defensible closed collective parentage exists; collective recurrence is explained entirely by a persistent environment rather than ancestry and reconstruction; no heritable collective variation exists; an asserted selectable organizational feature has no causal effect on reproductive contribution; the supposed type difference disappears under causally adequate recoding; or the allegedly discovered relation is directly implemented by a predefined collective operator. Failure of one proposed ablation refutes that component’s claimed necessity, not automatically the existence or novelty of the organization, because another realizer may exist.

## 13. Central decision

**B — CONDITIONAL EVIDENTIAL CONJUNCTION**

No single criterion establishes endogenous organizational-type creation. A defensible conjunction exists only after the scientific claim is typed—A, B, C, D, E, or a specific F—and the entities, ancestry, environments, reproduction/inheritance relations, and causal equivalence are declared.

Qualifications:

- **C is also true of Q013:** untargeted selection, ablation, rescue, and total scaffold independence are not universally necessary.
- **D is also true of Q013:** heredity, selection, intervention, and non-predefinition can all hold for a new trait value of an old individual, so a comparative causal identity condition was missing.
- **F remains true at the universal level:** no finite observer-free checklist uniquely discovers organizational types. Conditional claims remain possible and scientifically useful.
- **E is partly true terminologically:** when the intended claim is a new Darwinian individual, “ETI” should replace “organizational-type creation.” The workspace term is useful only for explicitly broader, relation-level comparisons.

The selected decision is B because it captures the positive result: a claim-relative standard can discriminate the target phenomenon without pretending to be universal.

## 14. Status of the Q013 conjunctive gap

**PARTIALLY OVERSTATED**

Q013 correctly found that no reviewed case supplied its entire package. That absence is not a robust empirical deficit because two demanded elements—untargeted selection and ablation/rescue—are not necessary, and scaffold independence is stage- and claim-dependent. Conversely, the package was not sufficient because it lacked an explicit type-identity/equivalence condition and a bounded ancestral capacity comparison.

The empirical gap that survives is smaller:

> In the closest level-blind model, it has not yet been shown under a declared and representation-robust collective-life-cycle mapping that descendants acquire internally reconstructed reproduction/inheritance relations absent from matched ancestral reaction norms, with heritable fitness-relevant variation at that collective level. [Fernandes, Vroomans & Colizzi 2026, preprint v3](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.08.681199)

This is primarily a measurement and formalization gap plus model-specific causal validation. It is not evidence for a missing biological mechanism.

## 15. Remaining explanatory gap

Established theory says what would count conditionally, but it does not determine whether the closest level-blind case passes the test. The narrow next gap is to apply, in one recorded evolutionary history, the same declared mapping to ancestor, descendant, null, and perturbation data and ask whether:

1. a collective parent–offspring life cycle closes;
2. the organization is reconstructed by evolved internal relations;
3. matched ancestors lack the capacity across a bounded environment/intervention set;
4. type-relevant collective variation is heritable and fitness-relevant;
5. the result survives alternative causally adequate partitions.

The missing result is no longer “ablation plus rescue under untargeted selection.” It is comparative causal individuation of a new reproduction/inheritance organization. Knowing how to validate that result still does not explain what dynamics make such transitions common, recurrent, or open-ended.

## 16. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### KNOWN / ESTABLISHED

- Evolutionary individuality, evolutionary novelty, organizational closure, and causal emergence answer different questions and cannot be substituted for one another.
- Externally specified selection pressure is compatible with an endogenously evolved organizational solution.
- Collective organization may be reconstructed through lower-level heredity; a separate collective code is unnecessary.
- Origin, heritable establishment, selection, adaptive refinement, and ETI completion are temporally and conceptually distinct.
- Collective selection is unnecessary for first origin but necessary for claims of realized collective adaptation.
- Ablation and rescue establish background- and outcome-relative mechanistic dependence, not historical novelty, type identity, or individuality.
- No observer-independent universal type criterion is established; causal relations can constrain domain-specific classifications.

### UNDEREXPLORED

- Integrated application of life-cycle closure, ancestral reaction-norm comparison, internally generated individuation, collective heredity/fitness relevance, and representation robustness in a level-blind artificial model.
- Operational audits that distinguish generic substrate affordances from designer-enumerated organizational alternatives without demanding an impossible structure-free substrate.
- Negative controls in which the same post hoc detector correctly rejects recurrent aggregates and new values of existing collective traits.

### POTENTIALLY NOVEL

None. The conditional conjunction composes established evidential resources and is not a new scientific theory or mechanism.

### UNKNOWN

- Whether the propagule-forming Fernandes lineages pass the refined conditional standard.
- How robust candidate collective ancestry and life-cycle closure are to alternative causal coarse-grainings in aggregative or overlapping systems.
- Whether any general dynamical conditions promote repeated creation of new reproduction/inheritance organizations rather than one bounded ETI.
