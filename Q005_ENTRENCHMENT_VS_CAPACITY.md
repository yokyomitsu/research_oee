# Q005 — Entrenchment versus newly enabled capacity

2026-09-07. Exactly one theoretical research pass. This pass asks only how to distinguish an added dependency that entrenches an existing function from an organization that enables a genuinely new heritable capacity. It does not assume that the distinction is the key to OEE.

**Result:** The distinction is scientifically meaningful but not already unified by one accepted formalism. Existing theories cover different slices:

- Constructive Neutral Evolution (CNE) explains complexity and dependency accumulation without increased function.
- Generative entrenchment explains how upstream developmental features acquire many downstream dependencies; “generative” there means downstream production/dependence, not automatically generation of new evolutionary possibilities.
- Combinatorial entrenchment explains how standardized components can become a constructional alphabet for many later forms.
- Developmental bias, facilitated variation, genotype–phenotype maps, neutral networks, modularity, and evolvability describe changes in the distribution and accessibility of heritable variation.
- Evolutionary-novelty theory distinguishes new character identity, new function, innovation, and ecological success; Erwin explicitly separates potentiation, novelty, refinement, and innovation.
- Potentiation and exaptation describe routes by which prior states make later functions accessible or reuse old structures.

None of these, alone or in combination, gives a generally representation-robust criterion that identifies all newly enabled heritable capacities while excluding observer-selected trivialities. **Hypothesis decision: NO HYPOTHESIS JUSTIFIED.** The remaining gap is narrower than “new dependency versus new capacity” in the abstract: it is the lack of a validated comparison that preserves endogenous, heritable future accessibility while distinguishing it from dependency, reuse, or one-off function.

## 1. Problem statement

The V-ATPase case establishes the negative inference:

\[
\text{new obligatory component/dependency}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{new function or new evolutionary capacity}.
\]

A useful provisional distinction is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{entrenching change:}&\quad \mathcal K_{t+1}\approx\mathcal K_t,\\
\text{capacity-enabling change:}&\quad \mathcal K_{t+1}\supsetneq\mathcal K_t.
\end{aligned}
\]

The notation is not adopted as a theory because \(\mathcal K\) requires a choice of capacities, admissible environments, mutation/developmental dynamics, time horizon, and beneficiary. Existing literature uses more specific terms: **dependency**, **generative entrenchment**, **developmental bias**, **facilitated variation**, **evolvability**, **novelty**, **innovation**, **potentiation**, **exaptation**, **modularity**, and **character identity**. They should not be collapsed into one quantity.

The requested six-way distinction is necessary:

1. an added component;
2. an added dependency;
3. an added function;
4. a new character identity;
5. a new adaptive capacity;
6. a new future-generative capacity.

These can come apart. A component can become obligatory without adding a function. A new value of an existing function can be adaptive without being a new character. A new function can fail to alter future evolvability. A novel character can arise but have no later ecological or combinatorial exploitation. Conversely, an initially neutral or entrenched feature can later be exapted or incorporated into a reusable developmental or constructional system.

The maintained baseline is therefore:

\[
\text{organizational complexity}\not\Rightarrow\text{functional novelty}
\]

and the literature supports the further caution:

\[
\text{functional novelty}\not\Rightarrow\text{future generativity}.
\]

## 2. Constructive Neutral Evolution

**Fact:** CNE describes complexity increasing through effectively neutral steps rather than positive selection. A typical sequence is: an excess capacity permits a gratuitous interaction; a mutation whose effect is buffered becomes effectively neutral; the mutation drifts; the interaction becomes obligatory; purifying selection then prevents dissociation. The number of required parts or interactions increases while the higher-level function remains relatively unaltered. [Stoltzfus 1999](https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00006540), [Muñoz-Gómez et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00239-021-09996-y)

**Dependency and irreversibility:** CNE's ratchet is not absolute logical irreversibility. Reverse mutations or restoration of independence may be possible. The practical asymmetry comes from a biased random walk through dependency states: after many compensatory changes, returning to the independent state is increasingly improbable. Thus CNE can decrease reversibility and increase entrenchment without changing the system's main functional repertoire.

**Strong negative case:** CNE directly supplies the requested counterexample:

\[
\boxed{\text{dependency accumulation without possibility expansion}.}
\]

A newly essential paralog, editing dependency, chaperone dependence, or required interaction can preserve the same task while narrowing the set of viable component deletions. Complexity is increased, but functional novelty is not established. The word “constructive” refers to construction of complexity, not to an assured construction of new future evolutionary functions.

**Can CNE later create opportunities?** It can be compatible with later opportunity creation. A neutral change may alter robustness, degeneracy, interaction topology, or available substrates; a later mutation may then exploit that altered organization. The CNE literature distinguishes such histories from explicitly exaptive or adaptive phases. A CNE-derived dependency can therefore become a scaffold for later innovation, but that later use is an additional historical event, not a consequence guaranteed by CNE. CNE and later innovation are not mutually exclusive; neither is CNE sufficient for generativity.

This is an important rejection of a too-simple graph rule. Adding edges to a dependency graph can both entrench current operation and, in a later context, expose new routes. The sign of the change is not determined by edge count.

## 3. Generative entrenchment

**Fact:** Wimsatt and Schank define a feature as more generatively entrenched in proportion to the number of downstream features that depend on it. The original developmental-lock idea predicts that early developmental features tend to be more deeply entrenched because more later structure depends on them. [Wimsatt & Schank 1986](https://doi.org/10.1086/psaprocbienmeetp.1986.2.192789)

Here **generativity** means generation or dependence of downstream developmental structure/activity. It does **not** by itself mean that a feature increases the set of future evolutionary possibilities. In fact, the main evolutionary implication is conservative: deeply entrenched features are harder to change because modifications propagate to many dependents. Generativity and future evolutionary openness are therefore potentially opposed senses of “generative.”

Generative entrenchment captures:

- downstream dependency;
- developmental lock;
- differential constraint on evolutionary modification;
- why earlier or foundational organization can be difficult to alter.

It does not by itself capture:

- a new heritable function;
- a larger set of independently recombinable capacities;
- an increase in accessible future character identities;
- a representation-independent distinction between useful and inert downstream structure.

It is therefore close prior art for the entrenchment side, but not a solution to the capacity-enabling side. Calling a new component “generative” because many descendants depend on it would invert the desired distinction.

## 4. Combinatorial entrenchment

**Fact:** Wimsatt later uses **combinatorial entrenchment** for cases in which standardized components become a constructional “alphabet” from which many adaptive devices or artifacts can be made. He argues that standardization and interchangeable parts can support adaptive radiations in biology and culture. [Wimsatt 2013](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/9780262019552.003.0004)

This does capture a form of:

\[
\text{constraint at one level}
\rightarrow
\text{expanded combinatorial freedom at another level}.
\]

The same standardization that constrains the details of a part can increase the number of combinations in which that part participates. This is a genuine partial answer to the reusable-primitives question.

But combinatorial entrenchment is not equivalent to an intrinsic OEE criterion. It usually presupposes:

- a repertoire of components already identified as interchangeable;
- a constructional space in which combinations count as forms;
- a user, engineer, lineage, or ecological process for which the combinations are adaptive;
- a boundary between reusable component and surrounding context.

It shows how local constraint can support combinatorial expansion, but not that every such expansion is a new evolutionary dimension, heritable at the relevant level, or open-ended. Its overlap with the earlier “generative constraint” idea is substantial. That phrase should not be claimed as new terminology.

## 5. Developmental bias / facilitated variation

**Fact:** Developmental bias is the non-isotropic production of phenotypic variation caused by the structure, composition, or dynamics of developmental systems. Facilitated variation emphasizes developmental organization that makes some adaptive phenotypic changes easier to produce. Regulatory-network, genotype–phenotype-map, modularity, canalization, and neutral-network research all address how the map from heritable variation to phenotype shapes accessible evolution. [Uller et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.118.301760), [Wagner & Altenberg 1996](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1996.tb02339.x), [Kirschner & Gerhart 2008](https://doi.org/10.1002/9780470388160)

These theories distinguish more than variation quantity. They study:

- which phenotypes are generated readily;
- which are rare or inaccessible;
- how neutral networks preserve current phenotype while exposing alternative genotypes;
- how modularity reduces pleiotropic interference;
- how robustness can increase access to alternative phenotypes at the phenotype-network level.

They therefore supply established formalisms for changes in **accessible variation** and **evolvability**. They do not, by themselves, establish a new dimension of phenotype space. A changed developmental bias can redistribute probability over an existing phenotype space, change local neighborhoods, enlarge a neutral network, or reveal cryptic variation. Whether the resulting phenotype is a new character, a new function, or only a new value of an existing character remains a separate classification.

Wagner makes an especially useful distinction: a trait may be evolvable in the sense that heritable variation changes its value, yet not be evolvable in the stronger sense of innovation, such as acquiring a new catalytic activity. [Wagner 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.febslet.2005.01.063) This directly supports:

\[
\text{heritable adaptive variation}\not\Rightarrow\text{innovative future capacity}.
\]

Developmental bias and facilitated variation therefore cover much of the mechanism of capacity access, but retain model-relative phenotype and function descriptions. They are not a solved observer-free criterion.

## 6. Novelty / character identity / innovation

**Fact:** Müller and Wagner distinguish evolutionary novelty from ordinary modification of an existing character. Their character-based account requires a new structure to be neither homologous to an ancestral structure nor homonomous to another structure in the same organism. Wagner later grounds character identity in historically continuous character-identity networks, especially conserved regulatory kernels, rather than in one fixed shape or function. [Müller & Wagner 1991](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.22.110191.001305), [Wagner 2014](https://doi.org/10.23943/princeton/9780691156460.001.0001)

This gives a strong prior-art distinction between:

\[
\boxed{\text{new value of an existing character}}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\text{new individuated character}}.
\]

**Fact:** Erwin separates the origin of novel attributes, new functions, new clades, and ecological impact. His framework distinguishes generation of potential, formation of a novelty, adaptive refinement, exploitation, and ecological establishment/innovation. Fossil lags between origin and ecological success show that novelty and innovation can be decoupled. [Erwin 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12643)

Therefore a new character identity is not sufficient for a new future-generative capacity. It can be a terminal novelty, can remain maladaptive or unused, or can be refined without becoming a reusable primitive. Conversely, a new adaptive function can arise through an old character's changed use and need not be a new character identity. The requested distinctions already exist in fragmented but substantial form.

## 7. Potentiation and exaptation

### Potentiation

**Fact:** In the LTEE citrate case, earlier mutations increased the later tendency to evolve aerobic citrate use without themselves producing the full Cit\({}^{+}\) phenotype. Replay experiments showed that later historical backgrounds were more likely to produce the innovation. The staged account is commonly summarized as potentiation, actualization, and refinement. [Blount, Borland & Lenski 2008](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0803151105), [Quandt et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.09696)

Potentiation is distinguishable from ordinary historical contingency when a prior state changes the conditional probability, path accessibility, or fitness of a specified later outcome. It is prospective in a limited, target-relative sense: replaying alternative ancestors can reveal that a target is more accessible from one background than another. It is still often recognized retrospectively because the target was selected after the historical outcome was observed.

Potentiation is not simply equivalent to evolvability. Evolvability can mean capacity to generate heritable variation, variation in a trait, or innovation. Potentiation is a historical relation to a later specified outcome. It can be intrinsic to the reachable dynamics once mutation, development, environment, and target class are fixed; it is not observer-free when the target class itself is open.

The LTEE example supports a crucial separation: a prior mutation can enable later innovation without itself being a new function. But it does not show that every change increasing access to one target increases a general future possibility space.

### Exaptation

**Fact:** Gould and Vrba introduced exaptation for a feature that contributes to current fitness but was not originally produced by selection for its current role, including nonadaptive by-products later co-opted for use. Exaptation separates historical origin from present utility. [Gould & Vrba 1982](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0094837300004310)

Exaptation can represent:

- reuse of an old structure within an already available capacity space;
- a new function assembled from old capacities;
- the beginning of a new ecological or developmental trajectory;
- subsequent adaptive refinement of that reuse.

It does not, by definition, guarantee a new reusable primitive or an expanding future space. A single repurposing can solve a new problem and then become specialized or entrenched. A structure can also be exapted repeatedly and thereby participate in combinatorial expansion. Thus exaptation is a mechanism of possible capacity creation, not a sufficient criterion for it.

## 8. Reusable evolutionary primitives

Biology contains recognized analogues of reusable primitives, but no single necessary-and-sufficient definition of “generatively reusable.” Relevant cases include:

- developmental modules and modular genotype–phenotype maps;
- conserved regulatory motifs and character identity networks;
- gene duplication followed by subfunctionalization or neofunctionalization;
- protein domains reused in different molecular contexts;
- regulatory networks whose components can be recombined or redeployed;
- standardized or interchangeable components in combinatorial entrenchment.

The key contrast is not “necessary versus unnecessary.” A necessary component may be a bottleneck with one highly constrained role. A reusable component has, under specified developmental and ecological conditions, multiple heritable placements or interactions that preserve viability while differentiating downstream effects.

An operational but non-adopted diagnostic would require evidence for all of the following:

1. the component or organization is heritable and reconstructible;
2. it participates in at least two distinct selectable trajectories or functional roles;
3. those trajectories are reachable through the system's own variation and developmental dynamics, not only through engineering;
4. the extra routes are not merely observer-created relabelings of the same outcome;
5. the component's introduction changes future accessibility relative to a justified counterfactual.

This is a checklist for empirical analysis, not a new theory. Existing modularity, neutral-network, evolvability, character-identity, and exaptation literatures cover its parts.

## 9. Prospective versus retrospective generativity

Actual descendants and latent capacity must be separated.

\[
\text{actual historical descendants}
\neq
\text{all latent descendants reachable under the same dynamics}.
\]

A component can appear generative in hindsight because one descendant happened to exploit it. Conversely, cryptic variation or a large neutral network can provide latent potential not yet expressed. Existing approaches address this through replay experiments, ancestor reconstruction, mutational neighborhood analysis, neutral-network topology, and genotype–phenotype maps.

However, latent capacity is always conditional on:

- a mutation/recombination/developmental process;
- environmental inputs and ecological interactions;
- a time horizon or probability threshold;
- a class of outcomes being counted;
- a boundary and level of inheritance.

The with/without comparison

\[
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{with}\ c}\setminus\mathcal R_{\mathrm{without}\ c}\neq\varnothing
\]

is therefore a useful counterfactual template but not a complete criterion. It can count engineered paths unavailable to the lineage, trivial microscopic differences, or observer-selected target outcomes. Formal reachability and causal counterfactual tools can make a specified comparison rigorous; they do not choose the relevant outcome ontology.

The strongest conclusion is conditional: generativity can be prospective relative to a specified dynamics and target class, but general “future generativity” remains partly retrospective and representation-dependent.

## 10. Counterexamples

### A — Complexity and dependency without new function

CNE and the V-ATPase reconstruction show that complementary losses and obligatory components can increase complexity and reduce reversibility while preserving the overarching operation. This defeats any criterion based on component count, dependency count, entrenchment, or organizational complexity alone.

### B — New function with reduced future evolvability

**Logical construction, not a biological universal:** add a specialized function through a mutation that makes the function available but strongly reduces viable mutational neighborhoods or modular recombination. The current function is new, but the subsequent accessible heritable set is smaller. This is consistent with evolvability theory's distinction between changing a trait and acquiring new innovation capacity. It defeats “new function implies future generativity.”

### C — Novel character with no identifiable later possibilities

Erwin's distinction between novelty and innovation permits a novel character to arise before, or without, ecological exploitation. A novel character identity can be a terminal or poorly integrated addition. Character identity is therefore not sufficient for a new future capacity.

### D — Reusable module with no long-term novelty

A module can be structurally reusable in principle but remain unused, ecologically neutral, or trapped in a lineage that never reaches its alternative combinations. Reusability is a property of conditional accessibility, not proof of realized long-term novelty or OEE.

### E — Evolvability relative to an observer-specified task

Genotype–phenotype and evolvability measures can report an increased number of accessible phenotypes or improved arrival probability for a selected task while leaving the phenotype vocabulary and task class fixed. The increase is real relative to the assay but does not establish a new dimension of evolutionary possibility.

### F — Entrenchment later used for innovation

CNE, neutral variation, developmental constraint, or a dependency can later provide a substrate for exaptation or facilitated variation. Entrenchment and generativity are therefore not mutually exclusive over a lineage's history. The initial change may narrow present independence while a later context makes the organization useful for a different capacity.

### G — One function without future generativity

An adaptive improvement in a fixed task can reach an absorbing optimum or a state with no allowed heritable variants that alter the relevant task. It is new value or even new function relative to the previous state, but does not establish sustained future capacity.

## 11. Existing formal criteria

The following partial formalisms already exist:

| Question | Existing analogue | What it solves | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does a dependency increase? | CNE; generative entrenchment | Dependency, downstream reliance, practical irreversibility | Not whether future functions expand |
| Which variants are generated? | Developmental bias; facilitated variation; GP maps | Nonuniform variation and mutational neighborhoods | Not a universal character/function ontology |
| Can a phenotype reach alternatives? | Neutral networks; genotype neighborhoods; evolvability measures | Conditional accessibility and robustness | Usually local, target-relative, or representation-specific |
| Is there a new character? | Müller–Wagner novelty; character identity networks | Individuation and historical identity | Character novelty need not be future-generative |
| Did a prior state enable a later target? | Potentiation; replay and historical contingency | Conditional target accessibility | Target and horizon are selected; often retrospective |
| Was an old structure reused? | Exaptation | Historical origin versus present use | Reuse need not enlarge future capacity |
| Can standard parts support many forms? | Combinatorial entrenchment; modularity | Constraint at one level, combinatorial freedom at another | No universal heritability/open-endedness criterion |
| Does a causal distinction matter to survival? | Organizational function; semantic information | Endogenous or causal significance under stated assays | No complete future-capacity classifier |

The provisional dependency graph \(G_t=(N_t,E_t)\) is therefore a useful bookkeeping device, but node/edge growth is not enough. Entrenchment can be approximated by increased downstream dependence or loss of independent alternatives. Generative expansion requires evidence that a heritable organization changes the set or distribution of later selectable trajectories. The latter is close to established evolvability and reachability analysis, not a new graph invariant.

## 12. Remaining gap

The exact unresolved gap is:

> Given a change in a heritable organization, how can one identify an endogenous increase in the set of nontrivial, developmentally reconstructible, selectable future trajectories, while excluding (i) added dependence that only preserves an existing function, (ii) a one-off exaptation, (iii) a new value of an old character, and (iv) differences created only by the observer's outcome vocabulary?

Existing theory solves the parts separately. It does not establish a generally accepted criterion that is simultaneously:

- causal rather than purely descriptive;
- heritable rather than transient;
- prospective rather than only a retrospective story;
- sensitive to latent/cryptic potential;
- robust to reasonable changes of representation;
- independent of a preselected catalogue of future functions;
- strong enough to distinguish one innovation from sustained future generativity.

This is not evidence that the gap is a new biological principle. It is a missing synthesis/criterion in the reviewed literature, and may be impossible to make fully observer-independent if capacities are defined relative to environments, beneficiaries, and levels of organization.

## 13. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### Known / ESTABLISHED within cited scopes

- CNE can accumulate dependency and complexity without increased function, and practical reversibility can decrease.
- Generative entrenchment measures downstream dependence; its “generative” sense is not automatically future evolutionary generativity.
- Combinatorial entrenchment explicitly describes standardized components becoming a constructional alphabet for many later forms.
- Developmental bias, facilitated variation, GP maps, neutral networks, robustness, modularity, and evolvability already address biased and conditional access to heritable variation.
- Novelty, character identity, function, innovation, ecological success, potentiation, and exaptation are distinct concepts in existing evolutionary literature.
- New function and new character identity do not by themselves establish future generativity.

### Underexplored / PARTIALLY EXPLORED

- A common causal comparison that links dependency, heritability, accessibility, character identity, and future trajectory expansion.
- A criterion for reusable evolutionary primitives that does not assume a predefined task catalogue.
- Prospective assessment of latent capacity beyond a target chosen after historical discovery.

### Potentially Novel

No candidate terminology or mechanism is retained. “Generative constraint” overlaps substantially with combinatorial entrenchment, facilitated variation, developmental bias, modularity, and evolvability. “Generative entrenchment” cannot be repurposed to mean future possibility expansion without changing its established meaning.

### Unknown

- Whether a representation-robust and beneficiary-sensitive criterion for new heritable capacity is possible.
- Whether such a criterion can distinguish a reusable primitive from a merely necessary component without presupposing the future functions to be counted.
- Whether the resulting distinction has any necessity or sufficiency relation to a defensible form of OEE.

## 14. Hypothesis decision

**NO HYPOTHESIS JUSTIFIED.**

The novelty filter fails for the proposed “dependency versus capacity-enabling organization” distinction as a new hypothesis: its mechanisms and vocabulary are already distributed across CNE, generative/combinatorial entrenchment, developmental bias, facilitated variation, evolvability, modularity, character identity, potentiation, and exaptation. The search did not find a unified criterion, but the remaining gap is a difficult synthesis and measurement problem, not enough evidence for a new OEE mechanism.

The correct research result is therefore a narrowed boundary:

\[
\text{entrenchment is established as one route to complexity and dependence;}
\]

\[
\text{future-capacity expansion is established only conditionally and target-relatively;}
\]

\[
\text{no unified intrinsic criterion currently follows.}
\]

## 15. Highest-information next question

**Can an existing causal or predictive coarse-graining define a nontrivial, heritable, developmentally reconstructible future-capacity difference between two organizations while preserving cryptic potential and avoiding a prespecified catalogue of future functions?**
