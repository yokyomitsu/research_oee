# Q022 — Ecological construction of selection structure

Theoretical research pass, 2026-09-08. Scope: one prior-art and causal-boundary audit of F5. This pass does not implement or simulate anything, does not continue the Fernandes instrumentation branch, and does not perform Q023.

Epistemic convention: claims directly supported by the cited literature are **Fact**. Cross-literature syntheses and boundary judgments are **Interpretation**. Proposed tests are **Analysis specifications**, not hypotheses. No scientific **Hypothesis** is introduced.

## 1. Research question

> Do niche construction, ecosystem engineering, ecological inheritance, coevolution, and frequency-dependent selection fully explain how evolved organism–environment interactions create new selectively consequential ecological roles, dependencies, or beneficiaries; if not, what independently manipulable causal process distinguishes creation of a new selection structure from a fitness change within an existing one?

## 2. Prior-art boundary

The proposed frontier decomposes into established domains rather than one missing theory.

| Domain | Strongest established content relevant to Q022 | Boundary for Q022 |
|---|---|---|
| niche construction theory (NCT) | Organisms alter their own and other organisms' selective environments; construction can initiate as well as modify selection and can systematically bias its direction and temporal variance. | Already includes organism-generated new sources of selection. |
| selective niche construction | Persistent organismal modification affects differential survival or reproduction; the “evolutionary niche” is the sum of selective pressures acting on a population. | Allows a selective factor to enter or leave the effective niche, not only change magnitude. |
| ecological inheritance | Descendants encounter selection pressures or modified feature–factor relations caused by ancestral construction. | Can transmit a selectively consequential relation, not merely an inert environmental state. |
| ecosystem engineering | Organisms physically create, modify, maintain, or destroy habitat and resource availability for other organisms. | Covers production of new habitat and interaction opportunity; evolutionary response is not required. |
| eco-evolutionary feedback | Evolving traits alter ecological states, which alter subsequent selection and evolution. | A feedback may occur with fixed interaction identities or with changed realized interactions; feedback alone does not distinguish them. |
| frequency- and density-dependent selection | Fitness depends on frequencies or densities of conspecific and heterospecific types. | Represents changing ecological effects on fitness, but does not by itself identify the historical cause of an interaction. |
| coevolution and diffuse coevolution | Reciprocal selection changes interacting traits; additional community members can alter the strength or direction of pairwise selection. | Covers refinement, partner-dependent fitness, interaction sign changes, and multispecies selection context. |
| community evolution and evolutionary consequences of species interactions | Selection, drift, dispersal, and speciation alter community composition, while predators, competitors, mutualists, and indirect effects alter evolutionary trajectories. | Covers reciprocal evolutionary–community change without requiring a new generic kind called selection structure. |
| interaction-origin theory | Models and experiments address the origin of mutualism, antagonism, host use, syntrophy, food-web links, and interaction networks. | “Origin of an interaction” is an established research problem, not a Q022 discovery. |
| ecological opportunity and key innovation | Niche availability permits persistence and niche discordance generates divergent selection; dispersal, antagonist loss, environmental change, and key innovations can open previously inaccessible ecological states. | Covers access to new resources or adaptive zones, whether externally supplied or organism-enabled. |
| ecological fitting | Existing traits can permit persistence in a novel environment, resource, host, or association upon encounter. | A historically new interaction need not have been evolutionarily constructed. |
| dependency evolution | Mutualistic dependence, obligacy, specialization, syntrophy, endosymbiosis, and adaptive gene loss have explicit mechanisms. | A new dependency, including loss of independent viability, is not by itself a new theoretical category. |

The strongest NCT sources are explicit. Laland, Odling-Smee, and Feldman's population-genetic model makes a constructed resource a source of selection on a recipient locus and obtains fixation, polymorphism, and altered evolutionary dynamics. Matthews and colleagues distinguish constructor, environmental mediation, recipient, and recipient evolution in a pathway-based empirical framework. Laland, Odling-Smee, and Endler explicitly argue that niche construction *initiates and modifies* selection on constructors and other species and call novel construction “inceptive.” Community-level theory already places selection and other evolutionary processes alongside ecological species interactions, while coevolutionary theory treats pairwise, diffuse, and geographically variable interaction effects. [Laland, Odling-Smee & Feldman 1999](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.96.18.10242), [Matthews et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1890/13-0953.1), [Laland, Odling-Smee & Endler 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2016.0147), [Vellend 2010](https://doi.org/10.1086/652373), [Thompson 1999](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.284.5423.2116)

## 3. What niche construction already explains

**Fact.** Strong NCT already explains all of the following:

1. a constructor changes biotic or abiotic conditions;
2. the change modifies selection on the constructor, unrelated traits in the same population, descendants, or another species;
3. construction generates a persistent ecological legacy;
4. inherited environmental modification shifts later evolutionary dynamics;
5. construction generates stable, directed, or repeated selection rather than merely adding environmental noise;
6. inceptive construction initiates novel selection, while counteractive construction buffers or neutralizes existing selection;
7. relocation, habitat choice, and dispersal can alter the selective environment without physical engineering;
8. constructed environments can coordinate the evolution of suites of recipient traits and contribute to biodiversity.

NCT therefore includes more than

\[
\text{organism}\rightarrow\text{environmental state}\rightarrow\text{fitness value}.
\]

Its empirical schema is already

\[
C\rightarrow E_C\rightarrow S_B\rightarrow \Delta B,
\]

where \(C\) is a constructor, \(E_C\) its environmental effect, \(S_B\) selection on a recipient \(B\), and \(\Delta B\) an evolutionary response. Constructor and recipient may be the same population or different species. Niche construction exists when the modification mediates selection; “evolution by niche construction” additionally requires a caused evolutionary response in a recipient. This blocks the weaker claim that every engineering effect is evolutionarily consequential. [Odling-Smee et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1086/669266), [Matthews et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1890/13-0953.1)

**Interpretation.** “Creation of a new selection pressure” and “a new source of selection entering the selective niche” are already legitimate NCT descriptions. Q022 cannot retain those phrases as a distinct mechanism.

## 4. Sources and agents of selection

**Fact.** Selection measurements and causes are different. A phenotypic selection gradient identifies a statistical relation between traits and fitness; it does not by itself identify the environmental cause. Wade and Kalisz argue that identifying an agent of selection requires complementary manipulation of phenotype and environment. The biotic or abiotic agent supplies the ecological context in which phenotype–fitness covariance arises. [Wade & Kalisz 1990](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1990.tb04301.x)

The following distinctions should be kept:

| Term | Q022 use |
|---|---|
| **target of selection** | A phenotype or trait on which differential fitness is estimated; not the same as the organism, gene, or group described as a unit/object of selection. |
| **agent of selection** | A manipulable biotic or abiotic environmental cause of trait-dependent survival or reproduction. |
| **source of selection** | The process or environmental feature producing the agent or its statistical properties; in NCT, a constructed artifact, resource, or regulated condition can be such a source. |
| **selective environment / selective niche** | The set of conditions or selective pressures relevant to differential fitness in the specified population. |

Laland, Odling-Smee, and Endler distinguish a focal trait from a constructed versus nonconstructed source of selection and predict different variance and trait-coevolution patterns. Their analysis explicitly includes another species' construction as the source of selection—for example, an organism evolving to use a nest or other constructed feature. [Laland, Odling-Smee & Endler 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2016.0147)

**Interpretation.** If an evolved constructor creates a condition that makes a previously causally irrelevant environmental factor an agent of selection, established language already describes this as an inceptive, constructed source of selection. “Selection structure” adds no mechanism.

## 5. Fitness change within existing ecological relations

The provisional parameter-change case is well represented by quantitative genetics, population genetics, evolutionary game theory, adaptive dynamics, and eco-evolutionary models:

\[
w_i=w_i(z_i,\mathbf p,\mathbf N,E;\theta).
\]

A change in an optimum, selection gradient, payoff, density, frequency, encounter rate, or interaction coefficient can alter selection while the scientist retains the same variables and functional dependencies. Frequency dependence is precisely the case in which fitness varies with population composition. Density-dependent community models generalize this to heterospecific abundances and indirect effects. [Svensson & Connallon 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/eva.12714), [Vellend 2010](https://doi.org/10.1086/652373)

**Fact.** Strong niche construction can remain entirely in this category. Regulatory or counteractive construction can stabilize temperature, resources, or other already relevant variables, reducing the variance or magnitude of selection without adding a selective factor. Inceptive construction can change the optimum or initially intensify selection, after which adaptation may reduce directional selection. [Laland et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1086/706196), [Laland, Odling-Smee & Endler 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2016.0147)

Thus strong feedback, large fitness effects, and long persistence do not establish a change of “structure.”

## 6. Candidate structural change

Within a fixed ecological model, the proposed distinction is coherent:

\[
\text{same declared dependency, different magnitude}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\text{declared dependency absent, then present}.
\]

For example, a model may compare

\[
\frac{\partial W_B}{\partial R}=0
\quad\text{before an evolved modification, and}\quad
\frac{\partial W_B}{\partial R}\ne 0
\quad\text{after it}.
\]

**Interpretation.** This is a conditional description of how a selected ecological model changes. It is not an independently missing causal process. Existing NCT calls the relevant biological event initiation of selection by an inceptive constructed source; selection-agent research supplies causal identification; interaction-origin theory supplies domain-specific generating processes.

The distinction also fails as a universal topology claim. A small nonzero effect, a newly detectable rare interaction, a changed timescale, or a finer variable partition can turn a nominal zero into an edge without a new ecological kind. Conversely, an existing coarse edge can conceal a newly evolved mechanistic mediator. Edge identity must therefore come from domain knowledge and a declared intervention family, not from graph edit distance.

**Decision for this section:** a within-model structural/parametric distinction survives as an analysis convention, but no additional scientific mechanism survives prior-art absorption.

## 7. New ecological roles

“Role” has no sufficiently precise general evolutionary identity condition. It should be replaced by one or more operational relations:

- realized resource acquisition or transformation;
- trophic transfer with a stated consumer and resource;
- host use, infection, or enemy-mediated mortality;
- pollination, dispersal, protection, or another measured fitness-mediated service;
- habitat creation or occupancy;
- persistence or reproduction conditional on a partner or constructed state;
- a specified indirect effect through other community members.

New resource use, host shifts, trophic links, mutualisms, parasitisms, and partner switches all have established treatments. Key-innovation theory explicitly studies evolved traits that permit entry into previously inaccessible ecological states; ecological-opportunity theory studies whether the resulting state supports persistence and diversification. [Yoder et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2010.02029.x), [Schluter 2000](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-ecology-of-adaptive-radiation-9780198505228)

**Interpretation.** “New ecological role” is useful shorthand only after the relevant causal relation is specified. A human semantic relabeling is not a structural evolutionary event.

## 8. New dependencies

**Fact.** Dependence and obligacy have dedicated theory. The Black Queen Hypothesis explains adaptive gene loss when a costly, leaky function is supplied as a public good: beneficiaries can gain by losing the function until community production approaches the minimum that maintains it. This directly predicts the evolution of helper–beneficiary dependencies. Experimental evolution of syntrophy has shown rapid repeated erosion of independent function. Theory of mutualistic dependence separately analyzes multiple pathways toward and away from dependence. [Morris, Lenski & Zinser 2012](https://doi.org/10.1128/mbio.00036-12), [Hillesland et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1407986111), [Chomicki, Werner & Kiers 2020](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-110218-024629)

The proposed comparison

\[
W_A(B=0)>0
\quad\text{versus}\quad
W_A(B=0)\approx 0
\]

can operationalize one background-relative dependence transition. It is not universal: partner removal may change many variables, fitness can be frequency- and environment-dependent, and long-term lineage persistence is not reducible to one individual's instantaneous reproduction. “Obligate” and “facultative” are scientifically useful endpoints or regions, not proof of a new causal ontology.

**Interpretation.** Obligation is often a quantitative dependence crossing a biologically meaningful viability or life-cycle boundary. Its mechanisms—adaptive loss, relaxed selection, drift, trade-offs, partner fidelity, cotransmission, and ecological specialization—are established. It can reduce autonomy, rescue probability, or access to alternative environments, so dependency creation is not generativity or OEE. [de Mazancourt, Loreau & Dieckmann 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0022-0477.2004.00952.x), [Hammarlund et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008732)

## 9. New beneficiaries / recipients

**Fact.** Constructor and recipient are already separate roles in NCT. A constructor's modification must affect selection on a recipient; the recipient may be the constructor, descendants, another species, or several community members. Evolution by niche construction requires a caused evolutionary response in at least one recipient population. [Matthews et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1890/13-0953.1)

Ecosystem-engineering theory independently covers resource and habitat creation for other organisms, including effects that do not feed back to the engineer and do not cause evolution. Engineers can have positive, negative, large, or trivial effects on other species. [Jones, Lawton & Shachak 1997](https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658(1997)078%5B1946:PANEOO%5D2.0.CO;2)

For

\[
A\rightarrow E_A\rightarrow W_B,
\]

the appearance of \(B\) as a new beneficiary is therefore fully described by ecosystem engineering if only ecological consequence is claimed, and by NCT if \(E_A\) mediates selection on \(B\). Ecological opportunity describes whether \(B\) can establish and diversify; coevolution applies only if reciprocal evolutionary change follows.

**Interpretation.** No unfilled constructor–recipient mechanism remains. The open issue in any case is evidential: did the constructor cause the recipient-specific selection, did \(B\) merely encounter a latent compatibility, and was there an evolutionary response?

## 10. Ecological opportunity

Ecological opportunity is not simply an empty named niche. Wellborn and Langerhans define two lineage-relative components:

1. **niche availability:** a phenotype absent from the community can persist under the abiotic and biotic conditions;
2. **niche discordance:** those conditions generate diversifying selection rather than only shifting one optimum.

[Wellborn & Langerhans 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.1347)

Sources include dispersal into a new environment, antagonist loss, resource change, and key innovations. Organisms can also generate opportunity for other lineages by engineering habitat or resources. NCT supplies the causal provenance when that opportunity is constructed.

**Interpretation.** Ecological opportunity specifies prospective persistence and selection consequences better than “new role,” but it leaves historical provenance open. It can be externally created, encountered, or organism-generated. Combining it with NCT is sufficient to describe

\[
A\rightarrow E'\rightarrow
\bigl(\text{persistence of }B+\text{altered selection on }B\bigr).
\]

No additional “selection-structure creation” process is required.

## 11. Eco-evolutionary feedback

**Fact.** A canonical feedback is

\[
\text{evolving trait}\rightarrow\text{ecological state}
\rightarrow\text{selection}\rightarrow\text{evolving trait}.
\]

Post and Palkovacs explicitly require a population to alter its environment and the environmental change to feed back on subsequent evolution. Such feedbacks affect communities and ecosystems. [Post & Palkovacs 2009](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2009.0012)

A feedback can stay inside a fixed declared network: predator and prey traits and abundances can cycle while the predator–prey relation remains the same modeled dependency. Diffuse coevolution likewise asks whether a third species changes the strength or direction of selection in a focal pair, not whether a new edge exists. [Iwao & Rausher 1997](https://doi.org/10.1086/285992)

Evolution can also change realized interaction patterns. Empirical and theoretical work treats interaction turnover, partner switching, mutualism/antagonism shifts, and evolutionary emergence of food webs. But writing \(G_t\ne G_{t+1}\) does not identify a new mechanism: it records the outcome of evolved trait matching, behavior, abundance, dispersal, or environmental mediation.

## 12. Origin versus evolution of ecological interactions

The origin of selectively consequential interaction is not absent from evolutionary theory.

- Doebeli and Knowlton model the evolution of mutualism from a neutral relation when partner investment yields return and spatial competitive structure supports it. [Doebeli & Knowlton 1998](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.95.15.8676)
- Loeuille and Loreau derive complex size-structured food webs from mutation, ecological interactions, and adaptation starting from one ancestor. [Loeuille & Loreau 2005](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0408424102)
- Hansen and colleagues observed a simple mutation in one bacterium change a two-species biofilm association to mutual benefit; the mutation was deleterious without the partner. [Hansen et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05514)
- Contact-dependent antagonism has evolved repeatedly through single-base substitutions, showing that a small inherited change can create a newly measurable antagonistic interaction. [Navarro Llorens et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.1234)
- Thompson's coevolutionary framework treats interspecific interactions themselves as evolutionarily malleable and places pairwise, multispecies, and geographic variation in one program. [Thompson 1999](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.284.5423.2116)

**Interpretation.** Theory is fragmented by interaction type because the causal mechanisms differ. That fragmentation does not establish a higher-order mechanism gap. “Origin of interaction” is mature enough to absorb the generic Q022 claim, while particular origins can remain empirically or mechanistically unresolved.

## 13. Ecological fitting

**Fact.** Ecological fitting occurs when organisms persist in a novel environment, use a novel resource, or form a novel association using traits already carried at encounter. Proposed bases include phenotypic plasticity, correlated traits, and phylogenetic conservatism. It can initiate later evolution or maintain stasis. [Agosta & Klemens 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2008.01237.x)

This is the decisive counterexample to equating historical novelty with evolutionary construction:

\[
\text{new encounter}+\text{pre-existing compatibility}
\rightarrow\text{new realized interaction}.
\]

Migration, disturbance, range change, or species introduction can therefore produce \(R\rightarrow W\) at a location without an evolved trait change constructing \(R\). Later adaptation or coevolution does not retroactively make the first interaction evolutionarily constructed.

**Interpretation.** A bounded construction claim would require a specified evolved change to be necessary for the selective consequence under matched encounter conditions. This is Q015's construction-versus-latency discipline applied to ecology; it is not a new ecological mechanism.

## 14. Causal/intervention formulation

The proposed intervention language is defensible only as a case-specific analysis specification. For a declared population, life stage, ecological background, time horizon, fitness proxy, and relation variable \(R\), define

\[
\Delta_R^{(t)}=
\mathbb E[W\mid \operatorname{do}(R=r_1),t]
-\mathbb E[W\mid \operatorname{do}(R=r_0),t].
\]

A bounded irrelevance-to-relevance result requires:

\[
|\Delta_R^{(\mathrm{before})}|\leq \varepsilon
\quad\text{and}\quad
|\Delta_R^{(\mathrm{after})}|>\varepsilon,
\]

where \(\varepsilon\) is justified by biological resolution and uncertainty, not chosen to manufacture an edge. For evolutionary construction, a second intervention must show that a specified evolved change \(D\) is necessary or difference-making for the new effect:

\[
D\rightarrow R\rightarrow W,
\]

with mediation or matched restoration evidence excluding encounter alone, background environmental change, and pre-existing compatibility.

Feasible interventions can include partner exclusion/addition, resource restoration, constructor ablation/restoration, common-garden encounter, reciprocal transplant, or environmental manipulation. They need not literally set an abstract relation variable. Wade and Kalisz's agent-of-selection methodology already supplies the core logic. Structural causal notation adds bookkeeping, not a domain criterion or generating mechanism. [Wade & Kalisz 1990](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1990.tb04301.x)

**Interpretation.** Even a successful before/after test establishes a newly active selective pathway only relative to the declared causal model. It can still be represented as a smoothly changing interaction function whose value crossed the assay's resolution. The result should be reported as identification of an evolved, constructed source/agent of selection, not promoted to a universal “selection-structure” event.

## 15. Observer and representation dependence

Ecological networks do not supply observer-free edge identity.

- Nodes may be individuals, genotypes, populations, species, guilds, resources, or abiotic states.
- A direct interaction at one resolution can be an indirect pathway at another.
- Edges may represent encounter, consumption, demographic effect, selection gradient, material flow, or causal fitness effect.
- Absence may mean a forbidden link, no co-occurrence, low abundance, rare encounter, missed detection, or an effect below resolution.
- Temporal aggregation can turn intermittent interactions into persistent edges or erase short decisive interactions.

Probabilistic-network work explicitly warns that binary graphs turn every nonzero interaction into certainty and that thresholds discard low-probability interactions; sampling theory distinguishes missing interactions from biologically forbidden links. [Poisot et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12468), [Jordano 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.12763)

**Interpretation.** A causal model can validate a declared ecological relation, but cannot choose its nodes, outcomes, interventions, timescale, or tolerance. This reproduces Q016's domain-theory lesson. The representation dependence does not make every distinction useless; it prevents graph topology from defining a universal evolutionary kind.

## 16. Strong counterexamples

| Case | Counterexample | What it defeats |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Regulatory niche construction buffers temperature or another already relevant factor and changes the magnitude or variance of selection. | Strong construction does not imply a new selective relation. |
| **B** | Ecological fitting after migration or range change yields a new host/resource association using pre-existing traits. | Historically new interaction does not imply evolutionary construction. |
| **C** | An ecosystem engineer creates habitat and changes another species' abundance, but the recipient shows no caused evolutionary response. | New beneficiary and large ecological effect do not imply evolution by niche construction. |
| **D** | Black Queen dynamics generate helper dependence by adaptive function loss, narrowing independent viability and potentially increasing collapse risk. | New dependency does not imply increased evolvability or OEE. |
| **E** | Predator–prey or host–parasite coevolution produces large, persistent trait change while the declared interaction remains fixed. | Coevolutionary novelty does not imply topology change. |
| **F** | A simple mutation changes a biofilm association to mutual benefit; single substitutions can also generate contact-dependent antagonism. | Magnitude of genetic change does not determine whether an interaction is operationally new. |
| **G** | An engineer creates habitat or a resource later exploited by another lineage. | The focal constructor–recipient pattern is already covered jointly by engineering, NCT, and opportunity. |
| **H** | A novel selective interaction arises through dispersal plus ecological fitting, without environmental construction. | Niche construction is not necessary for new realized selective relations. |
| **I** | Nests, burrows, dams, and other constructed features become sources of selection on constructors, unrelated traits, descendants, and other species. | NCT explicitly predicts the strongest alleged residual: an organism-generated new source of selection. |

Case I is decisive. It is not merely compatible with NCT; “inceptive niche construction” is the established category for organismal activity that initiates novel selection. [Laland, Odling-Smee & Endler 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2016.0147)

## 17. Strongest prior-art absorption test

Attempted claim:

\[
\text{F5 is completely contained in NCT}
+\text{ ecological opportunity}
+\text{ coevolution}
+\text{ interaction-evolution theory}.
\]

The claim survives the destructive test.

1. If evolved activity modifies an environment and thereby initiates selection on self, descendants, or another population, it is selective/inceptive niche construction.
2. If the effect persists across generations, it is ecological inheritance of a modified selective niche or feature–factor relation.
3. If organisms physically create resource or habitat availability, regardless of evolution, it is ecosystem engineering.
4. If the constructed condition permits a new phenotype or lineage to persist and experience divergent selection, it is organism-generated ecological opportunity.
5. If a new association uses previously carried traits, it is ecological fitting, not evolutionary construction.
6. If interacting populations reciprocally change, it is coevolution; if community members modify that selection, diffuse or network coevolution applies.
7. If interaction traits, signs, partners, or trophic links originate or change, interaction-origin, mutualism, host-shift, game-theoretic, adaptive-dynamic, and evolving-food-web theories already address the event.
8. If fitness depends on type composition, frequency- or density-dependent selection supplies the general mathematical form.
9. If loss or specialization makes a partner necessary, dependency, obligacy, syntrophy, endosymbiosis, and Black Queen theories provide mechanisms.

**Interpretation.** The only remainder is a case-specific validation question: whether a declared relation became a causal agent of differential reproduction because of an evolved constructor trait rather than encounter, sampling, or latent compatibility. That is not an additional mechanism and does not justify preserving “selection structure” as a new theoretical term.

## 18. Central decision

\[
\boxed{\textbf{A — EXISTING THEORY SUFFICIENT}}
\]

NCT plus ecological opportunity, coevolution, ecological fitting, and interaction-origin/dependency theory capture every scientifically defensible reading found for a single transition. “Creation of new selection structure” adds no independently established mechanism beyond initiation of a constructed source/agent of selection or origin of a specified ecological interaction.

This decision does not claim that every particular interaction origin is empirically explained. It claims that Q022 found no general phenomenon requiring a new explanatory category.

## 19. Mechanism readiness

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NOT READY}}
\]

The proposed target is not distinct from standard inceptive niche construction and established interaction/dependency evolution. Its causal identification is possible only after domain variables and interventions are declared, and closest prior art already provides multiple generating mechanisms. Lack of a universal graph metric cannot upgrade a validation issue into a mechanism gap.

## 20. Remaining explanatory gap

No Q022-level mechanism gap survives.

The narrow residual is **case-specific causal validation**:

> For a specified constructor, recipient, ecological relation, background, and fitness outcome, did an evolved constructor trait cause that relation to become a selective agent, rather than merely change an existing effect or coincide with ecological fitting, migration, abundance change, or improved detection?

This can be resolved with established environmental/trait manipulations, ancestral–descendant common conditions, and mediator tests. It is not a general explanation of anti-closure and it does not establish OEE. Recursion of such events is explicitly outside Q022.

## 21. Problem classification

**Primary classification: VALIDATION.**

**Secondary classification: FORMALIZATION, conditional and case-specific.** A declared causal model can separate within-model effect-size change from activation/removal of a pathway.

**Not a surviving classification: MECHANISM.** Established theories already supply the relevant mechanisms. The general term also has a definition problem if detached from domain variables, but that is a reason to retire it, not a new frontier.

## 22. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

- **ESTABLISHED:** organisms can initiate and modify selection on themselves and other species; constructors and recipients can differ; ecological legacies persist; ecological engineering creates habitats and resources; ecological opportunity can be organism-generated; interactions and dependencies originate and evolve; ecological fitting can generate new associations without prior evolutionary construction.
- **PARTIALLY EXPLORED:** how entire interaction networks and multispecies selection contexts change through eco-evolutionary dynamics. Many domain-specific frameworks exist; coverage is uneven across systems.
- **UNDEREXPLORED:** matched before/after causal identification of a newly active recipient-specific selection pathway in particular natural systems, especially with detection, abundance, and latent compatibility controlled. This is a validation program, not evidence of a missing general mechanism.
- **POTENTIALLY NOVEL:** none justified by Q022.
- **UNKNOWN:** whether any particular claimed interaction origin passes the bounded causal test without case-specific data; whether recursively generated selective relations contribute to OEE was not investigated.

## 23. Deep-learning contribution

**None.** Deep learning adds no necessary abstraction to Q022. The fixed-representation/changed-representation analogy does not resolve ecological variable choice, interaction identity, or causal provenance; evolutionary ecology and causal manipulation already state the problem directly.

\boxed{\textbf{EXISTING THEORY SUFFICIENT}}
\]

No candidate causal or dynamical relation survives the prior-art firewall. `HYPOTHESES.md` remains unchanged.
