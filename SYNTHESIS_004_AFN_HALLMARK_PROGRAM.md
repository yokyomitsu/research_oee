Internal consistency therefore must not be described as independent reproducibility, independent replication, or external validation.

## 12. Empirical constraint level

\]

It is not strongly constrained because the positive within-history record is sparse: Stringmol supplies two events, while Avida and Geb supply one each under the reviewed criterion. It is not weakly constrained because the evidence distinguishes real alternatives. Negative controls fail at different N/F/H/A/C links, and the frozen workflow handles three substrates without logical exceptions.

The program can reject inflated AFN claims. It cannot yet discriminate general theories of continued AFN generation.

### Return to the field-level problem

The AFN program sharpens, but does not answer:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Why do artificial evolutionary systems rarely}
\atop
\text{show continued adaptive functional innovation?}
}

**Facts / established within the reviewed record:** genuine finite AFN is achievable; self-reproduction and Darwinian adaptation lie well below that target; evolutionary activity and complexity growth do not by themselves establish functional innovation; a platform's functions across separate experiments are not a within-history sequence; and accepted functional-class recurrence is empirically sparse. Reconstructing it requires histories that align novelty, causal role, heredity, adaptive consequence, and class comparison.

**Interpretation:** the observed field-level shortfall combines sparse phenomenon evidence with difficult observability. The matrix's heterogeneous failures prevent attributing that shortfall to one common causal bottleneck.

**Unknown:** why some artificial histories generate even finite class-distinct AFN and why continued class expansion is rarely established. The current evidence does not yet turn that unknown into a mechanism frontier.

## 13. Mechanism readiness

The AFN hallmark program is:

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NOT READY FOR MECHANISM}}
\]

A falsifiable mechanism program would require reproducible event classification, several positive and negative histories with comparable causal contrasts, and observed or manipulable variation in candidate conditions. The current record has none of these in combination.

Stringmol's two events establish that finite AFN is achievable, but they do not isolate why two occurred, why no third was observed, or which condition differs from an appropriate control history. The no-third observation is censored and measurement-limited. A system-specific “why two but not three?” question would therefore overinterpret one record. A cross-system “why finite AFN but little continued expansion?” question would aggregate heterogeneous and sparse evidence before a common causal contrast exists.

The two most tempting mechanism frontiers fail their first attack:

- **“Why does Stringmol generate two classes but no third?”** fails because one censored record does not establish a terminal transition, and no matched causal contrast identifies what differs after the second event.
- **“Why do artificial histories show finite AFN but little continued class expansion?”** fails because the positive histories are too few, differently observed, and causally unmatched to establish one shared explanandum beyond publication-level non-demonstration.

No new mechanism frontier survives attack at this stage.

External researchers are one way to supply that condition, not a separate theoretical necessity.
