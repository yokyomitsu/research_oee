# Formalization 001 — AFN Functional-Class Identity

Date: 2026-09-09  
Operation: exactly one M3 formalization/methodology pass  
Scope: `SAME / DISTINCT / UNRESOLVED` functional-class comparison only; no empirical event discovery or reclassification, event-method revision, ongoingness analysis, mechanism research, simulation, or hypothesis creation

## Decision summary

The central decision is:

\[
\boxed{\text{B — PAIRWISE CAUSAL-ROLE INTEGRATION SUFFICIENT}}
\]

Established function pluralism already supplies the conceptual separation M3 needs. Activity identifies what happens; causal-role analysis identifies how an activity contributes to a declared system capacity; biological-advantage and selected-effect analyses address counterfactual value and selection history. Because the AFN framework already validates adaptation separately, functional-class identity should compare the middle, causal-role level—not past selection, fitness outcome, or implementation identity.

No universal function ontology is required. The weakest adequate comparison is indexed to a predeclared local comparison contract:

\[
D=(K,Q,g),
\]

where (K) is the causally relevant context, (Q) is the domain-grounded explanatory question/target, and (g) is the named explanatory resolution. At that contract, an event's minimal functional-role signature is:

\[
\Phi_D(e)=\bigl(B,\;P\xrightarrow{E}Y\bigr),
\]

where (B) is the bearer/actor, (P) the process or relation acted upon, (E) the causally relevant effect, and (Y) the system capacity or relation to which that effect contributes. Context and resolution are contract metadata rather than role fields. Beneficiary is recorded in the separate adaptation evidence unless it is itself part of the interaction represented by (P\xrightarrow{E}Y).

The classification rule is:

- **SAME:** distinct realizations preserve the same relevant causal-contribution relation under (D);
- **DISTINCT:** a domain-grounded causal contrast changes (P), (E), or (Y) in a way relevant under (D), rather than merely changing implementation, activity detail, selective challenge, or ultimate fitness outcome; and
- **UNRESOLVED:** the evidence or admissible explanatory resolution does not determine whether the apparent difference is role-level or implementation-level.

This is a structured expert judgment, not an algorithm. Its constraint comes from freezing (K,Q,g), making the role signatures and causal evidence explicit, recording provenance, and auditing classifications across admissible alternative resolutions.

\]

The historical artifact is not modified. Functional-identity readiness is:

\boxed{\text{READY WITH STRUCTURED JUDGMENT}}
\]

## 1. Why M3 was selected

The remaining weakness was concentrated in class identity. M1 therefore decided **B — METHOD VALIDATED WITH FUNCTIONAL-CLASS LIMIT**, assigned **READY WITH LIMITATIONS**, and selected M3. The present pass asks only how already reconstructed events should be compared as `SAME`, `DISTINCT`, or `UNRESOLVED`. It does not reopen N, F, H, A, event representation, or ongoingness and does not revise any earlier event count.

## 2. Exact formalization problem

Given two reconstructed event claims (e_i,e_j), the problem is to decide:

\[
C(e_i,e_j)\in\{\mathrm{SAME},\mathrm{DISTINCT},\mathrm{UNRESOLVED}\}
\]

without assuming that:

- different mechanisms imply different functions;
- common activity implies common function;
- a common selective challenge implies common function;
- a common fitness outcome implies common function; or
- all systems share one function taxonomy.

The desired result is a reproducible within-history comparison protocol. It is not a universal definition of biological function, an automatic classifier, or a new AFN theory.

## 3. Function-theory prior art

### Four notions and function pluralism

Wouters distinguishes four explanatory uses: activity, biological role, biological advantage, and selected effect. Activity is what an item does; role is its contribution to a larger activity or capacity; advantage is its counterfactual value relative to an alternative; selected effect explains how a trait gained or maintained representation through past selection. These notions answer different questions and need not be unified. [Wouters 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsc.2003.09.006)

Godfrey-Smith likewise argues for consensus without unity: Cummins-style current causal contribution and historical/etiological function are both legitimate and distinct. This supports a task-relative methodological choice rather than a claim that one philosophical account eliminates the others. [Godfrey-Smith 1993](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0114.1993.tb00358.x)

### Causal-role / systemic function

Cummins analyzes a function as a component capacity singled out by its contribution to an analytical account of a containing system's capacity. The same activity can have different functions in different analytical contexts, and not every effect is explanatorily useful. Functional analysis therefore supplies the needed relational form, but only after a scientifically relevant containing capacity and decomposition are declared. [Cummins 1975](https://mechanism.ucsd.edu/~bill/teaching/w10/cummins.functions.1975.pdf)

Amundson and Lauder show that nonhistorical causal-role functions are indispensable in comparative and functional anatomy and can themselves be used in historical evolutionary analysis. This directly supports using present causal role to identify a function while investigating its historical origin separately. [Amundson & Lauder 1994](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00850375)

### Selected-effect function

Selected-effect theory identifies a proper function with the effect for which past tokens of a trait were selected. It is useful for explaining trait presence and grounding normativity. It is not suitable as the AFN class-identity rule because AFN already has an independent adaptation criterion and because historical selection can differ while current causal roles match. [Neander 1991](https://doi.org/10.1086/289610)

### Biological advantage

Wouters' biological-advantage notion uses counterfactual comparison to ask how one trait state benefits a bearer relative to another. That is valuable evidence for AFN's A criterion. If used to define class identity, however, it would collapse roles that share an ultimate reproductive advantage and duplicate the adaptation test.

### Organizational function

Mossio, Saborido, and Moreno define biological functions as causal relations in differentiated, organizationally closed self-maintaining systems: the trait contributes to maintaining the organization and is itself produced and maintained under organizational constraints. This gives a principled teleological and normative grounding where organizational closure is established. [Mossio, Saborido & Moreno 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axp036)

It cannot serve as the general AFN identity criterion. The event-history method deliberately covers molecular replicators, digital programs, embodied agents, collectives, and ecological relations without first proving closure or autonomous self-maintenance. Even inside an autonomous system, “contributes to closure” may be too coarse to distinguish discrimination, repair, avoidance, and interference. Organizational function is therefore:

`DOMAIN-SPECIFIC OPTION ONLY`.

### Prior-art boundary

The philosophical literature already establishes the ingredients: plural senses of function, causal-role relativity to system capacity, historical selected effects, counterfactual advantage, and domain-specific organizational grounding. M3 does not contribute a new theory of function. Its contribution is to select and integrate the causal-role sense for C while explicitly reserving selection/advantage for A.

## 4. Why function and adaptation must remain separate

The AFN conjunction is evidentially useful only if F and A can fail independently:

\[
F=\text{what causal role is realized},
\qquad
A=\text{whether that role is adaptively favored or maintained}.
\]

Defining F by selected effect would make successful A evidence partly constitutive of F and then reuse it to validate A. Defining functional sameness by biological advantage would classify fight, flee, repair, and avoidance together whenever they deliver the same survival or reproductive benefit. Defining function by current causal role avoids both circularities.

Two counterfactual questions must therefore remain distinct:

| Counterfactual | Question | Methodological location |
|---|---|---|
| Functional-role counterfactual | Would removing, replacing, or disabling the activity change the declared system capacity or relation (Y)? | F/C reconstruction |
| Adaptive counterfactual | Would the presence of the role change reproductive success, persistence, or competitive contribution relative to an alternative? | A validation |

The first can identify a contribution without proving that it is favored. The second can establish advantage without determining whether two advantageous roles are the same function.

## 5. Functional homology / equivalence prior art

Love distinguishes activity—what something does—from use—what it is for—and argues that assessments of activity-function homology require more than superficial sameness. Relative position, connection, temporal organization, and hierarchically interconnected dependencies help determine correspondences. He also argues that selected-effect function is not primary for much comparative, structural, and experimental reasoning. [Love 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-007-9093-7)

AFN classes are not homologues. Love's account uses ancestry/developmental constraint and treats homology as an equivalence relation; AFN may compare independent implementations and local causal roles without common ancestry. The transferable methodological lesson is narrower:

\[
\boxed{\text{functional identity is relational and organizational, not activity or implementation identity}.}
\]

“Functional equivalence” is similarly useful only after a context and target capacity are declared. Same output value, task success, or reproductive outcome does not imply equivalence. Two realizations are equivalent for M3 only with respect to the same relevant causal contribution under the declared comparison contract.

## 6. Implementation versus function

Let (M(e)) denote implementation and \(\alpha(e)\) the observed activity or execution. Neither determines class identity alone:

\[
M_i\neq M_j\not\Rightarrow C(e_i,e_j)=\mathrm{DISTINCT},
\]

\[
M_i=M_j\not\Rightarrow C(e_i,e_j)=\mathrm{SAME}.
\]

Different sequences, opcodes, neural circuits, or molecular traces can preserve the same causal contribution. Conversely, one substantially unchanged mechanism can enter a new context and acquire a new role. The comparison must pass through the role signature rather than from implementation directly to class.

Evidence for functional-role novelty requires:

1. a reconstructed activity and causal pathway;
2. a role-relevant difference in the target process, effect, or contributed capacity under the frozen contract;
3. domain evidence that the difference is explanatory rather than a trace-level variation; and
4. independent N/H/A support before the role becomes an accepted AFN event.

## 7. Outcome versus function

Ultimate outcomes such as survival, persistence, population increase, or reproductive success belong to adaptation evidence. They are deliberately too coarse for function identity:

\[
\text{same reproductive outcome}\not\Rightarrow\text{same function}.
\]

At the opposite extreme, matching one immediate output is too weak. The same numeric or physical result can be generated through causally different relations. Functional comparison therefore requires the intermediate description (P\xrightarrow{E}Y): what is acted upon, what causal difference is made, and what system capacity or relation that difference realizes.

## 8. Selective challenge versus function

A selective challenge is contextual metadata, not a class criterion:

\[
\text{same selective challenge}\not\Rightarrow\text{same functional class}.
\]

Fighting and fleeing may both address a threat, while one reduces an opponent's capacity and the other increases spatial separation. Repair and damage avoidance can both answer the same hazard while acting on different processes. Conversely, the same causal role may be advantageous under different challenges.

The comparison record therefore asks whether the selective challenge is shared, but never infers `SAME` from that answer.

## 9. Context dependence

Function is represented conditionally:

\[
\Phi(e\mid K).
\]

Here (K) is not the complete microstate. It is the smallest defensible set of environmental, interaction, organizational, and experimental facts on which the claimed causal contribution depends. It should normally include:

- relevant interaction partners or resource regime;
- the containing system or ecological relation being analyzed;
- environmental conditions required for the effect; and
- any experimenter-supplied task or interface that defines the capacity.

Context is too broad if it omits a condition whose change alters the role. It is too narrow if it includes token identity, irrelevant microstate, or the candidate's unique history such that every event becomes incomparable. The choice must be justified by system dynamics, experiment design, or established domain theory and frozen before the pairwise judgment.

The same mechanism may therefore be neutral in (K_1) and functional in (K_2), or realize different roles in each. Context dependence is a feature to expose, not eliminate.

## 10. Functional-role signature

The provisional signature was:

\[
\Phi(e)=(K,B,P,E,Y).
\]

After critique, (K) is moved to the comparison contract because two events cannot be compared until the relevant context is declared. Explanatory question (Q) and resolution (g) are likewise contract metadata. The retained role signature is:

\[
\boxed{\Phi_D(e)=\bigl(B,\;P\xrightarrow{E}Y\bigr),\qquad D=(K,Q,g).}
\]

| Element | Retained meaning | Why it is needed |
|---|---|---|
| (B) | Bearer/actor that performs the causal contribution | Separates actor from affected partner or beneficiary and locates the role |
| (P) | Process, object, or interaction relation acted upon | Distinguishes, for example, modifying spatial relation from modifying opponent viability |
| (E) | Causally relevant effect on (P) | Distinguishes blocking, copying, separating, repairing, or transforming |
| (Y) | System capacity or relation supported by that effect | Prevents every incidental effect from becoming a function and supplies the Cummins-style explanatory target |

Beneficiary is not a mandatory role field. It belongs primarily to A, where cooperative, parasitic, or collective cases may separate actor and recipient of advantage. When recipient identity is constitutive of the interaction itself, it is represented inside (P) and (E), not duplicated as a universal beneficiary slot.

Implementation (M), activity \(\alpha\), and adaptive consequence remain recorded separately. The signature is a local comparison aid, not an ontology of all biological functions.

## 11. Functional resolution

Functional identity is resolution-relative. The comparison must record a named, nonnumeric (g), for example:

- molecular interaction relation;
- organismal behavioral capability;
- collective organization;
- experiment-defined task transformation; or
- system-level capacity.

At a coarse “threat response” resolution, fight and flee may be grouped. At a behavioral causal-role resolution, they differ. Neither choice is valid merely because it changes the desired event count.

An admissible resolution must be justified before classification by at least one of:

- actual causal organization;
- experimental design;
- system execution semantics;
- ecological interaction structure;
- established domain theory; or
- an explicit scientific explanatory question tied to those facts.

“Everything increasing reproduction” is inadmissibly coarse because it erases the intermediate causal organization under study. “Every opcode trace” is inadmissibly fine because it substitutes implementation identity for function. There is no universal privileged resolution between these extremes.

## 12. Pairwise versus taxonomic classification

The minimal object remains:

\[
C_D(e_i,e_j)\in\{\mathrm{SAME},\mathrm{DISTINCT},\mathrm{UNRESOLVED}\},
\]

not a global class label (C(e)). Pairwise comparison:

- makes the evidence and contrast explicit;
- admits unforeseen functions without extending a closed taxonomy;
- permits unresolved edges;
- avoids cross-system commensuration; and
- restricts AFN recurrence to the earlier repertoire in one history.

The operational rule is:

### SAME

Use `SAME` when, under frozen (D), the evidence supports that implementation/activity differences preserve the same relevant (B,P,E,Y) causal-contribution relation. Literal interchangeability is not required, but role-relevant counterfactuals should remain invariant within the declared context.

### DISTINCT

Use `DISTINCT` when, under frozen (D), a domain-grounded causal contrast changes at least one of (P,E,Y) in a way relevant to (Q), and the difference is not exhausted by implementation, low-level activity, common selective challenge, or ultimate adaptive outcome.

### UNRESOLVED

Use `UNRESOLVED` when evidence cannot reconstruct the relevant role, when competing role descriptions are both admissible and not adjudicated, or when the apparent difference cannot be located above implementation level. It is not a midpoint or weak positive.

## 13. Transitivity / hierarchy

Three levels must be separated:

1. **Semantic identity under one contract.** If events have been mapped to the same normalized causal-role signature under the same (K,Q,g), `SAME` can be treated as an equivalence relation, including transitivity.
2. **Evidence judgments.** Pairwise evidence can be incomplete or inconsistent. `SAME(A,B)` and `SAME(B,C)` do not license automatic closure to A–C unless all three share the same contract and the evidence supports the same role mapping.
3. **Different contracts.** No transitivity is licensed across changed contexts, explanatory questions, or resolutions.

Thus the reported object remains a partial evidence graph. It need not be forced into a global partition.

A hierarchy can display multi-resolution results—for example, defense containing discrimination, avoidance, and interference—but it is optional. It does not remove the need to justify the level at which AFN is being asked and can merely relocate an arbitrary boundary. M3 therefore does **not** require a hierarchical extension. Where multiple admissible resolutions matter, report separate (C_{D_1},C_{D_2}) judgments and their robustness status.

## 14. Activity → causal role → adaptive consequence

The three-level decomposition survives prior-art testing:

\[
\boxed{
\text{activity}
\rightarrow
\text{causal role}
\rightarrow
\text{adaptive consequence}.
}
\]

| Level | Question | Evidence examples | AFN location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Activity | What physically, computationally, or behaviorally occurs? | Execution trace, motion, reaction, signal, computation | Reconstruction input; not class identity by itself |
| Causal role | How does that activity contribute to a declared capacity/relation under (K,Q,g)? | Mechanistic decomposition, interaction semantics, behavior analysis | F and C |
| Adaptive consequence | Does possessing that role alter evolutionary success or establishment? | Lineage comparison, sweep, persistence with causal linkage, intervention | A |

Wouters' distinctions and Cummins' analytical relativity support the separation; selected-effect theory occupies a different, historical explanatory role. The decomposition is methodological pluralism, not a claim that causal role is the only legitimate meaning of biological function.

## 15. Intervention / counterfactual contribution

Intervention logic is a strengthening probe, not a universal requirement. Ask whether replacing, disabling, or varying an implementation while holding the relevant background fixed preserves the declared contribution:

\[
do(M_i\leftarrow M_j)
\quad\text{preserves}\quad
P\xrightarrow{E}Y.
\]

If yes across the admissible comparison, different mechanisms support `SAME`. If interventions reveal different pathways or effects relevant to (Q), they support `DISTINCT`.

Literal substitution may be impossible, destructive, or undefined. Historical substitutes include loss/re-emergence, sister-lineage comparison, independent convergence, execution semantics, ablation, or known mechanistic dependence. Absence of an intervention does not force `UNRESOLVED` if other causal evidence is adequate.

Causal abstraction can formalize invariance once variables and interventions are chosen, but it does not select the scientifically meaningful (K,Q,g,P,E,Y). This preserves Q016's result: formal causal abstraction cannot choose the biological abstraction by itself.

## 16. Exaptation

Exaptation is a decisive counterexample to implementation-based identity. A structure or program can remain substantially unchanged while entering a different context or contribution relation:

\[
M_{\mathrm{old}}\approx M_{\mathrm{new}},
\qquad
\Phi_{D_1}(e_{\mathrm{old}})\neq\Phi_{D_2}(e_{\mathrm{new}}).
\]

The protocol permits `DISTINCT` when the new (P\xrightarrow{E}Y) role is historically new and independently adaptive, even without a new mechanism. It dates functional origin separately from structural origin, preserving the repository's Q005/Q015 boundary.

## 17. Multiple mechanisms / same role

Multiple realization supplies the converse case:

\[
M_i\neq M_j,
\qquad
\Phi_D(e_i)=\Phi_D(e_j).
\]

When the relevant causal contribution is invariant, the events are `SAME` class despite molecular, program, neural, or behavioral differences. The Stringmol defense calibration illustrates the rule without empirical re-analysis: additional checks and changed execution sequences remain partner discrimination when they continue to condition copying on partner state to deny exploitative copying.

## 18. Multifunctionality and pleiotropy

One event can support several role claims:

\[
\Phi_D(e)=\{\phi_1,\phi_2,\ldots\}.
\]

The method must distinguish:

- **historical event count:** the innovation transition is one event node;
- **role additions:** one node may add zero, one, or several class-distinct role claims; and
- **AFN validation:** each claimed new role needs F/C evidence, while the event-role association needs N/H/A support.

A pleiotropic side effect is not AFN merely because it differs causally. At least one new role must itself be connected to the event's adaptive establishment. Multiple role claims do not multiply the historical event count. They can be recorded as several role edges attached to one node, preventing both erasure and double counting.

## 19. Loss, regain, and role substitution

Loss is not AFN. It can strengthen a prior role attribution when loss changes the predicted system capacity or adaptive outcome. Re-emergence is:

- `SAME` when the same role returns under the frozen contract;
- an independent re-origin when a new implementation recreates the role; or
- `DISTINCT` only if a different causal contribution is established.

Role substitution is more difficult. If (Y) remains stable while the new realization changes only (M) or low-level activity, classify `SAME`. If the substitute preserves (Y) through a different (P\xrightarrow{E}Y) causal route relevant at the declared resolution, it can be `DISTINCT` at that role resolution while `SAME` at a coarser capacity resolution. The correct report is then `RESOLUTION-SENSITIVE`, not one context-free answer.

## 20. Within-history restriction

AFN recurrence primarily compares a candidate with the previously represented roles in its own continuous history:

\[
C_D(e_k,e_j),\qquad j<k.
\]

This restriction is legitimate because N is historical, the causal context is inherited from or contrasted with the local trajectory, and recurrence concerns sequential expansion rather than platform capability. It removes most pressure for a universal cross-system ontology.

Cross-run and cross-system role comparisons may be scientifically useful for convergence or methodology calibration, but they do not determine within-history count. M3 does not ask whether Stringmol discrimination and a Geb behavior share a global class.

## 21. Functional-repertoire representation

A simple monotonic set \(\mathcal F_t\) is inadequate because roles can be lost, regained, multiply realized, context dependent, or unresolved. A mandatory hierarchy is also excessive.

The weakest adequate representation is the existing pairwise event graph augmented by time-indexed role claims:

- event nodes carry one or more \(\Phi_D\) claims;
- edges carry `SAME`, `DISTINCT`, or `UNRESOLVED` and their contract (D);
- role claims can be `ACTIVE`, `LOST`, `REGAINED`, or `UNRESOLVED` at time (t); and
- disconnected histories remain disconnected.

A set view \(\mathcal F_t\) can be derived for one fixed contract when needed. Its size is not an OEE score and need not be monotonic.

## 22. Observer-dependence controls

Complete observer independence is neither available nor required. Uncontrolled post hoc flexibility is unacceptable. Before judging a candidate pair, record and freeze:

1. **context (K):** the causally relevant background;
2. **explanatory question (Q):** why this system capacity or interaction is being decomposed;
3. **resolution (g):** which causal distinctions count at that scale;
4. **admissibility basis:** system semantics, experiment design, causal organization, ecological structure, or domain theory;
5. **role signatures:** (B,P,E,Y) for both events;
6. **causal evidence and provenance;** and
7. **predeclared alternative admissible resolutions** for robustness analysis.

For retrospective data, prospective preregistration is impossible. A credible substitute is to freeze the comparison contract after the corpus and scientific question are declared but before assigning `SAME/DISTINCT/UNRESOLVED`, publish the contract with the judgment, and retain alternatives rather than tuning granularity to recover a desired count.

Semantic labels such as “parasite,” “defense,” “cooperation,” “fight,” or “flee” are never sufficient evidence. Each must reduce to a role signature and causal provenance.

## 23. Robustness across admissible resolutions

Robustness annotations add value without pretending that resolution is numeric:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ROBUST SAME` | `SAME` across the predeclared scientifically admissible resolutions |
| `ROBUST DISTINCT` | `DISTINCT` across those resolutions |
| `RESOLUTION-SENSITIVE` | Classification changes across admissible resolutions; report each contract-specific result |
| `UNRESOLVED` | Evidence is insufficient even after a resolution is declared |

These annotations do not replace the class edge. They disclose sensitivity to defensible explanatory choices. “Reasonable” is constrained by the admissibility sources in §11, not by analyst preference after seeing the events. No percentage, distance, or robustness score is introduced.

## 24. Pairwise comparison record

The proposed record in the prompt is useful but can be shortened. Activity and implementation belong in evidence rows; adaptive consequence must be referenced but not used to determine C. The final minimal template is:

```text
Event A:
Event B:

Comparison contract
  Context K:
  Explanatory question / target Q:
  Functional resolution g:
  Admissibility basis:

Role A
  Bearer/actor B:
  Target process/relation P:
  Causal effect E:
  Contributed capacity/relation Y:

Role B
  Bearer/actor B:
  Target process/relation P:
  Causal effect E:
  Contributed capacity/relation Y:

Implementation/activity evidence:
Functional-role counterfactual or mechanistic evidence:
Adaptive-evidence reference and beneficiary (separate; not a C criterion):
Same selective challenge?: YES / NO / PARTIAL / N/A (metadata only)

Same causal role?: YES / NO / UNRESOLVED
Classification: SAME / DISTINCT / UNRESOLVED
Robustness: ROBUST SAME / ROBUST DISTINCT / RESOLUTION-SENSITIVE / UNRESOLVED
Evidence / provenance:
```

Every field has a distinct purpose. Removing (Q/g) restores granularity drift; removing (P/E/Y) collapses role to a label; removing provenance makes the judgment unauditable; omitting the adaptation reference risks silent reuse of fitness evidence as functional identity.

## 25. Counterexamples A–J

| Case | Required classification behavior | Result under pairwise rule |
|---|---|---|
| **A — same mechanism, new function** | Exaptation must permit `DISTINCT` | Context or (P/E/Y) changes while (M) remains; **DISTINCT** if role-relevant and supported |
| **B — different mechanism, same function** | Multiple realization must permit `SAME` | (M/\alpha) differ while \(\Phi_D\) is preserved; **SAME** |
| **C — same final fitness effect, different role** | Must permit `DISTINCT` | Fitness is A metadata; different (P/E/Y) gives **DISTINCT** |
| **D — same selective challenge, different behavior** | Fight/flee must be separable | Threat is shared context; damage versus separation effects give **DISTINCT** at behavioral-role resolution |
| **E — different low-level activity, same contribution** | Should often be `SAME` | Low-level activity differs but the typed contribution is invariant; **SAME** |
| **F — same activity, different context** | May be `DISTINCT` | Different (K) can change what the activity contributes to; contract-specific **DISTINCT** or `RESOLUTION-SENSITIVE` |
| **G — one event, several roles** | Preserve multifunctionality without double count | One event node, multiple role claims; validate each, count the historical event once |
| **H — role hierarchy** | Coarse same/fine distinct must be visible | Separate contract-specific edges; `RESOLUTION-SENSITIVE`; no mandatory hierarchy |
| **I — externally enumerated task** | Enumeration must not decide C automatically | Record design context; compare (P/E/Y). It may be `SAME`, `DISTINCT`, or resolution-sensitive |
| **J — distinct new interaction, A unresolved** | C may be clear while AFN fails | Record `DISTINCT` role edge; event remains unsupported because A fails |

All ten cases are handled without a system-specific exception. The rule does not guarantee that evidence will resolve every case.

## 26. Strongest prior-art absorption test

The proposed formalization is absorbed as:

\[
\boxed{
\text{AFN functional-class identity}
=
\text{causal-role function}
+\text{explicit context}
+\text{domain-specific explanatory resolution}
+\text{independent adaptation evidence}.
}
\]

Cummins supplies contribution relative to an analytical account of system capacity; Wouters and Godfrey-Smith supply pluralism and separation from advantage/selected effect; Amundson and Lauder show that causal-role analysis is legitimate in evolutionary research; Love supplies the relational, organizational, multi-level comparison lesson; interventionist reasoning supplies optional invariance probes; Brigandt and Love caution that novelty concepts can organize an explanatory problem space without delivering one universally decisive definition. [Brigandt & Love 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/jez.b.22461)

Nothing irreducibly new remains at the level of function theory. M3 is a methodological integration and constraint record. It does not establish priority for the notation, a universal formula, or a new natural kind.

## 27. Strongest positive interpretation

M3 repairs the practical M1 limitation sufficiently for continued retrospective work. An analyst cannot justify a class edge merely by naming behaviors, choosing a shared fitness outcome, pointing to mechanism difference, or changing granularity silently. The analyst must state a domain-grounded comparison contract, decompose both roles into the same causal fields, keep A separate, preserve unresolved cases, and disclose sensitivity across admissible resolutions.

This is enough for a reproducible comparison protocol in principle. It deliberately stops short of an algorithm or universal ontology.

## 28. Strongest skeptical interpretation

Cummins-style functions remain relative to analytical interests, and no formal tuple determines which system capacity is explanatorily important. (P,E,Y) descriptions can themselves be broadened or narrowed. “Admissible resolution” is judged by domain experts, intervention substitutes are often imperfect, and a robustness audit can only range over alternatives someone thought to include. The signature may discipline prose without forcing convergence between analysts.

This does not make the method useless. It means the scientific product is an auditable expert judgment whose reliability remains empirical. The next unresolved issue is no longer the absence of a comparison rule but whether independent coders using it reach stable conclusions.

## 29. Central decision

\[
\boxed{\text{B — PAIRWISE CAUSAL-ROLE INTEGRATION SUFFICIENT}}
\]

Why not A: established theories supply the ingredients, but M1 showed that an explicit comparison contract and robustness record add a meaningful AFN-specific constraint.

Why not C: hierarchy is useful as an optional view but does not remove granularity choice and is unnecessary for the ten counterexamples.

Why not D: the local rule is adequate even though it is not algorithmic.

Why not E: structured judgment remains necessary, but causal-role signatures, frozen contracts, and robustness categories provide more constraint than provenance-backed prose alone.

Why not F: within-history, context- and resolution-indexed comparisons remain scientifically stable enough to support explicit positive, negative, and unresolved judgments.

## 32. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### Known / established

- Biological “function” serves several explanatory roles; activity, causal role, advantage, and selected effect should not be conflated.
- Causal-role function is relative to an analytical account of a system capacity and is legitimate in historical evolutionary analysis.
- Functional identity need not track implementation, activity, selective challenge, or ultimate fitness outcome.
- Organizational function offers strong grounding only where differentiated closure/self-maintenance is established; it is not the general ALife criterion.
- Within-history comparison removes the need for a universal cross-system function ontology.

### Potentially novel
