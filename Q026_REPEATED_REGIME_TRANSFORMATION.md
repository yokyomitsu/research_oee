# Q026 — Repeated evolutionary-regime transformation

Date: 2026-09-08  
Pass type: exactly one theoretical literature pass; no implementation or simulation  
Epistemic convention: **Fact** reports a source or established result; **Interpretation** is the conclusion of this pass; **Hypothesis** and **Speculation** are used only where explicitly marked.

## 1. Research question

> What, if anything, allows products of one evolved regime to create several later hereditary, reproductive, or selectable regimes without relying on a predefined hierarchy or a one-off historical prerequisite?

Equivalent probe:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does repeated evolutionary-regime transformation require a distinct general recurrence mechanism,}
\text{ or can contingent, transition-specific mechanisms explain the sequence?}
}
\]

The second question is not whether one transition makes another transition more likely. It is whether anything causally distinct remains after each transition and its historical setting have been explained locally.

## 2. Boundaries inherited from Q007 / Q012

**Fact — Q007 boundary.** Infinite meta-level regress is unnecessary. Fixed microscopic rules can support changing effective evolutionary mechanisms. Self-reference does not by itself prevent closure, and coevolution or environmental openness alone does not explain OEE. A recurrence account therefore cannot consist merely of rules changing rules, self-reference, or an open environment. [Q007](Q007_META_EVOLUTION_CLOSURE.md)

**Fact — Q012 boundary.**

\[
\boxed{
\text{one completed transition}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{generally increased propensity for another}
}
\]

Transition-enabling and transition-suppressing consequences are historically and target specific. Losses and reversions defeat a monotonic transition ladder, and no universal scalar transition propensity has been established. This pass does not reopen that question. [Q012](Q012_TRANSITION_PROPENSITY.md)

**Interpretation.** The admissible explanandum is consequently narrower than “why transitions happen” and cannot be a scalar tendency that rises after every transition. It must be either a manipulable process that renews transition conditions across heterogeneous transitions, or no additional mechanism at all.

## 3. Evolutionary regime

**Interpretation — minimal operational use.** An *evolutionary regime* is the effective organization, in a declared domain and interval, of some combination of:

- reproduction;
- heredity;
- generation of variation;
- selection;
- individuality; and
- ecological coupling.

A regime transformation is a scientifically consequential change in one or more of these relations, not every phenotypic novelty or ecological expansion. Regime identity remains claim-specific: reproduction theory can identify a life-cycle change, inheritance theory an information-transmission change, and individuality theory a change in the bearer of fitness. No universal equivalence relation, distance, or scalar ordering over regimes is assumed. This preserves Q016's result that identity-bearing relations are domain-specific. [Q016](Q016_CAUSAL_REWIRING_VS_TYPE_CHANGE.md)

## 4. Major-transition prior art

**Fact.** Maynard Smith and Szathmáry organized major transitions partly around changes in how hereditary information is stored, transmitted, and interpreted. Their list includes both hierarchical changes and non-hierarchical transformations such as RNA-to-DNA/protein organization, sex, and language. Later theory separates transitions in individuality from innovations in inheritance and information transmission. [Maynard Smith & Szathmáry 1995](https://academic.oup.com/book/40636), [Jablonka & Lamb 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2005.08.038), [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112)

**Fact.** Evolutionary-transitions-in-individuality theory explains a strong subset of transitions through lower-level cooperation followed by integration into a new individual. Multilevel selection, ecological benefit to cooperation, mechanisms aligning lower-level interests, division of labour, communication, bottlenecks, conflict mediation, and mutual dependence explain formation and stabilization within this class. [West et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421402112), [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112)

**Fact.** Life-cycle theory sharpens the emergence of a new individual as formation of a new embedding life cycle and supplies local criteria for when a stage-structured collective can be treated as a fitness-bearing unit. Ecological scaffolding explains how an external population structure can initiate higher-level selection. These are causal accounts of particular transitions. [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162), [Black, Bourrat & Rainey 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-019-1086-9)

**Interpretation.** Major-transition theory is strongest when it explains why a specified transition can start and persist, or identifies recurring constraints on a specified class such as ETIs. It does not contain a demonstrated law by which completion of one transition regenerates the causes of a later, qualitatively different transition. “Cooperation plus conflict mediation” is not an account of the origin of sex, the genetic code, or symbolic cultural inheritance. Conversely, this absence does not show that a general recurrence mechanism is required: the theory may correctly be a family of local explanations.

**Answer to the firewall question.** Major-transition theory explains how important transition types occur and identifies some common conditions within transition classes. The historical plurality of transitions is evidence for repeated transformation, not by itself a recurrence mechanism.

## 5. Ongoing major transitions and OEE

**Fact.** The York OEE synthesis distinguishes one major transition from *ongoing major transitions*: one transition is insufficient for OEE, whereas continuing emergence of new evolutionary entities or levels would be an especially strong hallmark. It separately states that hallmarks should guide the search for candidate mechanisms and that different hallmarks may have heterogeneous mechanisms. [Taylor et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1162/ARTL_A_00210)

**Interpretation.** “Ongoing major transitions” is a **behavioral hallmark**—a description of the desired historical pattern. It is not a causal explanation of its own recurrence. Treating the phrase as a mechanism would make the explanans restate the explanandum.

## 6. Evolved open-endedness

**Fact.** Pattee and Sayama reverse the usual explanatory direction: rather than treating open-endedness as an initial prerequisite, they argue that degrees and conditions of open-endedness can themselves arise through evolution. They explicitly connect this view to evolution of evolvability, multilevel selection, major transitions, information processing, symbolic systems, sensing, and social organization, while proposing taxonomies and research questions about benefits, costs, and selection for or against open-endedness. They describe this as an evolved-open-endedness *view*, not as a single new dynamical law. [Pattee & Sayama 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00276)

**Interpretation.** EOE contributes three things:

1. a historical framework in which enabling conditions need not be present at the origin;
2. classes of enabling innovations whose detailed mechanisms come from existing theory; and
3. an explanatory perspective that makes changes in open-endedness legitimate evolutionary outcomes.

It does not specify a general variable \(M\) whose removal selectively abolishes repeated regime transformation. Nor does a bounded-to-more-open-ended transition imply a later succession of changes in reproduction, heredity, selection, or individuality. EOE therefore cannot be counted as the missing recurrence mechanism.

## 7. Cultural evolved open-endedness

**Fact.** Borg and colleagues propose human cultural evolution as a second candidate history of evolved open-endedness and distinguish bounded from effectively unbounded cumulative culture. Their account identifies a package including high-fidelity social learning, teaching, language, cooperation, institutions, recombination, and externally supported information. These processes change the scale, fidelity, persistence, and combinatorial reach of cultural transmission. [Borg et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00406), [Tennie, Call & Tomasello 2009](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2009.0052)

**Fact.** A subsequent macro-level cultural model represents cultural systems and search spaces as coevolving under stochastic and selective processes. It produces open-ended-looking cultural growth under conditional parameter regimes, including continued perturbation and resource production, without requiring a succession of new cultural evolutionary individuals or inheritance regimes. This is literature evidence, not a simulation performed in this pass. [Winters & Charbonneau 2026](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2025.0255)

**Interpretation — destructive stress test.** Cultural open-endedness can presently be explained as a contingent conjunction of known transmission, recombination, coordination, storage, and ecological/resource innovations. Some of these can count as a historical regime transition—for example, externalized symbolic inheritance—but the case does not establish that later cultural novelty requires repeated changes of the cultural evolutionary regime. It therefore supports, rather than defeats, the contingent-sequence null.

## 8. Initiation versus endogenization

**Fact.** Ecological scaffolding can initiate an ETI by imposing population structure that makes collective-level selection effective. Evolutionary hysteresis/endogenization can then retain a collective phenotype after that scaffold is removed when the scaffolded trajectory crosses into the basin of a stable state in the unscaffolded environment. Formal conditions include a barrier in the unscaffolded landscape, its removal under scaffolding, and arrival in the alternative basin. [Black, Bourrat & Rainey 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-019-1086-9), [Doulcier et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50625-1)

**Fact.** Ratcheting mutations can stabilize multicellularity by making unicellular revertants less fit or making reversion less mutationally accessible. This explains resistance to reversal of one transition. [Libby et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2015.0444)

**Interpretation.** Initiation and stabilization solve different problems. Neither result implies that stabilized \(R_{i+1}\) supplies conditions for \(R_{i+2}\). Hysteresis and ratcheting may expose new organizational opportunities as a contingent side effect, but their established causal role is to enter and retain a basin. In a bounded system they can instead reduce transformability by removing routes back or by fixing dependencies.

\[
\boxed{\text{irreversibility}\neq\text{open-endedness}}
\]

## 9. Transition-specific versus recurrence mechanism

**Interpretation.** A transition-specific mechanism has the form

\[
R_i\xrightarrow{M_i}R_{i+1},
\]

where \(M_i\) may be scaffolding, conflict mediation, symbiosis, a bottleneck, an inheritance innovation, or another local cause. Nothing requires \(M_i=M_{i+1}\).

A distinct recurrence mechanism would add causal content to the sequence

\[
R_0\rightarrow R_1\rightarrow R_2\rightarrow\cdots
\]

by regenerating or maintaining conditions under which later, not-predefined transition-specific mechanisms can operate. Provisionally call the transition-enabling conditions at stage \(i\), without imposing a common ontology or cardinality, \(\mathcal C_i\). The missing-mechanism claim would require more than observing that different \(M_i\) happened: it would require a defined process \(M\) such that suppressing \(M\) would reduce recurrent regime transformations while leaving ordinary within-regime evolution substantially intact.

\[
\operatorname{do}(M=0)
\Rightarrow
\text{fewer or no recurrent regime transformations, with within-regime evolution largely retained.}
\]

**Interpretation.** No reviewed candidate meets this separation. “Generation of \(\mathcal C_{i+1}\)” merely renames recurrence unless the conditions, generator, scope, and counterfactual are independently specified.

**Interpretation — transformation of transformation mechanisms.** Fixed microdynamics can make an embodied transition-enabling mechanism—an inheritance channel, life cycle, developmental map, or conflict-control system—an object of ordinary variation and selection. This avoids an explicit meta-rule regress, as Q007 requires. When the claim is only that variation-generating or inheritance machinery can evolve, however, it is already evolution of evolvability or major-transition theory. Evolvability of \(M_i\) does not imply production of a qualitatively different \(M_{i+1}\), so it supplies no recurrence result by itself.

## 10. Contingent-sequence null model

**Null explanation.** Earth's observed chain can be a historically contingent sequence of distinct local mechanisms. Large physical and ecological opportunity, changing environments, many interacting lineages, accumulated innovations, and occasional transition-specific mechanisms jointly produce a long finite history. This null does not claim that state-space size alone causes accessibility, nor that every transition has already been completely reconstructed.

**Attack.** The null would fail if the same manipulable causal process were necessary across otherwise heterogeneous transitions, or if local explanations left a stable residual: after a transition consumes or stabilizes its prerequisites, some process must restore a non-predefined class of transition conditions. The literature reviewed here instead supplies class-specific commonalities, historical prerequisites, and external as well as endogenous opportunity. No evidence makes a shared restorer necessary.

**Interpretation.** After accepting local mechanisms and historical contingency, the residual question “why could another transition happen?” is answered case by case by the causes of that transition. Demanding a further cause common to the entire sequence is a category error unless recurrence is independently operationalized. The null therefore survives.

## 11. Endogenous versus exogenous opportunity renewal

**Fact.** Later opportunities can arise exogenously through geological or climatic change, invasion, encounter with a symbiont, or other environmental history. They can also arise endogenously through niche construction, ecosystem engineering, coevolution, altered interactions, or accumulated innovations. Q022 established that these endogenous ecological processes are already covered by ecological theory. [Q022](Q022_ECOLOGICAL_SELECTION_STRUCTURE.md)

**Interpretation.** OEE does not logically require every transition to generate the cause of the next. A process may remain non-absorbed through continued external or historical contingency. If “open-ended” is defined to require endogenous opportunity renewal, that is an additional design criterion, not an inference from natural history.

Nor does ordinary niche construction become a recurrence mechanism merely because it sometimes supplies a later prerequisite. To exceed F5 it would need a cross-transition causal claim showing that evolutionary activity recurrently reconstructs transition conditions, rather than locally altering selection. No such general relation survived review.

## 12. Key innovations / inheritance changes

**Fact.** Key innovations can relax constraints or open adaptive zones. Pharyngeal-jaw innovations, for example, have evolved repeatedly and are associated with major radiations, yet the relevant outcomes are feeding versatility and ecological diversification rather than changes in reproduction, heredity, selection level, or evolutionary individuality. [Wainwright et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/sys060)

**Interpretation.** Repeated key innovations explain repeated access to new phenotypic and ecological opportunities. They do not explain repeated regime transformation unless each innovation demonstrably changes the effective evolutionary relations in section 3. Inflating every adaptive-zone expansion into a regime change would erase the question.

**Fact.** Major-transition classifications include repeated changes in information transmission: genetic coding, chromosomes, sex, multicellular bottlenecks, epigenetic systems, nervous-system-mediated learning, and linguistic/cultural inheritance. [Maynard Smith & Szathmáry 1995](https://academic.oup.com/book/40636), [Jablonka & Lamb 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2005.08.038), [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112)

**Interpretation.** Successive inheritance innovations are genuine non-hierarchical regime transformations and can become prerequisites for later events. Their historical succession, however, supplies no rule that generates the next inheritance system. One new inheritance mechanism is neither recurrence nor OEE.

## 13. Dynamical hierarchy / transformational novelty

**Fact.** Dynamical-hierarchy work demonstrates that one low-level physicochemical framework can successively generate robust higher-level structures. The OEE literature explicitly notes that the canonical demonstration is self-organization rather than adaptive evolution: reproduction, heritable variation, and selection of the emergent wholes would still be required. [Rasmussen et al. 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11911785/), [Taylor et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1162/ARTL_A_00210)

**Interpretation.** Dynamically generated units avoid a fully explicit biological-unit template, but hierarchy generation is insufficient. A finite stack of self-organized levels does not establish hereditary/adaptive exploitation, indefinite scalability, or recurrence of qualitatively different regimes.

**Fact.** Taylor's transformational novelty requires revision of the observer's phenotype meta-model. Proposed routes include multiple behavioral domains, transdomain bridges, and non-additive compositional systems. A transdomain bridge exploits different properties of one component in different domains; its later use can be an exaptation. The domain partition remains observer-relative, and the proposed routes are a high-level framework rather than a recurrence theorem. [Taylor 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00290)

**Interpretation.** Ongoing transformational novelty is not equivalent to F2. A lineage can repeatedly require new phenotype descriptions while retaining its reproductive and hereditary regime; conversely, sex or a new inheritance channel can change the evolutionary regime without guaranteeing ongoing transformational phenotype novelty. Transdomain bridges and non-additive composition may cause particular innovations, but the reviewed work does not show that bridge formation regenerates new domains or later regime transitions.

**Fact.** Evolutionary “bootstrapping” is existing terminology. Conrad's origin-of-life model describes stepwise evolution of structures that improve effective evolutionary search, and later work on autocatalytic organization and symbolic information likewise uses self-facilitating trajectories. [Conrad 1982](https://doi.org/10.1016/0303-2647(82)90006-5)

**Interpretation.** Bootstrapping can name a scoped mechanism when a specific feedback is formalized. As the generic claim that one organization makes another possible, it only describes a trajectory and overlaps evolution of evolvability, major transitions, key innovations, and the adjacent possible. It adds no independent recurrence mechanism here.

## 14. F2 versus F3

**Interpretation.** F2 and F3 are semantically distinct:

- F3 asked whether an evolutionary organization retains broad resource-feasible access to later adaptations.
- F2 asks whether reproduction, heredity, variation, selection, individuality, or ecological coupling itself changes repeatedly.

Nevertheless, a formal probability that \(R_i\) can reach any member of a declared class of later regimes is an accessibility claim. Without an additional causal generator, “recurrence capacity” becomes target-relative accessibility to rare transitions. Q024's no-free-lunch boundary then applies: broad recurrence can be supported only relative to persistent physical regularities, composability, ecological continuity, or another declared relation among transitions. [Q024](Q024_BROAD_SCOPE_EVOLVABILITY.md)

**Decision on collapse.** F2 does not collapse *definitionally* into ordinary adaptive accessibility, because its targets are changes of evolutionary process. But its proposed general mechanism collapses into conditional accessibility unless a causally separable renewer of transition conditions is supplied. No such renewer was found. F3 remains closed.

## 15. Finite sequence versus recurrence capacity

**Fact / deduction.** A finite history

\[
R_0\rightarrow R_1\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow R_n
\]

does not establish capacity for indefinite continuation. It is compatible with a long transient ending in a permanently stable regime, and finite observation cannot in general distinguish permanent non-accessibility from a waiting time longer than observation. [Q003](Q003_CAPACITY_AND_OBSERVER.md), [Q009](Q009_ACCESSIBILITY_SCALING.md)

No constant transition rate is required: a conditionally continuing process may remain within one regime for long intervals. The relevant formal probe is only whether a regime is permanently absorbing relative to a declared class of later transformations.

**Interpretation.** One may define a conditional non-absorption property relative to a declared transition class and dynamics:

\[
R_t\notin\mathcal A_{\mathrm{permanent}}.
\]

But this is a formal property, not a mechanism; “permanent” is not empirically certified by prolonged stasis, and the accessible transition class inherits Q002/Q008's model and resource dependence. Adams and colleagues formalize unboundedness relative to recurrence of an isolated dynamical subsystem, not recurrent changes of a Darwinian evolutionary regime. No reviewed OEE source supplied the proposed permanent-absorbing-regime criterion as an established recurrence account. The vocabulary therefore does not improve the causal account enough to adopt it. [Adams et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-00810-8)

**Unknown.** Earth's finite transition record underdetermines whether it is a long contingent chain or evidence of a conditionally continuing capacity. The negative result of this pass is not proof of eventual natural closure.

## 16. Strong counterexamples

These cases attack implications, not all are claims that a natural lineage is literally and permanently closed.

### A — A major transition followed by closure

**Constructive counterexample.** An ecologically scaffolded system with a finite trait space can cross into and become endogenized in a collective basin, then remain at a stable endpoint because no further organization is represented. One genuine transition therefore does not entail recurrence. [Doulcier et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50625-1)

### B — Exogenously renewed contingent transitions

**Logical counterexample.** A sequence in which independent climatic changes, invasions, symbiont encounters, and local innovations trigger different \(M_i\) is fully coherent. Each event has a cause, but there is no common recurrence mechanism. Historical transition count alone cannot reject this null.

### C — Many key innovations without a regime change

**Empirical scope counterexample.** Repeated feeding innovations and adaptive radiations in fishes can create ecological diversity while leaving organismal reproduction, genetic heredity, and individuality intact. Key-innovation sequences therefore do not imply regime sequences. [Wainwright et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/sys060)

### D — Changing heredity/reproduction followed by stabilization

**Constructive counterexample.** Ratcheting can make a newly multicellular state resistant to reversion and stable within its modeled space. That is a changed reproductive organization with no modeled implication of another transition. [Libby et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2015.0444)

### E — Repeated nesting from a predefined hierarchy

**Established counterexample.** DISHTINY can produce several nested individuality transitions, but its hierarchy levels are substantially pre-enabled by the model. Repeated levels therefore do not establish non-predefined recurrence. [Q012](Q012_TRANSITION_PROPENSITY.md)

### F — Stabilization suppresses later routes

**Mechanistic counterexample.** Endogenization enters a stable basin; ratcheting lowers access to prior organization. Neither guarantees new outgoing paths, so strong persistence can coexist with reduced local transformability. This defeats the inference from stabilization to renewed opportunity.

### G — Cultural open-endedness without repeated cultural-regime change

**Model-based scope counterexample.** Coevolutionary cultural/search-space growth can remain open-ended-looking under one fixed macro-level process. Cumulative recombination and expansion need not repeatedly replace the cultural inheritance regime. [Winters & Charbonneau 2026](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2025.0255)

### H — Transformational novelty without an ETI

**Conceptual counterexample.** Taylor's transdomain bridges and non-additive composition permit phenotype descriptions to acquire new domains or functions without requiring a new level of individuality. Thus transformational novelty is broader than hierarchical major transitions and non-equivalent to F2. [Taylor 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00290)

### I — Fixed substrate, several effective regimes

**Historical/formal scope counterexample.** Fixed low-level physical regularities are compatible with biological histories containing different inheritance, reproduction, and individuality regimes; explicit higher-order rule replacement is unnecessary. Dynamical hierarchy and artificial-chemistry examples reinforce the logical possibility, though they do not establish indefinite evolutionary recurrence. This preserves Q007 without turning fixed microdynamics into an OEE guarantee. [Rasmussen et al. 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11911785/), [Q007](Q007_META_EVOLUTION_CLOSURE.md)

## 17. Prior-art absorption test

Tested decomposition:

\[
F2
\stackrel{?}{=}
\text{major transitions}
+\text{ evolved open-endedness}
+\text{ key innovations}
+\text{ evolution of evolvability}
+\text{ historical contingency}.
\]

**Interpretation.** This sum is an explanatory partition, not a newly discovered mechanism. It covers:

- event initiation and class-specific preconditions;
- stabilization, endogenization, conflict mediation, and ratcheting;
- changes in inheritance and individuality;
- innovations that open adaptive or cultural domains;
- evolved changes to generators of variation; and
- endogenous or exogenous arrival of later opportunities.

The only apparent residue is the desire for one law explaining why the heterogeneous sequence continues. But no independent observable or intervention distinguishes that desire from (a) the later events' local causes, or (b) conditional accessibility to those events. Requiring \(R_i\) itself to generate \(\mathcal C_{i+1}\) is unsupported and excludes legitimate exogenous histories by stipulation.

**Absorption result: PASSES.** No independently missing causal process survives. This does not imply that every transition in Earth's history is fully explained, only that unresolved event histories do not jointly establish a general recurrence mechanism.

## 18. EOE classification

\[
\boxed{\textbf{EXPLANATORY PERSPECTIVE}}
\]

EOE reorients explanation toward the evolution of conditions that make later evolution more open-ended. It organizes known mechanism families and a historical research agenda, but does not itself supply the causal recurrence variable required in section 9. “Historical framework” is also descriptively accurate, but **EXPLANATORY PERSPECTIVE** is the single primary classification because that is the contribution common to Pattee–Sayama and the cultural extension.

## 19. Central decision

\[
\boxed{\textbf{B — NO GENERAL RECURRENCE MECHANISM REQUIRED}}
\]

The demand for one recurrence mechanism is not supported as a distinct causal problem. Recurrent transformation can consist of heterogeneous, contingent transition mechanisms. This is preferred to **A — EXISTING THEORY SUFFICIENT** because the causal history of particular natural transitions remains incomplete; the conclusion is about the absence of an additional *general* explanandum, not completeness of all local explanations.

## 20. Mechanism readiness

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NOT READY}}
\]

F2 does not survive the absorption test as an independent mechanism frontier. A finite historical chain does not establish recurrence capacity, and any general transition probability is conditional accessibility unless a separable process is named. The question can be asked without universal regime identity, but no causal recurrence phenomenon meeting the intervention criterion is currently specified.

## 21. Remaining explanatory gap

**Interpretation.** The narrow residue is case-specific:

> For a declared sequence of regime changes, which products of an earlier regime were causally necessary or enabling for specified later transitions, compared with external events and independently available historical conditions?

This asks for partial causal mappings between particular transitions. It does not presume that the earlier transition generated the next transition's cause, that the mappings share a mechanism, or that several mappings establish indefinite recurrence.

## 22. Problem classification

\[
\boxed{\textbf{VALIDATION}}
\]

The live work is historical and comparative causal validation of particular prerequisite claims. A subsidiary formalization problem remains—finite sequence versus conditional non-absorption—but no distinct mechanism variable is ready for theory-building.

## 23. Autopoiesis / Lenia / deep-learning contribution

- **Autopoiesis — ANALOGY ONLY.** Organizational closure and endogenization clarify how a regime can maintain itself after a transition. They do not explain why another regime transition occurs or recurs at the population level.
- **Lenia — NONE.** Continuous self-organizing substrates may be permissive, but no reviewed Lenia result bears on recurrent hereditary, reproductive, or selectable regime transformations.
- **Deep learning — NONE.** Generalization and meta-learning analogies would reopen F3 without adding a recurrence-specific causal relation.

## 24. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### ESTABLISHED

- Major-transition, ETI, life-cycle, multilevel-selection, conflict-mediation, ecological-scaffolding, endogenization, and ratcheting theories explain important transition-specific events or classes.
- Major transitions include both changes of individuality and non-hierarchical changes in inheritance/information transmission.
- Ongoing major transitions are an OEE hallmark, not by that fact a mechanism.
- EOE treats open-endedness-enabling conditions as evolvable and draws on established mechanism families.
- A completed or stabilized transition does not generally create the cause of another.

### PARTIALLY EXPLORED

- Common prerequisites within transition classes, especially ETIs.
- Historical dependencies among inheritance, information-processing, ecological, and individuality transitions.
- Conditional cultural/search-space coevolution as a route to open-ended growth.

### UNDEREXPLORED

- Explicit causal graphs linking products of named earlier regimes to several later, qualitatively different transitions while distinguishing them from shared background and exogenous events.
- Comparative negative cases in which similar stabilized regimes differ in whether later transformations occur.

These are validation gaps, not evidence for a general recurrence law.

### POTENTIALLY NOVEL

- None justified. “Regeneration of transition-enabling conditions” is presently a relabeling unless an independent generator and intervention are specified.

### UNKNOWN

- Whether Earth's finite history reflects a conditionally continuing capacity or a long finite contingent chain.
- Whether any scoped process will eventually satisfy the recurrence intervention criterion across more than one transition class.

## 25. Hypothesis decision

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NO HYPOTHESIS JUSTIFIED}}
\]

Major transitions occurred repeatedly, open-endedness can evolve, endogenization can stabilize transitions, culture became more open-ended, and opportunities are historically contingent. None of these facts supplies a falsifiable, recurrence-specific dynamical relation beyond established local mechanisms. `HYPOTHESES.md` therefore remains unchanged.

## 26. Highest-information next question

> Which partial causal mappings support claims of accumulated evolutionary change when hereditary or selectable units change, and which comparisons must remain undefined?

This is the next frontier only. It is not investigated in this pass.
