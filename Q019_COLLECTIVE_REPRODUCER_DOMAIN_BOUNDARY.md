# Q019 — Collective-reproducer domain boundary

Theoretical research pass, 2026-09-08. Scope: a domain-boundary audit of the frozen Q017 transfer result. This pass uses published theory and reported data only. It does not modify or rerun either model, search for an OEE mechanism, or perform Q020.

Epistemic convention: descriptions of sources and reported results are **Fact**. Cross-framework judgments and the Q019 decision are **Interpretation**. No new scientific **Hypothesis** or unsupported **Speculation** is introduced.

## 1. Research question

> Are clonal growth–fragmentation and aggregative/propagule multicellular reproduction genuinely different collective-reproducer identity domains, or did Q017 accidentally encode snowflake-specific implementation details as identity conditions?

The target is a domain-specific abstraction for collective reproducers during early multicellularity, not one invariant for all life. The audit asks whether formation mode

\[
F=\text{how a collective forms}
\]

was mistakenly built into reproductive identity

\[
R=\text{how a collective produces or reconstructs further instances of its life cycle}.
\]

## 2. Logical status of Q018

Three statements must remain separate.

1. **Fact — FROZEN Q017 TRANSFER FAILED.** Q017 defined candidate units as clonal assemblies produced by continuing daughter association and required at least two clonal cells in the offspring at separation. Fernandes reports aggregative, genetically heterogeneous clusters and a prominent single-cell propagule. No reported Fernandes case satisfies the unchanged conjunction. This historical result is retained exactly. [Q017](Q017_SNOWFLAKE_COLLECTIVE_REPRODUCER_EQUIVALENCE.md), [Q018](Q018_FROZEN_EQUIVALENCE_TRANSFER.md)
2. **Interpretation — Q017 WAS OVER-SPECIFIED for a general collective-reproducer domain.** Independent life-cycle theory explicitly includes aggregation, syngamy, transformation, and multiplication; it treats metazoan gametes and clonal fragmentation as different realizations of life cycles. Godfrey-Smith likewise treats a one-cell bottleneck as a dimension making collective reproduction more definite, not as the minimum size of an offspring collective. [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162), [Godfrey-Smith 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421378112)
3. **Interpretation — A biological identity-domain split was not established by Q018.** Clonality, daughter association, fragmentation, and propagule size have major evolutionary consequences, but reviewed reproduction theory does not make them necessary for every collective reproductive lineage. A split would require an independently supported difference in parentage, multiplication, closure, or reproductive autonomy, not merely different formation mechanics.

Therefore Q018 established the boundary of the **frozen Q017 model**, not by itself an ontological boundary between all clonal and aggregative collective reproducers:

\[
\boxed{
\text{failure of a frozen equivalence did not establish ontological non-equivalence;}\\
\text{it established failure of that particular equivalence.}
}
\]

This is a qualification of the canonical interpretation, not a retroactive claim that the transfer succeeded.

## 3. Q017 condition audit

The classifications below are relative to the narrower domain **collective life-cycle reproduction in early multicellularity**. A condition can play another role in a theory of Darwinian individuality.

| Q017 condition | Provisional class | Audit |
|---|---|---|
| **clonality** | **Realization-specific** | Neither Doulcier et al.'s life-cycle processes nor Godfrey-Smith's collective reproduction requires clonal development. Clonality raises relatedness and suppresses conflict, so it bears strongly on individuality and evolvability, not on the bare existence of a reproductive cycle. |
| **continuing daughter association** | **Realization-specific** | This is the established “staying together” route to construction. “Coming together” is a distinct formation route with different cooperation problems, not automatically a different reproductive kind. [Tarnita, Taubes & Nowak 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2012.11.022) |
| **fragmentation** | **Realization-specific** | Fragmentation realizes multiplication in snowflake yeast. Gamete production, propagule release, budding, and migration to offspring patches can realize multiplication without physical fracture. |
| **minimum offspring cell number** | **Realization-specific** | A single-cell zygote, spore, or propagule can be a stage in reproduction of a multicellular individual. The life-cycle bearer is not required to be multicellular at every stage. |
| **collective parentage** | **Identity-bearing** | A later instance must arise after, and be causally produced by, one or more earlier instances of the same relevant life-cycle class. Mere recurrence or observer regrouping is insufficient. [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-024-04880-w) |
| **multiplication** | **Identity-bearing** | At least one transition must produce additional candidate individuals/stage instances; in the matrix account this is a fertility flux. Growth, recurrence, and cell division that produces no higher-level descendants do not suffice. |
| **life-cycle closure** | **Identity-bearing** | Declared stages must be recurrently reestablished along the lineage; formally, Doulcier et al. require a path between every stage (irreducibility of the projection matrix). Closure distinguishes a recurrent life cycle from one-way development or a gerrymandered succession. |
| **genetic homogeneity** | **Realization-specific** | Homogeneity is a frequent consequence of clonal development or a narrow bottleneck. It increases alignment and collective-trait fidelity but is not required by reproduction as such; sexual organisms and symbiotic collectives are direct counterexamples. |
| **propagule stage** | **Realization-specific** | A propagule is one way to transmit developmental capacity. Binary fission need not have a distinct propagule, while complex cycles can include unicellular or multicellular propagules. Its existence and isolation are useful evidence, not a universal identity bearer. |
| **material overlap** | **Unknown** | Griesemer and Bourrat treat material overlap across the full process as required for a reproducer; Godfrey-Smith rejects it as universally necessary, and Bourrat permits reproducee relations without it. The dispute prevents making it a consensus identity condition. [Griesemer 2016](https://doi.org/10.1086/687865), [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-024-04880-w) |
| **reproductive autonomy** | **Identity-bearing under Bourrat's strict “reproducer” terminology** | A reproducee becomes a reproducer insofar as it can reproduce across variation in relevant background conditions. This is graded and environment-relative. It is not part of Doulcier et al.'s minimal multiplication-plus-closure test. |
| maximal physical cohesion and separability | **Evidential** | Boundaries and separation help identify candidate units and parent–offspring events, but syngamy, symbiosis, dispersal, and gradual fission show that one topology is not constitutive. |
| juvenile/adult two-stage partition | **Evidential** | Stages are required to estimate a life-cycle model, but `J/A` is one coarse-graining. Doulcier et al. explicitly allow different numbers and definitions of stages, constrained by predictive adequacy and data. |
| Q017 association, isolation, and mechanics interventions | **Evidential** | These tests establish causal dependence and robustness in snowflake yeast. Their exact budding-specific form is not reproductive identity and is partly undefined in Fernandes. |
| positive-probability threshold and common opportunity budget | **Evidential** | They are required for quantitative validation of recurrent multiplication/closure, but the numerical threshold was never fixed. Q019 introduces no replacement tolerance. |

The result is not that every implementation clause is irrelevant. Formation mode, relatedness, bottleneck, and material transmission can determine whether a weak collective cycle becomes a stable Darwinian individual. They simply do not all define the broad reproduction relation.

## 4. Life-cycle closure theory

**Fact.** Doulcier, Takacs, and Bourrat describe life cycles using three general process categories:

- **aggregation**, including syngamy and the social cycle of *Dictyostelium*;
- **transformation**, including growth and metamorphosis;
- **multiplication**, in which one candidate produces new candidate individuals belonging to a declared stage.

Their stage-structured criterion requires both:

\[
\boxed{\text{at least one multiplicative transition}}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\text{closure: every declared stage is reachable from every other along the cycle}.}
\]

In a finite matrix population model, closure is irreducibility of the projection matrix. Multiplication without closure can leave disconnected developmental sets; closure without multiplication is recurrence or perpetual development, not a branching lineage. Seasonal biosphere/ecosystem cycles are their negative cases for multiplication, and the proposed “duck–chicken” supercycle and disconnected germ/soma partition are negative cases for closure. [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162)

**Fact.** The framework intentionally places sexual metazoan cycles, aggregative *Dictyostelium* phases, ecologically scaffolded collectives, and clonal growth–fragmentation in one process vocabulary. Its worked ETI examples explicitly contrast ecological-scaffolding multiplication with snowflake-style incomplete separation and fragmentation. Its simple matrix presentation sometimes ignores aggregation in the same way demography uses one-sex models, so it is not a completed formal treatment of every fusion or many-parent case.

**Interpretation.** This is a better independent domain model than Q017 for the narrow question “does a collective life cycle multiply and recur?” It is not automatically sufficient for evolutionary individuality. The theory itself proposes closure as completion of an ETI, but other frameworks demand higher-level heredity, fitness-relevant variation, integration, conflict mediation, or reproductive autonomy. Q019 uses multiplication plus closure to classify the **reproductive cycle**, not to settle all those stronger claims.

## 5. Clonal reproduction

**Fact.** “Staying together” means that lower-level individuals remain associated after reproduction. Snowflake yeast realizes this route through incomplete mother–daughter separation, clonal growth, and mechanical fracture. High relatedness follows naturally and collective size at reproduction is heritable. [Ratcliff et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7102)

**Fact.** Comparative ETI work finds that clonal group formation is associated with obligate multicellularity, more cell types, and sterile cells; all well-supported transitions to obligate multicellularity in the surveyed within-species cases used clonal group formation. This is evidence that clonality facilitates alignment and complex individuality, not that nonclonal collectives cannot reproduce. [Fisher, Cornwallis & West 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.05.004), [West et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421402112)

**Interpretation.** Q017 promoted an especially effective **realization and conflict-suppression mechanism** into the identity of reproduction. The implication

\[
\text{collective reproducer}\Rightarrow\text{clonal development}
\]

is not supported. A narrower implication—clonal formation strongly favors faithful, conflict-limited, obligate collective individuality—has substantial empirical support.

## 6. Aggregative reproduction

**Fact.** “Coming together” means independently existing units aggregate. It occurs in social amoebae, biofilms, symbioses, syngamy, and microbial communities, and it poses cooperation and assortment problems different from staying together. *Dictyostelium* can form genetically chimeric fruiting bodies, demonstrating that aggregation need not be clonal. [Tarnita, Taubes & Nowak 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2012.11.022), [Jack et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2148-8-293)

**Fact.** Under Griesemer's reproduction account, as applied by Ereshefsky and Pedroso, a mature biofilm can release cells that aggregate with other cells and form a new biofilm; material overlap transmits developmental capacity even without a narrow bottleneck. The authors treat this as reproduction under a recognized account while emphasizing that biofilm evolutionary individuality remains debatable. [Ereshefsky & Pedroso 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421377112)

**Interpretation.** Aggregative origin is therefore not an automatic domain disqualifier. It changes parentage topology, expected relatedness, transmission fidelity, and exposure to within-group conflict. With many contributing source collectives, parentage can become diffuse enough that individual collective lineages and collective heredity cease to be useful; Doolittle and Inkpen's “too many parents” problem is a genuine negative boundary. [Doolittle & Inkpen 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1722232115)

Material-overlap theories do not imply a clonal/aggregative domain split. Snowflake fragmentation has extensive direct overlap; a single-cell propagule supplies a narrower but continuous material path across the whole cycle; and an aggregative offspring can overlap with several parent collectives. On Griesemer/Bourrat terms all can qualify if propagated material carries developmental capacity and the whole-cycle object has autonomy. What the account excludes is a supposedly autonomous reproducer reconstructed with no material propagation at all. On Godfrey-Smith's looser account, even that exclusion is disputed. The live distinction is therefore overlap/autonomy and the topology of parentage, not clonality.

## 7. Single-cell propagules

**Fact.** Doulcier et al.'s canonical metazoan cycle begins with syngamy, continues through zygote development, and includes multiplication through gamete production. The multicellular individual therefore has a life cycle that passes through unicellular gametes and a unicellular zygote. Godfrey-Smith similarly treats the human one-cell zygotic bottleneck as a high value on a gradient of definite collective reproduction. [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162), [Godfrey-Smith 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421378112)

**Fact.** Single-cell bottlenecks reduce within-organism conflict, expose genotypes to selection, and restart development; they are repeatedly analyzed as facilitators of collective heredity and individuality. [Grosberg & Strathmann 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-5347(97)01313-X), [Grosberg & Strathmann 2007](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.36.102403.114735)

**Interpretation.** A transition

\[
C\rightarrow p\rightarrow C',\qquad |p|=1,
\]

does not by itself destroy collective reproductive identity. Under a life-cycle view, (p) is a stage through which the spatiotemporally extended reproducer reconstructs (C'). It would count only as cell reproduction if no defensible causal relation connected the mature collective to recurrence of the same higher-level cycle. Q017's two-cell-at-separation cutoff is therefore a snowflake-specific boundary, not a general principle of multicellular reproduction.

## 8. Reproducer versus Darwinian individual

**Fact.** Bourrat distinguishes:

1. a **reproducee**: (B) comes into existence after (A), (A) causes (B), and both belong to the same relevant class;
2. a **reproducer**: a reproducee with some reproductive autonomy across relevant environmental backgrounds;
3. a **Darwinian individual**: a reproducer with high reproductive causal control, such that fine-grained parental differences can generate correspondingly fine-grained offspring differences and support cumulative selection.

These properties are graded and relative to object/environment boundaries. Ecologically scaffolded collectives can have externally supplied collective multiplication and high causal control yet remain reproducees rather than reproducers because the imposed patch regime supplies nearly all reproductive autonomy. [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-024-04880-w)

**Interpretation.** Q018's frozen unit bundled a reproductive process with two traits that promote Darwinian individuality: clonality and a narrow multicellular propagule. Snowflake and Fernandes can share a broad collective life-cycle reproduction relation while differing sharply in evidence for reproductive autonomy, collective heredity, and higher-level selection. Thus Q018 partly conflated the **existence/type of reproductive cycle** with the **strength of Darwinian individuality**.

## 9. Formation mode versus reproductive identity

**Fact.** “Staying together” and “coming together” are established terms for formation operations. Doulcier et al.'s independent process vocabulary makes aggregation one possible transition inside a life cycle rather than an exclusion condition. Sexual syngamy, *Dictyostelium* aggregation, and snowflake fragmentation are all described without making their formation operations synonymous with the identity of the whole cycle. [Tarnita, Taubes & Nowak 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2012.11.022), [van Gestel & Tarnita 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1704631114)

**Interpretation.** The supported separation is:

\[
F\in\{\text{staying together, coming together, symbiotic association, syngamy}\},
\]

while

\[
R=\text{causal multiplication plus recurrent reconstruction of declared life-cycle stages}.
\]

Two systems can have different (F) and the same coarse (R). This is an analytical separation, not causal independence: (F) strongly affects relatedness, partner number, developmental fidelity, autonomy, and conflict, and can therefore move a system along an individuality axis.

Biological surveys normally describe clonal and aggregative multicellularity as different **group-formation or developmental modes** and compare their consequences for relatedness, cell-type complexity, obligacy, and conflict. They do not establish that those modes are, merely by being different, fundamentally different kinds of reproducing biological individual. [Fisher, Cornwallis & West 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.05.004), [Brunet & King 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2017.09.016)

## 10. Independent biological stress tests

The following cases were selected from the question and established prior theory before formulating the Q019 abstraction; none was chosen to rescue Fernandes.

### 10.1 Sexual metazoan life cycle

An adult produces unicellular gametes; syngamy produces a genetically nonidentical unicellular zygote; development reconstructs a multicellular adult. This is an uncontroversial multicellular reproductive cycle and directly rejects minimum two-cell offspring, parent–offspring clonality, and fragmentation as general identity conditions. It preserves causal parentage, multiplication, and closure.

### 10.2 Aggregative *Dictyostelium*

Independent amoebae can aggregate into a genetically chimeric slug and fruiting body; some cells form a dead stalk and others become dispersing spores. This is an accepted aggregative multicellular/social life-cycle phase. Yet Wilmsen and Kost classify the slug as a Type V physiological unit whose subunits retain evolutionary autonomy and whose composition is remixed, rather than a Type VI unit with one joint evolutionary lineage. It therefore supports life-cycle recurrence while attacking the inference from recurrence to strong collective Darwinian individuality. [Strassmann, Zhu & Queller 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35050087), [Wilmsen & Kost 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.70098)

### 10.3 Scaffolded *Pseudomonas* collectives

Experimental/theoretical *Pseudomonas* systems can be propagated through a two-phase collective life cycle or single-cell-founded patches and can evolve collective-like heredity and fitness effects. The multiplication and recurrence are real under the protocol. Bourrat's audit nevertheless classifies exogenously patch-scaffolded collectives as reproducees with little reproductive autonomy until the scaffold is endogenized. This is a strong case in which multiplication plus closure and reproductive autonomy come apart. [Hammerschmidt et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13884), [Black, Bourrat & Rainey 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-019-1086-9), [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-024-04880-w)

### 10.4 Multispecies biofilms and vertically transmitted symbioses

Some biofilms reconstruct through release and aggregation of cells from several lineages; some symbiotic collectives vertically cotransmit unlike constituent lineages. Griesemer-style material propagation can recognize reproduction in the former, while Wilmsen and Kost place strictly cotransmitted, physiologically integrated symbioses in their strongest Type VI category. Genetic homogeneity is therefore not constitutive of either reproduction or every strong evolutionary collective. Parent number, cotransmission, and fidelity—not taxonomic sameness—carry the relevant evolutionary information. [Ereshefsky & Pedroso 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421377112), [Wilmsen & Kost 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.70098)

## 11. Wilmsen–Kost 2026 framework

**Fact.** Wilmsen and Kost independently separate a focal unit's **physiological component**—its integrated survival/maintenance organization—from its **evolutionary component**—heritable fitness-relevant variation forming a traceable evolutionary lineage. Their six structural categories range from units possessing only one component through multipartite associations to Type VI units with one coherent physiological system and one joint evolutionary component. The classification is context- and life-stage-dependent. [Wilmsen & Kost 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.70098)

Relevant contrasts are:

- Type IV: an aggregation of lower-level physiological/evolutionary units that retain autonomy;
- Type V: a coherent physiological collective whose subunits retain separate evolutionary lineages, including *Dictyostelium* slugs and horizontally reconstructed obligate associations;
- Type VI: a coherent physiological collective with a joint evolutionary component, commonly supported by strict cotransmission or reproductive division of labour.

**Interpretation.** The framework does not replace the reproduction analysis, but it explains why it was a mistake to let clonality decide both reproduction and individuality. Snowflake's clonal bottleneck and collective trait heredity provide evidence toward a joint evolutionary component. Fernandes' aggregates have coordinated migration and regulated propagules, but the modeled evolutionary consequences accrue to cell lineages, clusters contain distant lineages, and no collective parentage/heritability assay was reported. Depending on how much physiological interdependence is required, Fernandes could fall in Type IV or V during its collective phase; Type VI is not established. Early snowflake's exact Wilmsen–Kost category is also not fully inferable without their prescribed autonomy/interdependence diagnostics.

Thus the 2026 framework does not yield a clean same/different category assignment from current evidence. It does independently support the decisive two-axis structure: similar life-cycle organization can coexist with different physiological integration and evolutionary unity.

## 12. Snowflake classification

**Fact.** Snowflake yeast has a collective growth–fragmentation cycle: incomplete post-budding separation constructs a clonal cluster; growth and mechanical stress generate multicellular propagules; propagules reconstruct mature clusters. Collective life-history traits are heritable, and later large/tough forms retain the clonal cycle. [Ratcliff et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1115323109), [Ratcliff et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7102), [Bozdag et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06052-1)

**Interpretation under independent theory.** Snowflake satisfies collective parentage, multiplication, and life-cycle closure without using `ACE2`, clonality, two-cell offspring, or fragmentation in the identity statement. Those facts specify its realization and help explain its high relatedness and evolutionary consolidation. It is a clear collective life-cycle reproducer and has stronger evidence for collective Darwinian individuality than Fernandes. The broader description does not erase the narrower clonal growth–fragmentation subtype.

## 13. Fernandes classification

The following returns to the preprint only after the independent criteria have been fixed.

1. **Higher-level multiplication — structurally supported for propagule regimes.** A migrating aggregate emits a cell or group; emitted material founds/reconstructs further aggregates. One source aggregate can emit multiple propagules. High-adhesion tearing is qualitatively multiplicative, but its reliability is unquantified.
2. **Life-cycle closure — qualitatively supported, not formally estimated.** The reported single-cell sequence recurrently connects aggregate, detached cell, division/re-adhesion, and aggregate stages. Multicellular-propagule cycles likewise return to an aggregate stage. The paper does not estimate a Doulcier-style projection matrix or test irreducibility under an independently chosen stage partition.
3. **Collective parentage — supported in a broad material-causal sense, unresolved in a strong one-parent lineage sense.** A source cluster causally supplies material that enters descendant clusters. Subsequent aggregation with other lineages makes parentage potentially many-to-one and prevents current evidence from assigning stable collective genealogies or transmission fidelity.
4. **Aggregation — not disqualifying for the broad life-cycle relation.** It is one recognized reconstruction process. It does weaken clonal parentage and increases internal conflict.
5. **Single-cell propagule — not disqualifying.** It is a life-cycle stage, as in many multicellular reproducers. It may increase a focal lineage's relatedness, although reaggregation later can undo that effect at the whole-cluster level.
6. **Unresolved evidence.** The preprint does not establish collective-level heritable fitness variation, a joint evolutionary lineage, quantitative stage-transition distributions, collective reproductive autonomy across controlled background changes, or whether offspring-cluster properties are predicted by parent-cluster rather than cell-lineage composition.

**Interpretation.** Fernandes belongs structurally to the broad **collective life-cycle reproduction** domain under Doulcier-style multiplication and closure. Whether its clusters are **reproducers** rather than merely **reproducees** in Bourrat's stricter sense, and whether they are Darwinian individuals, remains unresolved. This does not alter the paper's actual cell-level evolutionary semantics or convert aggregation into clonal descent. [Fernandes, Vroomans & Colizzi 2026, uncertified preprint v3](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.08.681199)

## 15. Counterexamples

| Requested attack | Strongest case | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **A — accepted aggregative, nonclonal multicellular reproducer** | Multispecies biofilm under Griesemer's material-propagation account; chimeric *Dictyostelium* supplies an accepted aggregative multicellular cycle but disputed collective reproduction | **Found under recognized, nonuniversal accounts.** Enough to defeat a universal clonality implication; not enough to settle individuality. |
| **B — multicellular reproducer with a single-cell propagule** | Sexual metazoan: adult → gametes/zygote → adult | **Decisive.** Q017's two-cell cutoff cannot be general. |
| **C — clonal cluster that grows/fragments but fails stronger higher-level reproduction** | No clean, independently accepted endogenous growth–fragmentation case was located. Externally scaffolded clonal collectives are the closest negative, but their multiplication is imposed rather than ordinary fragmentation. | **Not found cleanly.** No contrary case is invented. |
| **D — aggregative recurrent cycle that fails higher-level individuality** | *Dictyostelium* slug/fruiting-body cycle; Wilmsen–Kost Type V, with constituent evolutionary autonomy and mixing | **Found.** Recurrent multicellular development does not entail one joint evolutionary lineage. |
| **E — multiplication + closure with different autonomy** | Snowflake versus exogenously scaffolded *Pseudomonas* collectives | **Found.** Bourrat classifies the latter as reproducees with little autonomy until endogenization. |
| **F — different formation mechanisms, same reproductive type** | Sexual syngamy, clonal growth–fragmentation, and propagule reconstruction in Doulcier et al.'s life-cycle vocabulary | **Found at the coarse life-cycle level.** Formation remains evolutionarily consequential. |
| **G — closure groups cases that another ETI theory separates** | Doulcier-style closed aggregative cycles versus Wilmsen–Kost's separation of *Dictyostelium* Type V from Type VI joint evolutionary units | **Found.** Closure is a reproductive/temporal classifier, not a consensus sufficient condition for strong individuality. |

The strongest attack on domain unification is not aggregation itself. It is **diffuse many-parent reconstruction with weak parent–offspring fidelity or low reproductive autonomy**. Those properties can erase a collective lineage even when aggregates recur.

## 16. Central decision

**D — REPRODUCTION SHARED, INDIVIDUALITY DIFFERS**

Snowflake and Fernandes occupy the same broad, independently supported domain of **collective life-cycle reproduction**: candidate collective stages undergo causal multiplication and recurrent reconstruction. They realize that relation differently—clonal staying-together fragmentation versus aggregative propagule reconstruction—and those differences strongly affect relatedness, parentage topology, conflict, and trait transmission.

Snowflake has direct evidence for clonal collective heredity and collective life-history selection. Fernandes has qualitative collective multiplication and closure but cell-lineage fitness semantics, heterogeneous aggregates, and no demonstrated collective heredity/autonomy assay. Its status as a strong Darwinian individual, or even as a Bourrat-strict autonomous reproducer rather than a reproducee, is therefore weaker and partly unknown.

**Why not B alone:** Q017 is over-specified, but that label hides the important second axis exposed by Bourrat and Wilmsen–Kost.

**Why not C:** clonal and aggregative forms are useful reproductive/developmental subtypes, but no independent evidence found makes formation mode itself an identity-bearing split inside the broad reproduction domain.

**Why not E:** Fernandes is evidentially indeterminate on autonomy and Darwinian individuality, but its reported process is sufficient for a structural life-cycle-reproduction classification without inventing a numerical threshold.

This decision leaves Q018 historically intact and preserves Q016: it is a domain-specific classification, not a universal invariant.

## 17. Mechanism readiness

**CONDITIONALLY READY**

Independent theory now supports a conceptual target that covers sexual, aggregative, scaffolded, symbiotic, and clonal cases while rejecting recurrence without multiplication and multiplication without closure. It classifies Snowflake and the reported Fernandes propagule regimes structurally without target-aware adjustment.

Readiness is conditional because:

- Fernandes lacks an independent collective-lineage and parent–offspring fidelity analysis;
- Doulcier-style transition probabilities and irreducibility were not estimated;
- Bourrat-style reproductive autonomy was not intervened on;
- exact cross-system causal interventions remain unavailable;
- no numerical threshold is supplied or introduced here.

The next pass must validate which side of the reproduction/individuality boundary Fernandes occupies before a shared mechanism question is legitimate.

## 18. Remaining explanatory gap

The narrow remaining gap is:

> Does Fernandes' evolved aggregate → propagule → aggregate cycle possess enough internally generated collective parentage, trait transmission, and robustness to background change to be a reproductively autonomous collective lineage, or is it a recurrent reproducee process whose evolutionary continuity resides only in cell lineages?

This is not a search for what causes OEE. It is a lineage/autonomy validation problem.

## 20. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### ESTABLISHED / KNOWN

- Multiplication and life-cycle closure are established prior-art conditions for a higher-level life-cycle account.
- That account explicitly admits aggregation and clonal growth–fragmentation as process realizations.
- Sexual multicellular reproduction disproves a general requirement for multicellularity at the propagule stage.
