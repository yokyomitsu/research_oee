# Q027 — Continuity Across Changing Evolutionary Units

One theoretical research pass, completed 2026-09-08. No simulation or implementation was performed. `Fact` reports literature or a consequence of an explicitly stated formalism; `Interpretation` reports the conclusion of this pass; `Hypothesis` is reserved for a new falsifiable dynamical claim. No such hypothesis survives.

## 1. Research question

> Which partial causal mappings support claims of accumulated evolutionary change when hereditary or selectable units change, and which comparisons must remain undefined?

This is the exact highest-information question left by Q026. The practical issue is whether a transition can remain part of one cumulative evolutionary history when the entities realizing heredity, reproduction, or selection change, without inventing a universal identity bearer.

## 2. Difference from Q011

Q011 asked **how a transition is bridged** and found established, transition-specific mechanisms: ecological scaffolding, multilevel selection, nascent life cycles, and endogenization. This pass does not catalogue or reopen those mechanisms. It asks **what makes the history on the two sides one evolutionary process**.

The distinction is:

\[
\underbrace{R_A\xrightarrow{M}R_B}_{\text{transition mechanism}}
\qquad\text{versus}\qquad
\underbrace{R_A\rightsquigarrow R_B}_{\text{scientifically justified continuity relation}}.
\]

`Interpretation:` Q011's mechanisms already contain much of the answer, but only after their ancestry, reproduction, reconstruction, or selective-contribution relations are made explicit. Mere temporal adjacency and mere set inclusion, such as \(U_A\subset U_B\), do not do that work.

## 3. Meanings of evolutionary continuity

The five proposed meanings do not coincide.

| Meaning | What persists or connects | Strength | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material | Physical components or descendants contribute to later units | Grounds many organismal reproduction claims | Material can persist in a heap, aggregate, or ecosystem with no higher heredity |
| Genealogical | Parent–offspring or ancestor–descendant relations connect events | Supports descent and accumulation | Need not be a tree or a unique lineage; a genealogy alone need not transmit the focal organization |
| Hereditary | Variants are recurrently reconstructed with parent- or source-specific dependence | Can survive carrier turnover | Stable traits can be environmentally recreated without a cumulative lineage |
| Selective | Earlier differential reproduction changes the population from which a later selective organization emerges | Connects evolutionary causes through an overlap interval | Selection may pause during dormancy, and inheritance-system changes need not change selectable units |
| Process | A causally linked sequence reconstructs Darwinian relations while their realizers change | Most general scientifically useful sense | Becomes trivial if every historical causal influence counts |

`Interpretation:` process continuity is an umbrella conclusion, not an extra mechanism. It is warranted by one or more domain-specific material, genealogical, hereditary, reproductive, or selective relations. It does not entail preservation of an individual, genotype, trait, fitness value, or information quantity.

## 4. Units and roles

Let \(U_H\), \(U_R\), and \(U_S\) denote, only schematically, the entities assigned hereditary, reproductive, and selectable roles. Established units-of-selection and major-transition work gives no reason to assume

\[
U_H=U_R=U_S.
\]

A gene can be a transmitted hereditary difference without being a reproducing organism; an organism can reproduce while a trait is reconstructed through genetic, epigenetic, ecological, or cultural inputs; and a collective can be a target or beneficiary of selection before it becomes a paradigmatic reproducer. Godfrey-Smith's Darwinian-population framework deliberately admits marginal cases, while Okasha's multilevel framework separates selection among particles from selection among collectives rather than positing one universal evolutionary unit ([Godfrey-Smith 2009](https://academic.oup.com/book/4808), [Okasha 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199267972.001.0001)).

`Interpretation:` continuity must be claim-specific along these axes. A transition can preserve genetic heredity while changing the unit of reproduction and selection, or add an inheritance channel without forming a higher-level individual. Describing transitions as changes in relations among roles is more accurate than treating them all as replacements of one object called “the evolutionary unit.” Role preservation alone is insufficient: two causally unrelated systems can instantiate the same abstract Darwinian roles.

## 5. Major-transition prior art

`Fact:` Maynard Smith and Szathmáry treat major transitions as changes in both organizational level and information transmission, including chromosomes, the genetic code, sex, multicellularity, societies, and language. Their classification therefore does not require every transition to preserve a single kind of individual or to add one hierarchical level ([Maynard Smith & Szathmáry 1995](https://academic.oup.com/book/40636)). Szathmáry's later synthesis similarly combines information-limited and individuality transitions rather than deriving them from one invariant carrier ([Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112)).

`Fact:` Michod explains transitions in individuality through the emergence of heritable collective fitness, cooperation, and conflict mediation; Griesemer analyzes reproduction through material overlap and developmental capacity; Godfrey-Smith represents populations as more or less paradigmatically Darwinian; Okasha analyzes simultaneous levels of selection; and Doulcier, Takacs, and Bourrat derive candidate higher-level life cycles by coarse-graining lower-level trajectories ([Michod 1997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1688438/), [Griesemer 2000](https://doi.org/10.1556/Select.1.2000.1-3.7), [Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162)). None requires conserved numerical fitness, an instantaneous handoff, or continued identity of one carrier.

`Interpretation:` major-transition theory already treats continuity relationally. It supplies overlapping populations, reproductive histories, developmental contribution, trait reconstruction, and emerging life cycles. It describes reorganization or partial replacement of evolutionary roles, not conservation of an evolutionary substance.

## 6. Darwinian-population overlap

Godfrey-Smith's distinction among paradigm, minimal, and marginal Darwinian populations permits a transition trajectory in which a higher-level population becomes progressively more Darwinian while lower-level entities remain reproductively and selectively active. His analysis of eukaryogenesis moves from two simple reproducers through a collective reproducer toward scaffolded reproduction; it explicitly treats reproductive categories as gradual rather than separated by sharp cutoffs ([Godfrey-Smith 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4547265/)). A defensible schematic is therefore

\[
D_L\longrightarrow (D_L,D_H)\longrightarrow D_H,
\]

where the final term need not mean extinction of all lower-level selection.

`Interpretation:` an overlap interval often eliminates the need for a handoff instant \(t^*\). During \([t_0,t_1]\), lower-level lineages generate the material and variation entering collectives, differential collective outcomes begin, and higher-level reproduction or heredity becomes stabilized. This supplies continuity without unit identity. It is not a universal necessary condition: a dormant lineage can preserve descent without active selection, and a new hereditary channel can be added without a new higher-level Darwinian population.

## 7. Fitness reorganization

Michod's “fitness transfer” and “fitness reorganization” language identifies a genuine ETI pattern: cooperation and conflict mediation can reduce independent component fitness while increasing heritable variation in collective performance. It does **not** imply a conservation law

\[
W_L+W_H=\text{constant}
\]

or a literal movement of one quantity from particles to collectives.

`Fact:` Bourrat showed that particle and collective fitness are often evaluated over inconsistent timescales and that component fitness need not fall to zero in an ETI. Later work argues that fitness decoupling or export is at best a metaphor unless a specific trade-off-breaking mechanism is supplied ([Bourrat 2015](https://doi.org/10.3998/ptb.6959004.0007.001), [Bourrat et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9470156/)).

`Interpretation:` covariance at one level can causally alter the population in which covariance at another becomes meaningful, but \(W_L\) and \(W_H\) are generally different model-relative quantities. Fitness reorganization is evidence of changing selective organization; it is neither a conserved bridge variable nor, by itself, proof that the higher-level units reproduce or inherit variants.

## 8. Reproducer / material-overlap account

Griesemer's reproducer framework joins multiplication to development: offspring arise with material overlap from parental propagules that carry developmental capacity. This is stronger than resemblance and helps explain why a reorganized biological life cycle remains genealogically connected rather than being a fresh origin at each generation ([Griesemer 2000](https://doi.org/10.1556/Select.1.2000.1-3.7)). During multicellular transitions, lower-level material lineages can enter propagules, contribute to development, and thereby connect cell-level histories to collective reproduction.

Its scope is limited.

- Material continuity is not sufficient. Cells can persist through serial aggregates or ecological associations without any collective multiplication, closed life cycle, or transmitted collective variation.
- A single unbranched material lineage is not necessary. Aggregation, fusion, hybridization, and symbiogenesis have many-to-one ancestry.
- Material parent–offspring overlap in this biological sense does not straightforwardly cover cultural transmission, ecological inheritance, or some epigenetic reconstruction.
- Continuous metabolic or selective activity is not necessary. Dormant propagules preserve developmental and genealogical capacity through intervals with little or no active selection.

`Interpretation:` material overlap is a powerful domain-specific justification for a reproductive edge. It is not a universal criterion of evolutionary continuity and should not be weakened into mere persistence of matter.

## 9. Reconstitutor account

Veigl, Suárez, and Stencel define a **reconstitutor** relationally: elements or processes actively recreate a phenotypic variant across generations, whether or not there is stable material ancestry at the focal level. Their framework separates **Stability of Traits** (SOT) from **Stability of Lineages** (SOL), and states that all reproducers are reconstitutors but not all reconstitutors are reproducers ([Veigl, Suárez & Stencel 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-022-03810-y)). It can cover small-RNA, microbiome, ecological, and other inheritance relations missed by a narrow carrier account.

This supplies a bridge stronger than resemblance when the reconstructive factor is difference-making, recurs across generations, and has selective affinity to a declared variant. It does not by itself establish a continuous cumulative process: SOT without SOL can recur through the same environmental cause in unrelated systems, and reconstitution alone does not determine parentage, differential reproductive contribution, or a unique ancestry path.

`Interpretation:` hereditary continuity can be supported by stable reconstructive relations even when material carriers turn over, but cumulative evolutionary continuity still requires a historically justified source–recipient or ancestry network. The reconstitutor complements rather than replaces reproduction and genealogy.

## 10. Life-cycle closure

Doulcier, Takacs, and Bourrat construct candidate higher-level stages by coarse-graining trajectories of lower-level entities. A higher-level life cycle requires at least one multiplication transition plus closure: stages must lie in a connected, irreducible cycle rather than form a transient succession. Whole-cycle growth can then be assigned at the level of the closed cycle ([Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/evolutionary-transitions-in-individuality-and-life-cycle-closure/D4B2EDAD4E9F1C831B1DE72429791B62)).

The bridge is composite:

\[
\text{lower-level trajectories}
\xrightarrow{\text{domain-justified coarse-graining}}
\text{higher-level stages}
\xrightarrow{\text{multiplication + closure}}
\text{higher-level life cycle}.
\]

Lower-level trajectories provide causal and material history; coarse-graining proposes higher-level stages; multiplication and closure justify treating them as a reproducing cycle. Coarse-graining alone does not establish the biological interpretation, because many partitions of a trajectory can be mathematically available. This framework is explicit and strong for ETIs, but it does not directly cover sex, coding-system change, or cultural inheritance when no embedding higher-level individual is formed.

## 11. Price-equation / multilevel formalism

The Price equation is exact once two sets and a mapping between them are specified. It can accommodate changing counts, ancestor–descendant relations, and recursive partitions into within- and between-group change. That flexibility is useful when units are not ontologically identical ([Frank 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2012.02498.x)).

But the equation does not decide which objects are ancestors, which traits are comparable, or which grouping represents a biological level. Its causal interpretation requires a substantive causal model; an algebraically valid decomposition is not a unique causal decomposition ([Okasha & Otsuka 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7133506/)). Bourrat likewise argues that MLS1/MLS2 Price descriptions depend on mappings and explanatory purpose rather than isolating two uniquely objective processes ([Bourrat 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-023-04285-1)).

Therefore

\[
\phi:U_t\rightarrow U_{t+1}
\]

is bookkeeping until material descent, reproduction, reconstitution, causal contribution, or another domain theory justifies \(\phi\). Recursive Price equations partition selection at already declared levels; they do not by themselves explain the emergence of an evolutionary level or establish continuity across it.

## 12. Hereditary-system changes outside ETI

At least three non-ETI cases defeat the identification of regime change with higher-level individuality.

1. **RNA-like heredity to DNA/protein division of labour.** Major-transition classifications treat the change in storage, catalysis, and translation as a transformation of information transmission. The historical route is incompletely resolved, but the conceptual transition does not consist in cells becoming components of a higher individual. Continuity would be grounded by chemical ancestry, template use, and coexisting old/new replication and decoding processes—not by a conserved information quantity.
2. **Sexual reproduction.** Sex reorganizes inheritance through recombination and biparental contribution. It creates reticulate ancestry and changes genotype transmission without necessarily changing the organismal unit of selection into a higher-level individual.
3. **Added epigenetic or cultural inheritance.** Genetic transmission can remain while new reconstructive channels alter what variants recur. Jablonka and Lamb's information-transition treatment explicitly includes genetic, epigenetic, behavioral, and symbolic systems ([Jablonka & Lamb 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2005.08.038)). Cultural transmission can be vertical, oblique, or horizontal and can use teaching, institutions, and artifacts rather than biological parent–offspring material overlap; whether a particular case is Darwinian still requires an explicit population and transmission model ([Lewens 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3982669/)).

`Interpretation:` no single material, organismal, or hierarchical criterion covers both ETIs and hereditary-system changes. What can be mapped is the causal participation of old processes in establishing or constraining new transmission relations. “Evolutionary information remained continuous” adds nothing unless content, carrier, decoder, and comparison relation are specified; no conserved information magnitude or semantics is established.

## 13. Addition, replacement, and reorganization of inheritance

The following is an analytical distinction, not a proposed universal taxonomy:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{addition: }&H_A\rightarrow(H_A,H_B),\\
\text{replacement: }&H_A\rightarrow H_B,\\
\text{reorganization: }&(H_A,H_B)\rightarrow H_C.
\end{aligned}
\]

- In **addition**, channel overlap directly supplies historical continuity, but the channels need not transmit the same traits or use the same units.
- In **replacement**, a justified transition requires evidence that the old evolutionary process causally produced, selected, or developmentally sustained the new channel during an overlap or conversion history. Simple succession is insufficient.
- In **reorganization**, old processes can persist as components of a new system while their former autonomy, transmission roles, and trait semantics change. Symbiogenesis is the clearest case.

Actual histories can combine all three. Genetic and cultural inheritance are often layered rather than replacements; gene transfer, loss, and new nuclear control during eukaryogenesis reorganize contributions rather than moving a complete hereditary store intact. Thus transmission-channel overlap is common and informative, but it is not a universal necessary condition or a conserved-channel theorem.

## 14. Reticulate and many-to-one continuity

Eukaryogenesis is a decisive stress test. An archaeal host lineage and bacterial endosymbiont lineage became causally integrated; organellar genes were transferred or lost, nuclear control expanded, and an initially collective reproductive relation became scaffolded. Godfrey-Smith emphasizes that “gene transfer” can involve coevolutionary loss and replacement rather than literal movement of all functions ([Godfrey-Smith 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4547265/)). There is no unique answer to which ancestral lineage is **the** continuous process: both contribute to the new lineage, although in unequal and changing ways.

Aggregation, fusion, hybridization, horizontal transfer, and cultural blending have the same structural lesson. Evolutionary ancestry can be a directed causal network with many-to-one and one-to-many relations. Requiring one parent, one lineage carrier, or one successor would misclassify accepted evolutionary histories. The relevant maps are partial: each ancestral source may map only some material, developmental capacity, hereditary differences, or causal roles into the successor.

## 15. Reversions

Schenkel, Ågren, and Patten distinguish **paradigmatic reversions**, which undo an organizational transition, from **agential reversions**, in which the higher organization persists but prevailing selection or adaptation shifts downward because of internal conflict. They also show that what counts as transition or reversion depends on the declared concept of individuality ([Schenkel, Ågren & Patten 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41604221/)).

Thus a continuity framework must allow

\[
D_L\rightarrow D_H\rightarrow D'_L
\]

without treating evolutionary history as broken merely because direction reverses. In an agential reversion, higher-level organization may remain materially and developmentally continuous while selective agency changes. In a paradigmatic reversion, lower-level descendants can remain genealogically linked even after the higher-level life cycle disappears. No monotonic hierarchy or permanent handoff is required.

## 16. Historical versus evolutionary continuity

**Historical continuity** says only that state \(B\) was causally produced from state \(A\). **Evolutionary continuity** is stronger: the route contains a domain-justified ancestry, transmission, reproduction, reconstruction, or selected-contribution relation through which the earlier evolutionary process helps constitute the later one.

A destructive thought experiment fixes the boundary. Suppose

\[
E_A\rightarrow X\rightarrow E_B,
\]

where \(X\) destroys every hereditary, reproductive, genealogical, and reconstructive relation of \(E_A\), and an independent Darwinian system later originates. Even if an artifact of \(E_A\) caused the destruction or set the background conditions for \(E_B\), causal adjacency alone does not make \(E_B\) an accumulated continuation of \(E_A\). Likewise, a meteor can alter selection and enable a later radiation, but impact causation alone is not a hereditary-system transition.

The converse dormancy case shows that continuously active selection is too strong. A propagule can retain genealogy and developmental capacity through an interval with negligible reproduction or selection, then resume. At the origin-of-life boundary, pre-Darwinian chemistry can causally generate Darwinian heredity, but it is more precise to call that the origin of a Darwinian evolutionary process unless a broader, independently defined pre-Darwinian evolutionary relation is supplied.

Continuity also need not be purely endogenous. Environmental scaffolds, geological change, or encounters with symbionts can be indispensable causes. What matters is whether evolutionary dynamics mediate the new organization—for example, by differentially retaining, reproducing, or reconstructing it—rather than whether every causal input originated inside the earlier population. Selection-mediated stabilization of an initially scaffolded organization is already the subject matter of ecological scaffolding and endogenization theory, not a separate continuity mechanism.

`Interpretation:` evolutionary continuity is not “historical continuity only,” but neither is it a new causal force. It is a warranted classification of a causal history whose constitutive evolutionary relations are linked rather than reset.

## 17. Candidate minimal bridge

Prior art supports the following **minimal relational schema**, not a universal invariant or new theory:

1. A transition-specific domain theory identifies a non-arbitrary causal ancestry, transmission, reproduction, reconstruction, or selected-contribution relation between pre- and post-transition organizations.
2. That relation carries developmental capacity, reconstructs a variant, or makes an evolutionary difference to the population from which the new organization emerges. Mere contact or common matter is insufficient.
3. The later organization supports its own defensible relations of variation, heredity, and differential reproduction; these need not be realized by the old units.

This can be represented schematically as a **partial**, possibly reticulate relation

\[
\Phi_D:\mathcal E_A\rightsquigarrow\mathcal E_B,
\qquad
\mathcal E=(U_H,U_R,U_S;H,R,S),
\]

where subscript \(D\) indicates that biological or cultural domain theory—not the notation—justifies the mapped entities and relations. No claim is made that all coordinates map.

This also limits observer discretion. Alternative coarse-grainings may remain possible, but an analyst cannot arbitrarily declare continuity: the proposed entities and edges must satisfy independently defended reproduction, heredity, ancestry, reconstruction, or selection criteria and support the relevant counterfactual or predictive claims.

What may remain undefined includes cross-level conserved fitness, a universal identity of \(U_H,U_R,U_S\), a conserved amount or semantics of hereditary information, a unique ancestor in reticulate histories, and trait comparisons for which no homology, descent, or reconstruction relation exists. Leaving those comparisons undefined is a strength, not an incompleteness.

## 18. Counterexamples

### A — Material lineage without higher-level heredity

Cells can persist through repeated aggregations or ecological groups while no parent-specific collective organization is transmitted and no closed collective life cycle exists. Material contribution alone therefore does not establish higher-level evolutionary continuity.

### B — Trait heredity without stable focal material lineage

Small-RNA, microbiome, ecological, and cultural factors can reconstitute variants across generations despite turnover or weak focal material ancestry. This supports hereditary continuity under a reconstitutor account, but SOT alone does not establish a cumulative genealogy.

### C — Higher-level Darwinian emergence while lower-level selection continues

During an ETI, collective heritability and differential success can strengthen while cells or symbionts continue to vary and undergo selection. The old and new Darwinian populations overlap; there is no instantaneous transfer.

### D — Partial reversion

Agential reversion can shift adaptive control downward while multicellular organization remains. Paradigmatic reversion can dismantle the higher organization while genealogical descendants continue. Continuity cannot require monotonic upward individuality.

### E — Symbiogenesis and many-to-one ancestry

Eukaryogenesis combines host and endosymbiont histories. No single ancestral carrier exhausts continuity, and functions can be lost, replaced, transferred, or newly constructed.

### F — Hereditary-channel change without a new individual

Sex, RNA/DNA/protein division of labour, and added epigenetic inheritance reorganize transmission without necessarily forming an embedding higher-level individual. An ETI-only criterion is therefore too narrow.

### G — Cultural heredity without biological material overlap

Horizontal and oblique learning, teaching, artifacts, and institutions can transmit reconstructed variants among biologically unrelated people. Where a cultural-evolution model independently establishes variation, transmission, and differential propagation, Griesemer-style organismal material overlap is not necessary.

### H — Causal contact without evolutionary continuity

A lineage can transform an environment, become extinct, and be followed by an independently originating hereditary system. The first history can causally affect the second's conditions without supplying ancestry, transmission, reconstruction, or selected contribution. Bare causal history is insufficient.

### I — A valid but arbitrary Price mapping

An analyst can assign descendants to ancestral groups by spatial proximity or a convenient label and obtain an exact Price decomposition. Unless reproduction, parentage, reconstitution, or another causal relation supports that assignment, the equation does not establish evolutionary continuity.

### J — An accepted transition with no single persistent unit, trait, or carrier

Eukaryogenesis begins with distinct reproducing lineages and ends with a scaffolded eukaryotic lineage after integration, gene loss, transfer, and changed reproductive control. No one fitness-bearing unit, material carrier, or trait remains the invariant of the entire transition. What persists is a network of partial material, genealogical, developmental, and hereditary contributions.

Together these counterexamples reject Candidates 1–5 as universal necessities: no particular material lineage, information item, phenotype, reproductive relation, or selectable unit must persist in every case. Candidate 7—historical dependence alone—is too weak. Candidate 6 survives only in plural form: at least one domain-justified causal ancestry or reconstruction chain must bridge the histories, but its realization is transition-specific.

## 19. Prior-art absorption tests

### Test 1

\[
\boxed{
\text{continuity across changing units}
=
\text{multilevel overlap}
+
\text{causal ancestry}
+
\text{reproduction/reconstruction}
+
\text{transition-specific domain theory}
}
\]

**Result: absorbed.** The plus signs denote complementary explanatory resources, not one new conjunctive mechanism. Darwinian-population and multilevel theory handle overlapping selective populations; reproducer and reconstitutor accounts supply different transmission relations; life-cycle theory supplies closure for ETIs; reticulate genealogy handles multiple contributors; and domain theory constrains the otherwise arbitrary mappings. No missing causal process remains after these are specified.

### Test 2

\[
\boxed{
\text{evolutionary continuity is historical,
not an additional mechanistic property}
}
\]

**Result: accepted with a qualification.** Continuity is a property of a properly specified causal evolutionary history, not a substance or mechanism additional to that history. But “historical” must not mean any causal succession: the history must contain a justified evolutionary bridge relation. The complete-discontinuity example excludes causal contact with total relational reset.

`Interpretation:` there is no need for a universal continuity theorem. There is a general relational schema and a family of established domain-specific bridge relations. The unresolved work is validating which relation actually holds in a case.

## 20. OEE relevance

OEE can coherently include changes in what reproduces, what is inherited, and what selection differentiates because cumulative history does not require one persistent unit. The old process need not be declared dead whenever its roles are reorganized, provided a scientifically justified evolutionary bridge links the organizations. Conversely, not every causally later evolutionary system is a continuation; that would trivialize “open-ended evolution across regime change.”

This conclusion is deliberately narrow:

\[
\boxed{\text{continuity through one or more transitions}\not\Rightarrow\text{OEE}.}
\]

It neither reopens F2 nor shows that transitions recur. It only makes cumulative evolutionary history across changing Darwinian realizers conceptually defensible.

## 21. Central decision

\[
\boxed{\textbf{A — EXISTING RELATIONAL THEORY SUFFICIENT}}
\]

Multilevel overlap, causal ancestry, reproduction and reconstruction, life-cycle closure, reticulate histories, and transition-specific domain theory adequately ground continuity claims. The subsidiary conclusion **NO CONSERVED UNIT REQUIRED** is true, but it is not selected as the primary result because it states only what is unnecessary; Result A also identifies what positively warrants continuity.

## 22. Mechanism readiness

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NOT READY}}
\]

No independently missing causal phenomenon survives the absorption tests. The mechanisms that generate and stabilize transitions are already studied by transition theory; “continuity” classifies their causally connected history. A new intervention on “continuity itself” would duplicate interventions on ancestry, reproduction, reconstruction, selection, or life-cycle organization.

## 23. Problem classification

\[
\boxed{\textbf{VALIDATION}}
\]

For a particular claimed transition, the remaining task is to validate the proposed partial bridge relations using parentage, developmental, transmission, reconstructive, or selective evidence. This is not a mechanism gap. It is also not primarily a definition or formalization gap: existing relational formalisms are adequate once domain theory justifies their mappings.

## 24. Remaining explanatory gap

No general explanatory gap remains. The narrow case-specific residue is:

> Which ancestry, reproduction, reconstruction, or selected-contribution relations actually connect the declared pre- and post-transition organizations, and which cross-regime quantities lack a justified comparison map?

This question can be difficult—especially for reticulate transitions, extinct histories, and changing trait semantics—but difficulty of historical validation does not imply an unknown continuity mechanism.

## 25. Evoloops / Lenia / Autopoiesis / deep-learning contribution

- **Evoloops — USEFUL.** They provide a fixed-unit Darwinian baseline in which genome, reproducer, and selectable loop remain comparatively stable. They help show what is absent when unit-changing continuity is not at issue, but do not supply a bridge theory.
- **Lenia — NONE.** Continuous self-organizing morphology does not by itself establish genealogy, heredity, reproduction, or a change of evolutionary units.
- **Autopoiesis — ANALOGY ONLY.** Organizational reconstruction despite material turnover is a useful analogy for realization change. Autopoietic closure alone neither supplies population genealogy nor warrants evolutionary continuity across hereditary or selectable units.
- **Deep learning — NONE.** Representation change and parameter continuity do not supply the ancestry, reproduction, heredity, or selection relations needed here. Q024's generalization analysis is not reopened.

## 26. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### KNOWN

- Major transitions can be gradual intervals in which lower- and higher-level Darwinian populations coexist.
- Heredity, reproduction, and selection can have different units, and major-transition classifications include non-hierarchical inheritance changes.
- Reproduction with material overlap, reconstitution of traits, reticulate genealogy, and life-cycle closure supply different scientifically grounded bridge relations.
- Fitness is not a conserved substance transferred between levels, and Price decompositions do not justify their own mappings.
- Reversions and symbiogenesis rule out monotonic, one-carrier histories.
- A persistent universal unit is unnecessary; bare historical causation is insufficient.

### UNDEREXPLORED

- Comparative empirical protocols for validating several partial bridge relations in the same transition, especially when hereditary, reproductive, and selectable roles change at different times.
- Historical reconstruction of which claims should remain undefined when trait semantics, channels, or contributors change in reticulate transitions.

These are validation and comparative-method questions, not evidence for a new continuity mechanism.

### POTENTIALLY NOVEL

- None justified. The partial-mapping schema only compresses established relational theories and adds no independent dynamical claim.

### UNKNOWN

- For many historical transitions, which specific contributors and reconstructive relations actually carried evolutionary effects across the interval.
- How much disagreement among alternative domain-valid coarse-grainings can be reduced by available historical evidence.

## 27. Hypothesis decision

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NO HYPOTHESIS JUSTIFIED}}
\]

The conclusions that units change, levels overlap, traits can be reconstituted, ancestry can be reticulate, and continuity is relational are prior art or theoretical classifications. They do not supply a novel falsifiable dynamical relation. `HYPOTHESES.md` therefore remains unchanged.
