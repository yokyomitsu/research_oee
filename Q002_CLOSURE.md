# Q002 — What can continue in an evolutionarily closed system?

One theoretical research pass, 2026-09-07. Question: distinguish evolutionary stopping, stagnation, convergence, and closure. No mechanism hypothesis adopted. **Fact** denotes a sourced finding or definition; **Interpretation** denotes the synthesis below; mathematical deductions state their assumptions. “Strong OEE” is not treated as a standardized class.

**Interpretation / result:** “Convergence” has been too coarse. Change can persist while a particular observable converges, while novelty at another level stops, or while a specified repertoire remains fixed. None of these observations alone establishes complete evolutionary closure. The literature supplies several precise but non-equivalent boundaries, not one accepted, observer-independent criterion for closure of evolutionary possibility.

## Compact taxonomy

**Interpretation / diagnostic vocabulary:** These are overlapping axes, not eleven mutually exclusive evolutionary regimes. Every diagnosis must name its entities/observables, environment, historical baseline, resolution, and time horizon. The population/genotype/phenotype distinctions specify *what is measured*; the remaining distinctions concern events, capacities, or dynamics.

| Proposed category | Retain, modify, or reject | What can continue despite it? |
|---|---|---|
| Population convergence | Split concentration into one/few types from convergence of a population distribution or statistic. Neither means extinction or cessation of births. | Replacement of individuals, mutation, and turnover beneath stationary aggregate statistics. |
| Genotypic convergence | Specify fixation, declining genetic diversity, or stable genotype frequencies; these are different claims. Fixation at an instant is not an absorbing state if mutation remains possible. | Reproduction and subsequent substitutions; environmentally induced phenotypic differences. |
| Phenotypic convergence | Specify the trait or behavior and tolerance. Convergence of size is much weaker than convergence of all functions. | Neutral genotype change, unmeasured functional change, or ever-smaller improvements in the measured trait. Evoloops already demonstrate the size/function distinction (K004). |
| Adaptive stagnation | Separate no beneficial replacements, no net fitness gain under a specified comparison, and a declining improvement rate. | Drift, cyclic readaptation, or adaptation to changing opponents without a rising global fitness score. |
| Novelty stagnation | Retain only relative to a novelty criterion and history: genotype, phenotype, function, interaction, etc. | Rediscovery and replacement among familiar alternatives; novelty at another level. |
| Complexity stagnation | Retain as boundedness or a plateau of a named complexity measure, with organism/ecosystem/history distinguished. | New adaptations and different organizations at comparable measured complexity. |
| Ecological stagnation | Split stable abundance/diversity from absence of new ecological functions or interaction types. | Changing occupants of established roles; conversely, a constant species count need not imply constant roles. |
| Loss of evolvability | Retain as a capacity claim, not a synonym for an observed pause: which heritable responses become inaccessible under which conditions? | Current adaptation, persistence, or variation in other directions. Partial loss is not total loss. |
| Recurrence | Separate revisiting an observed state from repeating the entire future trajectory. Exact full-state return guarantees the latter for autonomous deterministic dynamics; it does not do so in general. | Stochastic revisits with different futures; repeating local processes within a changing larger system. |
| Saturation of accessible states | Split exhaustion of a fixed repertoire from becoming trapped in a subset. Neither follows from visiting many states. State whether “accessible” means in one mutation or through arbitrary viable paths. | Turnover within the exhausted repertoire, or missed novelty outside the occupied subset. |
| Closure of evolutionary possibility | Retain as a question requiring a specified boundary and reachability claim. Reject its use as an already standardized scalar diagnosis. | Arbitrarily extensive exploration can remain inside a fixed descriptive vocabulary; whether this excludes OEE depends on the definition. |

The axes are motivated by [Bedau et al.'s activity classification](https://sfi-edu.s3.amazonaws.com/sfi-edu/production/uploads/sfi-com/dev/uploads/filer/b3/e9/b3e9317b-5798-485b-8bb1-3465cd336e0e/98-03-025.pdf), [MODES](https://cse.msu.edu/~dolsonem/pdfs/modes_paper.pdf), and [Taylor's novelty classification](https://www.tim-taylor.com/papers/taylor2019evolutionary.preprint.pdf); the table is a working synthesis, not a taxonomy attributed to any one paper. The capacity reading of evolvability follows the distinction between response to directional selection and richer evolutionary capacities in [Fernando, Kampis & Szathmáry (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2011.12.023).

**Interpretation:** Reserve “stopped” for a specified process, such as extinction of all hereditary lineages. Reserve “stagnant” for an observation over an interval. “Convergent” requires a variable and a limiting behavior. “Closed” requires an argument about possible continuations. An unvisited possibility is not necessarily reachable, and a reachable possibility is not necessarily likely to be realized.

## Counterexamples that determine the boundary

| Evidence status and system | What continues | What it fails to establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact:** Echo, Bedau et al. (1998), original class 2 | Positive new adaptive activity with bounded diversity and mean cumulative activity. | Their unbounded class. This is an explicit distinction between bounded evolution and absent adaptation. [Primary study, classification and results](https://sfi-edu.s3.amazonaws.com/sfi-edu/production/uploads/sfi-com/dev/uploads/filer/b3/e9/b3e9317b-5798-485b-8bb1-3465cd336e0e/98-03-025.pdf) |
| **Fact:** Echo's neutral shadow | Births, deaths, genotype turnover, and newly assigned genotype identities. | Adaptive significance of those identities. It supplies a weak-novelty control, not a model of qualitative innovation. [Same study, neutral shadow](https://sfi-edu.s3.amazonaws.com/sfi-edu/production/uploads/sfi-com/dev/uploads/filer/b3/e9/b3e9317b-5798-485b-8bb1-3465cd336e0e/98-03-025.pdf) |
| **Fact about the authors' analysis:** Tierra | New individuals and strains after distinctive ecological novelty plateaus. | Continued higher-level innovation in that analysis; not an impossibility theorem for all Tierra variants. [Banzhaf et al. (2016), pp. 152–153](https://www.cs.mun.ca/~banzhaf/papers/OEE_2016.pdf) |
| **Fact:** NK fitness-sharing experiments | Sustained change and high ecological diversity. | Final novelty increased by fitness sharing; authors interpret turnover as cycling. [Dolson et al. (2019), §§5.1–5.4](https://cse.msu.edu/~dolsonem/pdfs/modes_paper.pdf) |
| **Fact:** Avida in fixed experimental task environments | Long fitness improvement trajectories. | Unlimited adaptation or new task domains. Wiser's power-law fits are extrapolations, not infinity proofs. [Wiser (2015), chapter 4](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/3694) |

**Fact:** Evolutionary-activity counters can also grow through stabilizing selection preserving existing components. MODES discusses this “uncreative” case and remaining classification ambiguities. Its persistence filter can admit drift. Its four dimensions—change, novelty, complexity, ecology—are measurements, not a complete OEE certificate. [Dolson et al., §§2–3](https://cse.msu.edu/~dolsonem/pdfs/modes_paper.pdf)

**Fact / caution in the opposite direction:** Avida continuations exceeded asymptotes fitted to earlier data. Thus apparent closure can be an observational error. The study concerns fitness projections, not demonstrated expansion of functional possibilities. [Wiser et al. (2018), *The Boundedness Illusion*, preprint](https://peerj.com/preprints/27246.pdf)

## What the definitions actually keep open

**Fact:** The York workshop explicitly endorses multiple kinds of OEE. It separates continuing novelty/adaptation, new evolutionary entities, major transitions, and evolution of evolvability. Complexity requirements vary. It does not endorse one uniquely correct definition. [Taylor et al. (2016), §3](https://doi.org/10.1162/ARTL_A_00210)

| Framework | Relevant boundary and limitation |
|---|---|
| Evolutionary activity statistics | **Fact:** Distinguish absent, bounded, and unbounded adaptive activity relative to components and a neutral comparison. They are not a direct description of all possible future functions. Original class numbers differ from the later expanded classification. [Bedau et al. (1998)](https://sfi-edu.s3.amazonaws.com/sfi-edu/production/uploads/sfi-com/dev/uploads/filer/b3/e9/b3e9317b-5798-485b-8bb1-3465cd336e0e/98-03-025.pdf) |
| Combinatorial versus creative novelty | **Fact about a conceptual proposal:** Cariani distinguishes combinations of existing primitives from new primitives, and structural repertoires from functional possibilities. “Closed” combinatorics here is not topological closedness. **Interpretation:** This is direct prior art for the question, but does not provide a general computable test of future functional closure. [Cariani (2008), *Design Strategies for Open-Ended Evolution*](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=da9657ac7d0a53c283c364d38aad5a925e7656d1&repid=rep1&type=pdf) |
| Model-relative novelty | **Fact:** Banzhaf et al. distinguish variation within a model, innovation changing it, and emergence changing its meta-model. They reserve open-ended events for the latter two. [Banzhaf et al. (2016), novelty definitions](https://www.cs.mun.ca/~banzhaf/papers/OEE_2016.pdf) |
| Exploratory, expansive, transformational OEE | **Fact:** Taylor additionally accepts sustained adaptive exploration as effective OEE. Classification depends on the model; Taylor uses the initial reference model, unlike Banzhaf's current model. Model expansion need not expand the actual physical state space. [Taylor (2019), §§2, 4.3.1](https://www.tim-taylor.com/papers/taylor2019evolutionary.preprint.pdf) |
| Unbounded evolution plus innovation | **Fact:** Adams et al. compare a subsystem with equivalent isolated systems. UE exceeds the isolated recurrence horizon; INN uses trajectories unavailable to those isolated systems. These concern dynamical evolution, not necessarily Darwinian evolution. **Interpretation:** A rigorous counterfactual trajectory boundary exists here, but it is not a general criterion for hereditary functional possibility. [Adams et al. (2017), definitions 1–2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-00810-8) |
| Algorithmic complexity | **Fact:** Corominas-Murtra et al. postulate increasing normalized history complexity, unboundedness, and a separate heredity principle. Their general version permits decreases in individual-state complexity; a stronger version constrains states themselves. **Interpretation:** This closes an information-growth question, not automatically the question of new selectable functions. [Corominas-Murtra et al. (2018), §§3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6303796/) |

**Interpretation / search boundary:** Formal accounts therefore exist, but the searched literature supplies no satisfactory unified criterion equivalent to “closure of evolutionary possibility.” The disagreement concerns both the target property and its measurement. Recent dynamical work also uses an expressly chosen operational definition rather than a consensus one; its attractor metric does not resolve the functional question here. [López-Díaz & Gershenson, 2025 preprint, introduction and metric section](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15534v1)

## Answers to the eight questions

1. **Can adaptive substitutions continue in a fixed effective domain? — Yes, with scope stated.** **Fact:** The Avida results above establish long adaptation within fixed task environments. **Fact / mathematical deduction:** On a finite genotype set with a fixed scalar fitness function, a sequence of strictly improving substitutions cannot be infinite. Changing selection can repeatedly favor old alternatives. A fixed *infinite* domain admits endlessly improving trajectories without adding trait types.

2. **Can weak novelty be indefinite while qualitative possibilities remain closed? — Yes.** **Fact / constructed counterexample:** Consider heritable integers with only successor mutations, $n\mapsto n+1$, and reproductive fitness $f(n)=1-2^{-(n+1)}$. In an idealized successive-fixation process, every accepted successor is distinct and beneficial. The one-trait vocabulary and fitness rule are fixed from the outset. This assumes unlimited representation and time; it is a logical example, not a physical experiment or a new research hypothesis. **Interpretation:** It defeats the implication from endless substitutions to new *kinds* of function, while leaving classification as exploratory OEE definition-dependent.

3. **Is long-term non-recurrence sufficient? — No for evolutionary OEE generally.** **Fact / deduction:** An integer counter never recurs and has no heredity/selection process. Even the adaptive example above stays within one predefined task. Non-recurrence of exact values can coexist with their convergence to a limit. Adams's qualified dynamical definition must not be substituted for biological OEE.

4. **Is continuing adaptation sufficient? — Not across definitions.** **Interpretation:** Repeated readaptation can lack historical novelty. Continual *adaptive novelty* can satisfy an exploratory definition without satisfying a demand for expansive or transformational innovation. Adaptation must specify its comparison environment; opponent-dependent success need not be globally ordered.

5. **Is continuing phenotypic novelty sufficient? — Only under a suitably qualified definition.** **Interpretation:** New measured values alone need not be adaptive or introduce functions. Even adaptive phenotypic novelty can be exploratory. A phenotype description that omits interactions can miss consequential novelty; an excessively fine description can count inconsequential differences.

6. **Is complexity growth required? — Definition-dependent.** **Fact:** The algorithmic account requires specified complexity growth; Adams's definitions do not require increasing state complexity. **Interpretation:** Neither monotonic genome-length growth nor increasing mean organism complexity is a universal requirement supported here. A complexity plateau diagnoses only the chosen measure and level.

7. **Is evolutionary-possibility closure already rigorous? — In partial, different senses.** **Interpretation:** There are statistical bounds, trajectory counterfactuals, and model-relative novelty classes. No equivalence between them is established by the sources reviewed. An observed absence of novelty is not a proof that no admissible continuation can produce it.

8. **What separates a long trajectory from an open-ended process?** **Interpretation:** Length describes a realized history. OEE adds a specified claim about sustained production or capacity for a chosen class of evolutionary outcomes. The claim must survive controls for recycling, neutral novelty, measurement artifacts, and finite observation. A single innovation demonstrates neither indefinite capacity nor repeated realization.

## What closure is incompatible with OEE?

**Interpretation / conditional answer:** Permanent inability to generate further instances of the novelty or growth required by the chosen OEE definition is incompatible with that definition. For strict historical novelty in a fixed finite repertoire, exhausting the repertoire suffices. For expansive/transformational claims, restriction to exploratory variation fails their defining requirement. For complexity-based claims, a proved bound on the required complexity fails that requirement. These are conditional exclusions, not an explanatory theory of OEE.

**Fact / deduction:** Set invariance alone is insufficient: an invariant set can be infinite and contain endlessly distinct outcomes. A stationary distribution can support perpetual stochastic transitions. A finite autonomous deterministic system eventually repeats its full state, but a stochastic state revisit need not repeat its future. A fixed finite resource bound also does not settle *effective* OEE (K008, K012).

**Interpretation:** Therefore replace “the system converges” with a statement such as “size stabilizes, selected genotypes continue replacing one another, and continued novel ecological functions have not been demonstrated.” For Evoloops, the last clause remains an evidential limit, not proof of ecological or evolutionary closure.
