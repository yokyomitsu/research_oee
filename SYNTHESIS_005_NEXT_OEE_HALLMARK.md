## 1. Why a new hallmark synthesis is required

**Fact.** [Synthesis 004](SYNTHESIS_004_AFN_HALLMARK_PROGRAM.md) concluded **S1 — AFN METHOD PHASE COMPLETE, EXTERNAL VALIDATION PENDING**. Stringmol remains the strongest reviewed finite repeated-AFN case with two accepted ordered events; Avida and Geb each support one in their strongest reviewed continuous histories; and no reviewed case establishes ongoing functional-class expansion. The event-history method is conditionally validated, the functional-class comparison is structured under frozen \((K,Q,g)\), and the independent-coding protocol is ready for a pilot.

**Fact.** The missing AFN resource is not another internal formalization. It is at least two scheme-independent, sufficiently domain-literate coders applying identical blinded packets before adjudication. That resource is unavailable. M4 ongoingness inference is also postponed because the strongest history contains only two accepted events and cannot discriminate plausible continuing, saturating, or long-transient event processes.

**Interpretation.** AFN has therefore reached a legitimate pause boundary rather than a theoretical failure:

\[
\boxed{
\text{AFN hallmark program}
=
\text{PAUSED — EXTERNAL INDEPENDENT VALIDATION REQUIRED}
}
\]

The field problem remains open, so the next useful move is to select a different behavioral hallmark whose evidence can be localized before a mechanism is proposed.

## 2. Current global OEE research state

The central problem remains:

> Why do artificial evolutionary systems usually converge or close, while natural evolution appears capable of continually generating new evolutionary possibilities?

The current state is:

- **Closed as general mechanism families:** endogenous organizational-type creation (F1), recurrent regime transformation (F2), unconditional broad-scope evolvability (F3), self-maintaining plasticity (F4), and ecological construction of new selection structure (F5).
- **Closed as an independent conceptual frontier:** continuity across changing evolutionary units; use domain-specific ancestry, reproduction, reconstruction, life-cycle, or selected-contribution relations.
- **Paused:** ongoing adaptive functional novelty, pending genuine external independent validation.
- **Empirically blocked or deferred:** Fernandes collective heredity, snowflake matched causal equivalence, and the Evoloop plateau diagnosis.
- **Open at hallmark-selection scale:** ongoing complexity growth, transformational novelty, and the narrow relational reading of semantic evolution.
- **No active mechanism hypothesis:** [HYPOTHESES.md](HYPOTHESES.md) remains empty.

**Interpretation.** The repository has closed a mechanism map, not the OEE field. A new hallmark program must first establish the phenomenon and its evidential boundary.

## 3. Field hallmark taxonomy

**Fact.** The York synthesis separates behavioral hallmarks from mechanisms and recognizes ongoing adaptive novelty and ongoing complexity growth, with complexity divided at least into entity complexity and interaction complexity. It also discusses new evolutionary entities, major transitions, and evolution of evolvability without making them one natural kind. [Taylor et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1162/ARTL_A_00210)

**Fact.** The 2019 Tokyo overview groups the ongoing production of interesting new entities and interactions as Type 1, ongoing evolution of evolvability as Type 2, ongoing major transitions as Type 3, and semantic evolution as Type 4, while explicitly noting that semantic evolution might be a special case of Type 1. [Packard et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00291)

**Fact.** Taylor's exploratory, expansive, and transformational categories classify novelty relative to the descriptive phenotype/behavior model: exploratory novelty remains within the model, expansive novelty expands its states, and transformational novelty requires changing the model's descriptive dimensions or relations. The proposed routes include multiple behavioral domains, transdomain bridges, and non-additive composition. [Taylor 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00290)

**Fact.** The 2024 editorial returns to two especially central behavioral hallmarks—ongoing adaptive novelty of different kinds and ongoing complexity growth—and reports more agreement about hallmarks than about their generating requirements or mechanisms. The Type 1 procedure treats adaptive novelty and complexity growth jointly but measures them through distinct analyses. [Channon et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_e_00445), [Channon 2024](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00430)

**Interpretation.** The taxonomy is overlapping rather than a flat list:

| Field category | Best classification here | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Ongoing adaptive novelty | Primary behavioral hallmark | Directly describes an evolutionary history; currently paused as AFN. |
| Ongoing complexity growth | Primary behavioral hallmark | Directly describes growth of a declared entity or interaction property and is not entailed by novelty. |
| Exploratory / expansive / transformational novelty | Novelty subtypes | They classify how novelty relates to a descriptive model; transformational novelty can support a focused evidence program. |
| Evolution of evolvability / Tokyo Type 2 | Behavioral hallmark and possible enabling process, depending the claim | The ongoing history is a hallmark; a particular evolvability change may be a mechanism for another outcome. F3's unconditional mechanism reading is closed. |
| Major transitions / Tokyo Type 3 | Behavioral hallmark and family of historical processes | Ongoing transitions are an evidential pattern; transition-specific causes are mechanisms. F2's universal recurrence-mechanism reading is closed. |
| Semantic evolution / Tokyo Type 4 | Specialized relational hallmark, possibly overlapping Type 1 | Its distinct object is historical change in meaning relations, not semantic quantity or generic function. |
| Evolved open-endedness | Explanatory perspective | It asks how conditions enabling openness become evolutionary outcomes; it does not itself name one hallmark or recurrence mechanism. |

This synthesis therefore does not treat every named category as an independent candidate.

## 4. What is unavailable

The following are unavailable for selection or reactivation:

1. **AFN:** paused until external independent validation is supplied or the research strategy is explicitly changed.
2. **F2 / Tokyo Type 3 as a mechanism program:** ongoing major transitions remain a possible behavioral description, but no universal recurrence mechanism is required. A new Type 3 program would need a genuinely evidence-only question distinct from Q026; no such higher-information question beats the candidates below.
3. **F3 / Tokyo Type 2 as a general mechanism program:** unconditional broad-scope evolvability is incoherent without shared structure, current effects, descendant association, or a declared cross-regime relation. The hallmark does not justify reopening Q023–Q024.
4. **F4:** maintenance plus transformability is already distributed across closure, regulation, robustness, plasticity, development, and transition theory.
5. **F5:** niche construction, ecological inheritance, ecosystem engineering, opportunity/fitting, coevolution, and interaction/dependency theory absorb its general reading.
6. **Organizational individuality change:** Q013–Q027 already decomposed morphology, reproduction, heredity, individuality, autonomy, identity, and continuity; the remaining Fernandes and snowflake questions require unavailable empirical work.
7. **Blocked empirical branches:** they remain provenance-bearing controls and cannot be converted into new theoretical programs merely by changing terminology.

## 5. Candidate hallmark set

Exactly three candidates survive initial screening.

### H1 — Ongoing complexity growth

Sustained evolutionary production of increasingly complex entity or interaction organization under a declared, domain-appropriate complexity object. Raw size, arbitrary accumulation, and formal capacity are insufficient.

### H2 — Transformational novelty

A historical sequence in which a previously adequate, predeclared phenotype/behavior model and meta-model become insufficient because the evolved phenomenon requires a new descriptive concept, dimension, relation type, or behavioral domain at the declared granularity. A new model instance built from concepts already available in the frozen meta-model is expansive rather than transformational novelty.

### H3 — Semantic evolution

Historical change in the context-dependent meaning relation of an existing signal, tag, symbol, component, or relation—most clearly, change in what other signs or contexts make it signify—rather than merely the origin of relevance or a new causal function.

No fourth candidate is added. Ongoing evolution of evolvability and ongoing major transitions remain legitimate field categories, but selecting either would presently revisit the closed F3 or F2 neighborhood with less independent evidence value. Organizational individuality is already processed. A fourth label would therefore inflate rather than clarify the candidate space.

## 6. Complexity-growth audit

### What exactly can grow?

**Fact / established distinction.** Complexity is not one object. At minimum, the evidence may concern:

| Complexity object | Evidential attraction | Principal failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Genome or program complexity | Often directly archived; description length and executed instruction structure can be measured. | Neutral padding, unused code, redundancy, and encoding dependence. |
| Phenotypic or morphological complexity | Reconnects organization to realized bodies and dynamics. | Segmentation, boundary, scale, and observer choices; elaborate shape need not be adaptive. |
| Organizational complexity | Targets differentiated, coordinated, causally active parts or constraints. | Can collapse into domain-specific identity, closure, or function unless the measured relation is declared. |
| Behavioral complexity | Can capture richer policies, coordination, or response repertoires. | Can become an observer-chosen task list or duplicate AFN if every new behavior is treated as a new function. |
| Interaction or network complexity | Covers ecological, social, regulatory, or molecular relations even when individual internals remain stable. | Node/edge definitions, inactive links, and network-size effects can trivialize growth. |
| Individual complexity | Matches the York maximum-entity question and Geb's strongest quantitative result. | Mean, maximum, and modal complexity answer different questions; larger individuals need not be more organized. |
| Ecological or population complexity | Captures differentiated roles and interaction structure beyond one individual. | Diversity, abundance, and network size are not automatically complexity or adaptation. |

**Interpretation.** Metric plurality does not defeat the hallmark. The reusable AFN lesson is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{common logical evidence structure}
\neq
\text{one universal metric}
}
\]

A complexity program can compare system-specific objects through common questions: what bearer or interaction is measured, which components or relations are causally active, whether the increase occurred within an evolutionary history, whether it survives a triviality audit, how resource ceilings were handled, and whether continuation rather than a single increase is supported. This is an assessment of tractability, not the completed method.

### Hintze-style triviality firewall

**Fact.** Hintze constructs a simple evolving system satisfying weak OEE requirements under chosen compression and sequence-distance measures, showing that unbounded or indefinitely increasing numerical values can certify scientifically uninteresting systems when the complexity and diversity objects are underspecified. [Hintze 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00289)

The implication rejected here is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{unbounded numerical complexity metric}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{interesting OEE}
}
\]

A viable hallmark must reject increases due only to neutral padding, genome bloat, unused code, externally enlarged limits, or arbitrary state accumulation. The program need not solve this firewall before selection, but it must make each of these a negative control rather than a possible success route.

### Geb calibration

**Fact.** Geb measures maximum individual complexity as the number of distinct active genes involved in neural development. Across parameter-scaled runs, maximum individual complexity was asymptotically bounded when either population/world scale or maximum-neuron scale was increased alone, but was indefinitely scalable to the evaluated extent when both were increased together, with logarithmic scaling in the lower limiting resource. [Channon 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00285)

Geb therefore provides a strong positive or near-positive calibration because its measure concerns active developmental components and the study explicitly separates hard ceilings. It does **not** establish:

- an unbounded trajectory in one fixed finite run;
- a universal complexity measure;
- increasing behavioral or ecological complexity;
- adaptive complexity of each added active gene;
- ongoing adaptive novelty; or
- OEE as a whole.

The four levels must remain separate:

\[
\text{scalable maximum possible complexity}
\neq
\text{observed complexity growth}
\neq
\text{adaptive complexity growth}
\neq
\text{ongoing complexity growth}.
\]

### Tokyo Type 1 and adaptive complexity

**Fact.** Tokyo Type 1 joins adaptive novelty and complexity growth, but its procedure analyzes adaptive novelty, adaptive success, diversity, complexity, and scalability separately. [Channon 2024](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00430)

**Interpretation.** Complexity can be studied independently as one Type 1 component because:

\[
\text{complexity growth}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{AFN}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{AFN}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{monotonic complexity growth}.
\]

The meaningful target should eventually exclude evolutionarily inert complexity. A promising object family is **causally active organizational or interaction complexity**, with evidence that the elaboration participates in realized behavior, development, maintenance, interaction, or reproduction. Selection, performance contribution, or persistence under selection may strengthen that attribution without requiring a new functional class. Thus an adaptive filter need not smuggle AFN back in: a lineage can elaborate the organization supporting one existing function without acquiring a new function. The exact filter is deliberately not chosen in this synthesis.

**Viability assessment:** **TRACTABLE ENOUGH FOR A FOCUSED PROGRAM.** Geb supplies a quantitative calibration, Hintze supplies a false-positive stress test, and Evoloops supply a direct warning that genome/loop length can increase against prevailing selection without establishing adaptive functional innovation. The unresolved object/metric choice is serious but can be handled through system-specific measures and common evidential questions.

## 7. Transformational-novelty audit

**Fact.** Taylor distinguishes exploratory novelty, which remains within the current model, from expansive novelty, which requires model change using concepts already present in the meta-model, and transformational novelty, which introduces a new concept and therefore requires meta-model change. Multiple behavioral domains, transdomain bridges, and non-additive compositional systems are proposed routes. [Taylor 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00290)

The distinct historical target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{evolution creates a behavior or entity for which}
\atop
\text{the prior descriptive model is insufficient}
}
\]

This is not automatically a new function, a major transition, an ontological change, substrate growth, or expansion of physical state space.

### Q006 firewall

Q006 already established adjacent-possible expansion, changing genotype–phenotype maps, new reusable primitives, compositionality, and transdomain bridges as prior art. Transformational novelty survives only as an **observable history/model relation**. If it asks how the possibility space expands, it is absorbed by the closed generative-capacity frontier.

### Q016 firewall

Q016 established that identity-bearing relations are domain-specific and that causal abstraction cannot select one universal ontology. Transformational novelty therefore cannot require a representation-independent proof that “the ontology changed.” It may still use a system-specific phenotype/behavior model whose scientific purpose, variables, relations, and resolution are declared independently.

### Observer dependence

The strongest weakness is representation dependence. A sufficiently rich model can pre-encode all later behaviors, while an impoverished model can manufacture model escape. The minimal defensible distinction is:

> Freeze a scientifically justified phenotype/behavior model and meta-model before inspecting the candidate event; exploratory novelty remains within the model, expansive novelty changes the model using an already available concept, and transformational novelty requires a new descriptive concept at the same declared resolution.

Taylor supplies the conceptual framework for such a distinction, but the reviewed literature does not yet supply an AFN-like cross-system protocol for predeclaring model adequacy and adjudicating escape. A prospective freeze is principled; retrospective surprise is not.

### Evidence availability

Stringmol constructional transitions, major transitions, new behavioral domains, transdomain bridges, and compositional systems are plausible source classes. They are not one homogeneous evidence set. Many published claims are retrospective, and several candidate cases overlap the closed F1/F2/Q006 branches. Enough cases exist for a conceptual-boundary or positive-case audit, but not yet for a strong, comparable positive/negative matrix without first stabilizing the observer-model contract.

**Viability assessment:** **PARTIAL OVERLAP BUT SURVIVES.** It is independent enough to remain the runner-up, but representation dependence and thin predeclared evidence reduce near-term information gain.

## 8. Semantic-evolution audit

**Fact.** The Tokyo overview describes semantic evolution as historical change in semantic relations. Its clearest examples are changing tag meanings through shifting tag associations in web services and changes in the meaning or importance of keywords in technological clusters; it also asks whether biochemical function might support an analogous semantics. The overview notes that semantic evolution may be a special case of Type 1. [Packard et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00291)

**Fact.** Ikegami, Hashimoto, and Oka operationalize tag meaning through the types of associated tags and report tags whose association-defined meanings change over time. Their system includes human users as a constitutive resource and does not establish an autonomous artificial evolutionary-life process. [Ikegami, Hashimoto & Oka 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00287)

### Q004 and AFN firewalls

Q004 already covers emergence of relevance, function, semantic information, viability grounding, autopoiesis, autonomy, selected effects, and changing norms. A new program cannot ask merely how meaning or relevance emerges.

AFN already compares changes in causal roles. If biochemical semantics is defined only as chemical function, a semantic program would duplicate the paused AFN framework.

The exact surviving distinction is narrower:

\[
\boxed{
\text{semantic evolution tracks reassignment of an existing sign-like}
\atop
\text{item through changing relations to contexts, associates, or interpretants}
}
\]

This relational reassignment need not create a new adaptive function, and a new adaptive function need not reassign an existing sign. Tag association profiles illustrate the former. If no independently justified sign/context relation exists, the candidate collapses into causal function or relevance.

### Domain boundary

The strongest evidence is presently cultural and technological. That evidence is legitimate OEE evidence, but it gives a weaker first program for the repository's original ALife objective. Extending the object to biochemical or artificial-life semantics is presently a suggestion, not an established cross-domain measurement practice.

**Viability assessment:** **PARTIAL OVERLAP BUT SURVIVES NARROWLY.** It is not fully absorbed, but its best evidence is domain-specialized and its biological/ALife transfer risks duplicating Q004 or AFN.

## 9. Any fourth candidate

No fourth candidate is justified.

- **Ongoing major transitions** remain a strong possible behavioral hallmark, but an evidence-only program would revisit heavily processed individuality, heredity, and continuity material and quickly encounter the same historical/instrumentation limits. Its distinction from Q026 is real—the hallmark is the sequence, not a recurrence mechanism—but its expected information gain is lower than H1–H3.
- **Ongoing evolution of evolvability** is a recognized hallmark, but Q023–Q024 already show that evidence must be conditional on a shared target structure or declared cross-regime relation. A new program would have to repeat that boundary before it could localize cases.
- **Organizational individuality change** is already decomposed and does not survive as a fresh candidate.

## 10. Pairwise independence

| Pair | Independence result |
|---|---|
| Complexity growth vs AFN | Independent. Selected structural elaboration can increase within an existing function; AFN can occur through a simple role change without monotonic complexity growth. |
| Transformational novelty vs AFN | Independent. Model escape need not be adaptive or functional; a new adaptive function can remain representable inside the old model. |
| Semantic evolution vs AFN | Partially independent. Relational reassignment of an existing sign can occur without a new adaptive role, but function-defined biochemical semantics is absorbed by AFN/Q004. |
| Complexity growth vs transformational novelty | Independent. Complex elaboration may remain inside one descriptive model; a simple new interaction type may require changing that model. |
| Complexity growth vs semantic evolution | Independent. A semantic relation can change without greater measured complexity; network or representational complexity can grow while meanings remain stable. |
| Transformational novelty vs semantic evolution | Independent. A new semantic relation need not force a new descriptive model, and new embodiment, level, or behavior-domain variables need not be semantic. |

These are non-implications, not claims that the histories cannot co-occur.

## 11. Closed-frontier overlap firewall

| Candidate | Forbidden collapse | Surviving hallmark object |
|---|---|---|
| H1 — complexity growth | F3 evolvability, F4 closure/plasticity, generic “more parts,” or AFN under a new name | Historical growth of a declared nontrivial entity- or interaction-complexity object. |

## 12. Measurement and observer-dependence comparison

**Complexity.** Metric plurality is high, but measurements can be objective conditional on a declared bearer, active-part/relation rule, scale, history, and resource ceiling. Common evidence questions can compare logical status without making scalar values commensurable. Observer dependence is serious but exposed locally.

**Transformational novelty.** Measurement depends on the adequacy of a frozen descriptive model. System-specific evidence is possible, but both over-rich and under-rich models can change the verdict. The observer/model contract is therefore constitutive of the hallmark and currently less methodologically mature.

**Semantic evolution.** Association and relational change can be quantified inside tag, language, or technological networks, but the choice of sign, context, interpretant, and meaning relation is domain-laden. Human cultural cases provide clear semantics while complicating autonomous-ALife transfer.

The common methodological possibility is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{system-specific objects}
+
\text{common evidence questions}
\not= 
\text{one universal scalar}
}
\]

It is most immediately usable for H1, plausible but unstabilized for H2, and domain-narrow for H3.

## 13. Published artificial-system evidence comparison

This section localizes published anchors only. It does not reanalyze data or construct the selected evidence matrix.

### H1 — complexity growth

- **Geb:** strongest near-positive quantitative anchor. Maximum individual active-gene complexity is indefinitely scalable to the tested extent only when population and neural ceilings are scaled jointly. This distinguishes ceiling structure, realized maxima, and scaling order. [Channon 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00285)
- **Hintze's simple system:** strongest triviality control. Weak complexity/diversity definitions can be satisfied by a scientifically uninteresting process. [Hintze 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00289)
- **Evoloops:** genome/loop-size growth under hostile modifications can oppose prevailing selection and does not establish adaptive functional innovation; this is a direct size-is-not-enough control. [Salzberg, Antony & Sayama 2004](https://bingdev.binghamton.edu/sayama/papers/BioSystems-preprint.pdf)
- **Avida:** complex logic acquisition and task switching supply bounded functional/program elaboration, but the task family is enumerated and AFN validation does not establish continuing complexity growth. [Lenski et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01568), [Zaman et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002023)
- **MODES:** supplies separable complexity, novelty, change, and ecology measurements and shows why one cannot infer one dimension from another. [Dolson et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00280)

The contrast set is heterogeneous but already capable of separating strong, weak, and trivial claims.

### H2 — transformational novelty

- **Stringmol:** changes in constructional and semantic closure are plausible model-escape candidates, but finite organization changes are not automatically transformational histories. [Clark, Hickinbotham & Stepney 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2016.1033)
- **Major-transition systems:** provide candidate changes of entity and evolutionary relation, but many are already covered by F1/F2 and may retain a phenotype model adequate at a broader level.
- **Compositional and transdomain cases:** the Taylor framework identifies routes, but published artificial cases are not yet organized under predeclared model-escape tests.

The source classes exist, but comparability is weaker and retrospective interpretation dominates.

### H3 — semantic evolution

- **Web-service tags:** association-defined tag meanings change over time in a human-mediated evolving system. [Ikegami, Hashimoto & Oka 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00287)
- **Technological clusters:** the Tokyo overview cites shifting meanings and importance of cluster keywords as another empirical semantic case. [Packard et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00291)
- **Biochemical or autonomous artificial-life semantics:** proposed as an analogy, not established as a comparable evidence base.

The positive evidence is meaningful but concentrated in human cultural/technological systems.

## 14. Current-resource tractability

### H1 — HIGH

Published quantitative measures, histories, parameter-scaling studies, and explicit negative controls permit substantial literature-only progress before independent coding or new simulation is required. A first matrix can record what authors measured and what their evidence does not establish.

### H2 — MODERATE

A conceptual-boundary pass is possible from existing publications, but decisive classification requires evidence that the descriptive model was scientifically adequate and frozen before the candidate event. Many retrospective cases do not preserve that condition. Independent analysts may eventually be required to assess model adequacy.

### H3 — MODERATE

The cultural/technological literature permits taxonomy and case localization, but progress toward autonomous ALife would soon require a justified semantic relation and likely new cross-domain analysis. Existing AFN and Q004 work already occupies much of the biological-function route.

The AFN independence blockage therefore does not recur immediately for H1, while it threatens H2 sooner.

### Mechanism postponability

- **H1 — HIGH.** Candidate systems, declared complexity bearers, within-history trends, scalability claims, and triviality controls can be compared before asking what causes complexity growth.
- **H2 — MODERATE.** A descriptive-model and meta-model boundary can be stabilized without a causal theory, but many candidate cases cannot be classified until prior model adequacy is justified.
- **H3 — MODERATE.** Relational meaning change can be localized without explaining its cause in sign-rich domains, but extension to biochemical or autonomous ALife cases quickly entangles the semantic object with function and relevance grounding.

## 17. Destructive tests

### H1 — complexity-growth destructive test

> If arbitrary bloat, enlarged resource ceilings, and unused components are excluded, does a scientifically meaningful complexity-growth hallmark remain measurable in artificial evolution?

**Answer: YES, conditionally.** Geb's active developmental-gene measure, coupled scaling analysis, and explicit maximum-individual target show that a nontrivial object remains after raw length and one-ceiling growth are excluded. More generally, causally active organization or realized interaction structure appears to be the most promising object family. The evidence must still separate within-history realized growth, scalable maxima across runs, adaptive maintenance, and ongoingness. This answer selects a program; it does not validate Geb as ongoing complexity OEE or fix the universal measure.

### H2 — transformational-novelty destructive test

> Can transformational novelty be distinguished from ordinary novelty using a predeclared descriptive model rather than retrospective surprise?

**Answer: YES in principle, but not yet with a mature cross-system method.** The minimal observable distinction is whether the candidate can be represented by the frozen model, requires only a revised model assembled from concepts already in the frozen meta-model (expansive), or instead requires a new descriptive concept and hence meta-model revision (transformational). The result remains conditional on prior model and meta-model adequacy. Because few cases preserve that prospective contract, H2 is downgraded rather than rejected.

### H3 — semantic-evolution destructive test

> Does semantic evolution identify a phenomenon not already captured by changing causal function/relevance in Q004 and AFN?

**Answer: YES, narrowly.** Reassignment of an existing sign-like item through a changed context/association/interpretant relation can occur without a new causal function or adaptive consequence. The web-tag case exemplifies this relational reading. If “semantics” is defined only as biochemical function, viability contribution, or selected effect, the answer becomes NO and the case is absorbed by Q004/AFN. The surviving target is therefore domain-narrow.
