# Q009 — Accessibility Scaling

Theoretical research pass — 2026-09-07.

Scope: whether evolutionary accessibility can remain practically viable as evolved organization becomes richer. This pass does not ask whether viable paths exist at one moment, whether a substrate is universal, or whether evolvability can evolve once.

## 1. Problem statement

**Fact.** A substrate may support arbitrarily long descriptions and a lineage may have a viable route to its next innovation, yet neither fact bounds the difficulty of later innovations. For an outcome class at organizational scale \(C\), at least four claims differ:

1. some path exists;
2. a path has nonzero probability;
3. a population reaches the class in finite expected time under specified dynamics;
4. it reaches, establishes, and retains it before extinction or relevant conditions disappear.

The first is static reachability. The latter claims add path abundance, probability, population resources, establishment, and time. Thus accessibility at one scale,

\[
x\rightsquigarrow y,
\]

does not establish that access remains non-degenerate as organizational depth increases.

**Interpretation.** “Scalable accessibility” is useful here as a question, not yet as a universal quantity. It asks whether the distribution of resource costs or first-passage times to causally relevant novelty remains compatible with the resources generated by the evolving system. It does not require constant innovation rate, monotonically increasing complexity, or a single observer-independent complexity coordinate.

## 2. Existing OEE requirements

Taylor's early formulation requires “mutational pathways of practically unlimited length” to a wide variety of future organisms; his later five-requirement account says the search space must *typically* offer paths from viable individuals to other viable, potentially fitter individuals. The paper explains that a formally large complexity range is insufficient when viable populations are repeatedly trapped at local optima. This is stronger than one existential path and qualitatively implies recurring accessibility. [Taylor 2012](https://www.tim-taylor.com/papers/taylor2012exploring.pdf), [Taylor 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07403)

Ackley's indefinite scalability removes hard architectural ceilings as spatial and computational resources increase. It says neither that evolution uses the added resources nor that innovation cost grows slowly enough to be paid. It is substrate scalability, not accessibility scaling. [Ackley 2016](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v30i1.9802)

The requested three-way comparison is therefore real, although no reviewed literature standardizes the symbols: **substrate scalability** concerns support for larger organization; **constructive capacity** concerns what suitable descriptions or constructors can realize; **evolutionary accessibility** concerns what populations can reach, establish, and retain under specified dynamics and resources. None of the first two entails the third, and this pass found no established theorem requiring accessibility to follow either one's scale relation.

OEE metrics separate change, novelty, complexity, and ecology, and OEE theory distinguishes behavioral hallmarks from generating mechanisms. The reviewed OEE metrics do not impose a complexity-conditioned lower bound on path mass or an upper bound on innovation waiting time. A cumulative novelty trace can be unbounded even when gaps between innovations grow. [Dolson et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00280), [Taylor et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1162/ARTL_A_00210)

**Boundary decision.** OEE theory already contains the *qualitative* requirement that viable routes continue. It does not isolate a resource- and scale-dependent criterion for when those routes remain practically usable. The result is candidate outcome **B — Implicit but unformalized**, not a new mechanism.

## 3. Evolutionary accessibility theory

Fitness-landscape accessibility is commonly defined for a specified genotype graph, fitness assignment, start, target, and evolutionary regime. In accessibility percolation, a target is accessible when at least one fitness-monotonic path connects it to the start. The theory studies how the probability of such a path changes with sequence length, starting fitness, graph structure, and landscape correlations. It explicitly distinguishes the expected number of paths from the probability that any path exists. [Krug 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11913), [Franke et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002134)

This is already scaling theory, but under restricted coordinates. Monotonic accessibility usually assumes strong-selection/weak-mutation dynamics. Population size, mutation supply, fixation probabilities, clonal interference, recombination, tunneling, changing environments, and evolving genotype–phenotype maps can alter actual passage. A dynamically meaningful extension weights trajectories by their transition probabilities or studies hitting-time distributions rather than graph connectivity alone.

Neutral and holey-landscape theories replace strictly uphill routes with connected viable sets. Percolation results show both possibilities: depending on viable fraction and dimension, high-dimensional genotype spaces can contain large connected viable components, while subcritical structure leaves many genotypes isolated. Correlations or epistasis can likewise preserve or destroy long-range access. The relevant object is therefore not the cardinality of the genotype space but the geometry and probability measure induced by the evolutionary dynamics. [Gavrilets & Gravner 1997](https://doi.org/10.1006/jtbi.1996.0242), [Gravner, Pitman & Gavrilets 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2007.07.009)

## 4. Complexity versus path difficulty

No general empirical law \(D(C)\) was found. Different established quantities answer different questions:

| Quantity | What it establishes | What it omits |
|---|---|---|
| shortest viable or monotonic path | minimum step count | probability and population dynamics |
| probability that at least one path exists | ensemble reachability | typical traversal time in a realized landscape |
| number or weighted mass of paths | redundancy of routes | establishment and retention of the endpoint |
| first-passage or discovery time | resource-sensitive accessibility | observer-independent choice of target novelty |
| viable-neighbor fraction / robustness | local mutational tolerance | long-range access to new functions |
| mutational covariance or GP-map bias | directions of available variation | future changes in the map itself |

Fisher-geometric analyses supply a “cost of complexity”: adaptation can slow as the number of affected traits rises. Modularity may mitigate this by limiting pleiotropic coupling, but does not erase the cost in every model. [Orr 2000](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0014-3820.2000.tb00002.x), [Welch & Waxman 2003](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0014-3820.2003.tb00581.x)

That result is not universal. A recent synthesis finds little evidence that organismal complexity by itself generally lowers evolvability and emphasizes that more dimensions can also create evolutionary opportunity. In digital organisms, selected complex genomes can be more mutation-robust than simpler ones. A multilevel toyLIFE genotype–phenotype map found that adding genes and levels of phenotypic expression increased measured robustness and evolvability. In the opposite direction, an evolving regulatory-network model found that derived, more interactive developmental programs produced common phenotypes clustered into a smaller region of phenotype space than simpler ancestral programs. These are model-specific comparisons, not a common scale law. Complexity may therefore increase, decrease, or redistribute accessibility depending on pleiotropy, architecture, population state, and the chosen phenotype description. [Houle & Rossoni 2022](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-102320-090809), [Lenski et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/23245), [Catalán et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2017.0516), [Borenstein & Krakauer 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000202)

## 5. Existence versus abundance of viable paths

Accessibility-percolation results make the requested distinction precise. If \(X_C\) counts accessible paths at scale \(C\), then

\[
\mathbb E[X_C]>0
\]

does not imply

\[
\Pr(X_C\geq 1)
\]

is appreciable. Rare landscapes with extremely many paths can dominate the expectation while a typical landscape has none. Conversely, factorial growth in the number of possible paths can sometimes overcome a rapidly declining probability per path. Models exhibit thresholds between vanishing and non-vanishing accessibility, rather than a universal monotonic effect of dimensionality. [Krug 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11913)

Even \(\Pr(X_C\geq 1)>0\) is too weak for effective evolutionary history. Hintze's deliberately simple system has mutational paths between every genotype, yet this connectivity does not establish sustained meaningful innovation. [Hintze 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00289)

**Interpretation.** Strong OEE needs more than existential reachability, but no universal positive lower bound is warranted. Any bound must name a population process, novelty class, resources, and comparison across scales.

## 6. Timescale and waiting time

Chatterjee et al. ask directly how discovery time scales with sequence length. They distinguish polynomial from exponential expected time and prove that broad gradients and even many dispersed targets can still leave innovation exponentially slow. Polynomially many parallel searches or a polynomial improvement from recombination cannot generally turn an exponential search into a polynomial one. [Chatterjee et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003818)

This gives a sharper hierarchy:

\[
\text{reachable}
\supsetneq
\text{non-negligible hitting probability}
\supsetneq
\text{reachable within available population-time}.
\]

Ecological accessibility adds a deadline: the lineage and enabling environment must persist long enough, and the innovation must establish once produced. There is no context-free deadline. The defensible quantity is a hitting-time or hazard distribution conditioned on population size, mutation supply, demography, environment, and endpoint criterion—not an arbitrary finite observation window.

OEE studies measure novelty or evolutionary activity through time, but this pass found no established OEE test of waiting-time growth conditional on increasing organizational depth. Diverging gaps do not prove mathematical termination; they can nevertheless make formal infinity biologically or computationally ineffective.

## 7. Neutral networks / robustness / modularity

Neutral networks can preserve a phenotype while exposing diverse one-step phenotypes, and high-dimensional spaces can percolate despite sparse viability. Robustness can therefore support exploration rather than oppose it. But three scaling claims must not be conflated:

- a current genotype has many viable neighbors;
- its current phenotype occupies a large connected genotype network;
- progressively richer phenotype classes continue to have large, mutually reachable viable neighborhoods.

The first two are routinely measured. The third has been probed in fixed genotype–phenotype maps and small cross-size comparisons, but not as an indefinite lineage-level property. Fortuna et al.'s Avida map shows why the statistic matters: as focal phenotype complexity rises, a point mutation is more likely to produce *some* novel phenotype, yet complex phenotypes are rarer and less robust, and the total number of distinct novel phenotypes reachable is greatest for simple focal phenotypes. By contrast, toyLIFE's two- versus three-gene comparison found increasing robustness and evolvability. Local novelty probability, reachable repertoire, robustness, and their scale trends need not agree. [Fortuna et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005414), [Catalán et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2017.0516)

Modularity, robustness, degeneracy, developmental bias, and facilitated variation can enlarge or reshape accessible variation. Existing results establish local or task-family improvements, not a theorem that they compensate for every increase in organizational or search complexity. The empirical relationship between complexity and evolvability remains unsettled. [Gerhart & Kirschner 1997](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.94.16.8420), [Houle & Rossoni 2022](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-102320-090809)

## 8. Evolution of evolvability

The question substantially overlaps evolution of evolvability, but is not identical to every use of that term. A higher mutation rate, broader one-generation phenotype distribution, or one evolved GP-map improvement can raise current evolvability and still saturate. Tokyo Type 2 requires ongoing evolution of evolvability behaviorally, but does not itself specify the mechanism or the scale/resource relation that makes it ongoing.

The stronger phrasing is also prior art. Turney proposed indefinitely increasing evolvability and predicted accelerating evolutionary pace. His implemented fixed-length model approached an equilibrium near its representational limit; indefinite increase with variable genome length was proposed rather than demonstrated. The result shows both that long-run scaling has been asked and that a one-coordinate evolvability trend does not settle organizational OEE. [Turney 1999](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0212042)

Watson and Pollack explicitly use **scalable evolvability**. In their scale-invariant hierarchical landscape, mutation and a conventional evolutionary algorithm become increasingly ineffective, while composition assembles already adapted entities and recursively reuses composites at the next level. This is direct prior art for a mechanism that compensates for increasing search difficulty. It is an existence result in a designed abstract problem, not a necessary condition for OEE or evidence that endogenous evolution will repeatedly invent the composition operator. [Watson & Pollack 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0303-2647(02)00135-1)

Therefore “accessibility must scale” is not a new hypothesis. What remains is how to test that property across changing representations, not whether evolvability matters.

## 9. Search burden from expanding possibility spaces

An expanding possibility space has two opposed effects. It can add targets and alternate paths, but it can also dilute viable or valuable targets and increase coordinated search. Landscape theory shows no monotonic implication from \(\lvert\Omega\rvert\) to accessibility:

- combinatorial growth in possible paths can overcome small per-path probability in some ensembles;
- in other ensembles the probability of any long accessible path tends to zero, sometimes exponentially;
- more targets do not ensure efficient discovery when targets are too sparse or badly distributed;
- multiple alleles or neutral detours can change the threshold, showing that graph architecture matters more than raw cardinality.

[Franke et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002134), [Krug 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11913), [Chatterjee et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003818)

Thus expansion of the adjacent possible is neither automatically a search burden nor automatically increased opportunity. The balance is distributional: target density and organization, path correlations, transition probabilities, and available population-time.

There is also a second ambiguity: search hardness need not end adaptive activity. Kaznatcheev constructs finite landscape families on which reaching even a local optimum requires superpolynomial time and argues that this can sustain effectively open-ended fitness improvement by postponing equilibrium. The same intractability is a failure of accessibility if the target is a specified innovation, but a source of prolonged adaptive steps if the target is closure at an optimum. Thus waiting-time growth must be conditioned on *what* is awaited; slow optimization is not by itself either OEE or closure. The hardness results are worst-case constructions, not evidence that natural landscapes are typically hard. [Kaznatcheev 2019](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.119.302000)

## 10. Cumulative accessibility transformation

Several existing theories instantiate innovations that alter later search rather than merely adding one outcome:

- **Hierarchical composition:** pre-adapted entities become reusable components, and composites can become components at the next level. In Watson and Pollack's model this avoids the worsening performance of mutation on a scale-invariant hierarchical problem. [Watson & Pollack 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0303-2647(02)00135-1)
- **Regeneration near prior solutions:** Chatterjee et al. show that duplication or rearrangement can repeatedly provide starting sequences a bounded mutational distance from a target. Related past solutions can thereby change exponential discovery time to polynomial time under the model's assumptions. [Chatterjee et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003818)
- **Learned developmental bias:** evolving regulatory networks can internalize regularities shared among past selective environments and bias future variation toward unseen fit phenotypes. This helps only when future problems share learnable structure; arbitrary or unrelated landscapes need not become easier. [Kouvaris et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005358)

These are strong cases F/G from the requested counterexample search. They establish that prior innovations or evolved architectures *can* preserve accessibility across a task family. They do not establish a general endogenous tendency, unlimited applicability, continued maintenance, or protection from later lock-in. Facilitated variation, potentiation, exaptation, cumulative evolution, and major transitions remain the broader prior-art families; “cumulative accessibility transformation” should not be promoted as a renamed principle.

Biological innovations fit this interpretation only as evidence. Genome duplication and recombination can create nearby variants or rearrange existing material; regulatory and developmental modularity can reuse conserved components; multicellularity can change the unit of selection and construction; learning and cultural transmission can add within-lifetime or social routes for retaining useful change. Each can transform accessibility, but each can also introduce coordination costs, entrenchment, loss, or finite task-family limits. Historical examples therefore do not establish a general scaling law. [Wagner & Altenberg 1996](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1996.tb02339.x), [Gerhart & Kirschner 2007](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0701035104), [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112)

## 11. Counterexamples

### A — Unbounded genotype space, diverging innovation time

Sequence spaces can be extended with length while target discovery remains exponentially slow in length, even with broad gradients or many random targets. Formal capacity and nonzero probability therefore coexist with effective closure on feasible timescales. [Chatterjee et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003818)

This is an asymptotic family of increasing-length search problems, not a documented variable-length OEE lineage that stagnates; it establishes the logical capacity/accessibility separation.

### B — Large neutral networks, no guarantee of continuing novelty

A large neutral component guarantees movement among genotypes of the current phenotype, not an unbounded repertoire of reachable phenotype classes. Phenotype bias can make richer phenotypes increasingly rare, and a finite phenotype catalogue can exhaust despite extensive neutral genotype motion. [Fortuna et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005414)

### C — Higher local novelty probability, smaller reachable repertoire

In Avida, increasing focal phenotype complexity raises the probability that a point mutation yields some novel phenotype, while simple phenotypes have the greatest total number of distinct reachable phenotypes. “Evolvability increases” can reverse when the statistic changes from novelty probability to repertoire size. [Fortuna et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005414)

### D — Complexity can shrink viable neighborhoods, but not universally

In Fortuna et al.'s fixed Avida map, phenotype complexity correlates with a larger inviable-mutant fraction and lower phenotype-preserving robustness. Fisher-style models likewise predict a cost under stated assumptions. But selected complex digital organisms in another experiment were more mutation-robust than simpler ones, and broad empirical support for a universal cost is lacking. Shrinking neighborhoods are a real model-specific failure mode, not a general law. [Fortuna et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005414), [Lenski et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/23245), [Houle & Rossoni 2022](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-102320-090809)

### E — Scalable substrate, non-scalable evolutionary access

Indefinite substrate scalability and universal construction impose no discovery-time bound. Exponentially difficult sequence-space examples supply the missing negative witness: arbitrarily larger objects may be supportable while evolution cannot locate the required organization with feasible resources.

### F — Prior structures make later innovation easier

Regeneration from duplicated/rearranged versions of related solutions can keep target distance bounded and change discovery-time scaling; evolved GP maps can generalize shared constraints. Both effects are conditional on reusable structure in the problem family. [Chatterjee et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003818), [Kouvaris et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005358)

### G — Roughly preserved access across hierarchical depth

Watson and Pollack's recursive composition model maintains innovation performance through successive levels on its designed scale-invariant landscape, where ordinary mutation worsens. It is the clearest existence proof found for scalable evolvability, but not for spontaneous or general OEE. Separately, toyLIFE shows that adding a gene and additional organizational levels can increase rather than degrade local robustness and evolvability. That comparison spans only very small fixed maps and does not demonstrate an evolving lineage with indefinitely preserved access. [Watson & Pollack 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0303-2647(02)00135-1), [Catalán et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2017.0516)

## 12. Observer dependence

There is no representation-free \(A(C)\). Complexity may mean sequence length, number of traits, minimum description length, functional depth, organizational level, or something else. Novel outcomes may be genotypes, phenotypes, ecological roles, selectable units, or causal organizations. Fortuna et al.'s opposite trends for novelty probability and reachable-repertoire size demonstrate that this choice is substantive rather than cosmetic.

A defensible accessibility-scaling claim must therefore specify:

1. entities and novelty equivalence classes;
2. why distinctions are causally relevant to reproduction, viability, selection, or organization;
3. mutation/development/ecology/population dynamics;
4. the resource and time normalization;
5. how classes are reconstructed when the GP map, inheritance system, or selectable level changes.

This prevents a universal scalar but does not make the question incoherent. Target-relative hitting probabilities and first-passage distributions are useful once those commitments are explicit. Candidate outcome **D — Incoherent** is therefore rejected.

## 13. Remaining explanatory gap

The narrow gap is not the absence of viable pathways, evolvability, neutral networks, modularity, or known mechanisms that sometimes improve later search. It is the absence of a general cross-level test that distinguishes:

\[
\text{an infinite but increasingly negligible tail of reachable novelty}
\]

from

\[
\text{continued, resource-feasible access to causally new organization},
\]

when the GP map, heredity, ecological context, and selectable units can themselves change.

Landscape theory supplies scale-dependent path probabilities and first-passage times for fixed representations. OEE theory supplies qualitative continuing-path and ongoing-evolvability requirements. Scalable-evolvability models supply conditional existence proofs. No reviewed framework combines these into a representation-robust, endogenous, resource-normalized criterion across major organizational changes.

The abstract deep-learning analogy adds only the already-established separation between representational capacity and effective optimization. It suggests using search-complexity, hitting-time, and resource scaling to keep capacity distinct from accessibility; it supplies no biological mechanism and does not resolve how novelty classes should be defined.

This is a **measurement/theoretical gap**, not a new scientific hypothesis.

Among the candidate outcomes, **B — Implicit but unformalized** best fits the evidence. A is too strong because no general OEE scaling criterion is established; C overstates distinctness because continuing pathways, scalable evolvability, and scale-dependent search difficulty are prior art; D is too strong because target-relative probabilistic and first-passage measures are coherent and useful.

## 14. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### Known / ESTABLISHED

- OEE requires more than a large possibility space; Taylor explicitly requires recurring viable mutational routes and earlier described paths of practically unlimited length.
- Path existence, path count, existence probability, weighted trajectory probability, and expected discovery time are different properties.
- Accessibility can vanish or persist as genotype dimension grows, depending on landscape and graph structure.
- Search time can be exponential despite gradients, many targets, or polynomial parallelism.
- Search hardness can obstruct a specified innovation yet postpone arrival at an optimum; it is not intrinsically a closure mechanism.
- Neutrality, modularity, robustness, composition, duplication, and evolved developmental bias can improve accessibility under specified conditions.
- “Scalable evolvability” and indefinitely increasing evolvability are prior-art formulations.
- Increasing complexity does not have an established universal effect on evolvability.

### Underexplored

- Complexity-conditioned innovation waiting-time or hazard scaling in OEE experiments.
- Resource-normalized comparison of accessibility before and after endogenous changes in GP maps, inheritance, ecology, or individuality.
- Whether known accessibility-improving mechanisms continue to compensate as organizational depth grows, rather than delivering one gain or a finite hierarchy.

### Potentially Novel

No scientific claim from this pass qualifies. A cross-level measurement framework combining causal novelty classes with resource-normalized first-passage distributions may be methodologically new after a dedicated methods search, but absence of a unified metric is not a hypothesis.

### Unknown

- Whether natural evolution exhibits a persistent scale relation between innovation difficulty and endogenously available evolutionary resources.
- Whether any fixed-microphysics artificial system maintains non-degenerate accessibility across genuinely changing organizational levels.
- Whether a representation-robust comparison remains possible when evolution changes the entities and transformations being compared.

## 15. Hypothesis decision

**NO HYPOTHESIS JUSTIFIED**

The proposed necessity of scalable accessibility is already implicit in recurring-path OEE requirements and explicit in scalable-evolvability prior art. The literature supports several conditional mechanisms and counterexamples but no nontrivial universal relation or falsifiable prediction beyond those theories. The surviving issue is measurement and theory integration; it does not pass the hypothesis firewall.

## 16. Highest-information next question

Can resource-normalized first-passage distributions over causally grounded novelty classes remain comparable across endogenous changes in genotype–phenotype maps, inheritance systems, and selectable levels?
