# Q020 — Fernandes collective individuality

Theoretical research pass, 2026-09-08. Scope: validation of how far the Fernandes–Vroomans–Colizzi system progresses from collective life-cycle reproduction toward a reproductively autonomous higher-level Darwinian process. This pass uses published theory and the reported model results only. It does not run or modify the model, reopen Q019's broad reproduction-domain decision, search for an OEE mechanism, or perform Q021.

Epistemic convention: source claims and reported measurements are **Fact**. Cross-framework classifications are **Interpretation**. The genealogy-ablation exercise is a **Thought experiment**, not evidence. No new scientific **Hypothesis** is introduced.

## 1. Research question

> Does the Fernandes–Vroomans–Colizzi system exhibit a genuine collective genealogy with heritable collective traits and sufficient reproductive autonomy to support higher-level Darwinian evolution?

The ordered target is:

\[
\text{reproduction}
\rightarrow
\text{genealogy}
\rightarrow
\text{heritability}
\rightarrow
\text{reproductive autonomy}
\rightarrow
\text{Darwinian individuality}.
\]

Q019 already established the first term at the broad collective-life-cycle level. Q020 asks where the published evidence stops among the remaining terms.

## 2. Independent theoretical criteria

The criteria are fixed from prior theory before returning to Fernandes.

| Claim | Independent criterion | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **A — collective recurrence** | A collective organization appears at more than one time. | Causal descent, multiplication, or inheritance. |
| **B — collective multiplication** | A collective causally produces additional candidate collective instances. Doulcier, Takacs, and Bourrat represent this as a fertility transition inside a closed stage graph. | A stable pedigree, trait fidelity, or higher-level selection. |
| **C — collective genealogy** | For collectives \(C_i\) and \(C_j\), \(C_j\) follows \(C_i\), \(C_i\) contributes causally to \(C_j\), and both belong to the same relevant class. These are Bourrat's minimal reproducee conditions. | That the parent is reproductively autonomous or that its traits are transmitted. |
| **D — collective heritability** | Differences among parental collectives predict differences among their offspring collectives, relative to nonparents, for a declared collective trait. | That collectives differ in reproductive success or control reproduction autonomously. |
| **E — collective Darwinian individuality** | Collectives vary, those differences affect collective reproductive success, and the relevant differences are transmitted; stronger cases also exhibit reproductive autonomy and fine-grained reproductive causal control. | Completion of every physiological or evolutionary transition, and certainly not OEE. |

[Doulcier, Takacs & Bourrat 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/psa.2023.162), [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-024-04880-w), [Lewontin 1970](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.es.01.110170.000245)

### 2.1 Genealogy need not be a tree

Sexual reproduction and aggregative construction show that one offspring may have multiple parents. Bourrat's multiple-parent collective models explicitly assign contributions from several parental collectives. A collective genealogy can therefore be a directed pedigree or ancestry hypergraph rather than a tree. What is required is a non-arbitrary mapping of causally weighted parental contributions to a later collective. If mixing is so extensive that no collective boundary, contribution, or descent event can be tracked, only lower-level cellular genealogy remains identifiable. [Bourrat 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12064-019-00294-2)

### 2.2 Heritability is level- and trait-specific

Lower-level genetic inheritance can reconstruct a higher-level phenotype; a separate collective genome is unnecessary. Clonal snowflake work shows that emergent collective traits can be highly heritable because inherited cellular properties and development reconstruct them. But positive collective-level heritability alone is not always diagnostic of individuality: for additive collective traits it can be inherited mathematically from particle-level heritability even when offspring collectives have multiple parents. Bourrat therefore emphasizes parent-specific fidelity—the variance among offspring collective traits produced by a particular parent—and population structure, especially for nonlinear collective traits. [Herron, Zamani-Dahaj & Ratcliff 2018](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-018-0612-6), [Bourrat 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12064-019-00294-2), [Zamani-Dahaj et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes14081635)

### 2.3 MLS1 and MLS2 answer different questions

In MLS1, group context changes the survival or reproduction of particles, so evolutionary change is counted in particle types. In MLS2, collective types change in frequency through differential production or persistence of daughter collectives. Collective fitness-1 can be based on particle output; collective fitness-2 is based on collective offspring. These descriptions may coexist during a transition, but evidence for the first does not establish the second. An explicit group-fitness variable is not necessary for MLS2; an empirically identifiable collective pedigree, heritable collective variation, and differential collective output are. [Damuth & Heisler 1988](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00647962), [Okasha 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199267972.001.0001), [Gardner 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/jeb.12566)

### 2.4 Reproductive autonomy and transition in individuality

Bourrat distinguishes a **reproducee** from a **reproducer** by reproductive autonomy: the capacity of the whole spatiotemporal object to produce another member of its class across a range of relevant backgrounds. This is graded and background-relative. A paradigmatic Darwinian individual additionally has reproductive causal control: fine changes in parental properties can generate corresponding offspring changes, supporting cumulative selection. Nascent life cycles can partition variation and create heritable collective phenotypes before lower-level autonomy and conflict are strongly suppressed; group formation is therefore not identical to completion of a transition in individuality. [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-024-04880-w), [Ratcliff et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0420), [Shelton & Michod 2020](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2019.0364)

## 3. Fernandes life-cycle regimes

Only the reported multicellular regimes are relevant here.

| Reported regime | Relevant process | Individuality relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **multicellular + unicellular propagules** | A migrating aggregate leaves a dividing cell behind; it divides; its daughters reactivate adhesion, form a newborn cluster, and migrate. | Strongest local parent–propagule–offspring relation and strongest endogenous reproductive regulation. |
| **strictly multicellular with multicellular propagules** | Dividing cells reduce adhesion to migrating cells while retaining mutual adhesion and detach as a group. | Direct material collective offspring, but no collective pedigree, trait transmission, or output distribution is reported. |
| **high-adhesion strictly multicellular** | Large aggregative clusters perform collective chemotaxis and split rarely by physical tearing when regions follow different food patches. | Strong collective function with the weakest reported regulated multiplication; internal negative control for equating multicellular function with individuality. |

Across regimes, mutations occur at cell division and the explicit genealogies are cell genealogies. Reported clusters in both the single-cell-propagule and high-adhesion examples contain many distantly related cell lineages. [Fernandes, Vroomans & Colizzi 2026, preprint v3](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.08.681199)

## 4. Collective genealogy

**Fact.** Figure 3 follows a cell that begins inside a migrating cluster, switches adhesion state after feeding, detaches, divides, and produces two daughters that re-adhere as a newborn two-cell cluster. This establishes more than temporal succession:

\[
C_i\rightarrow p\rightarrow C_j.
\]

The source cluster causally supplies the propagule, and the propagule supplies all material in the initially observed newborn cluster. One local parent–offspring edge is therefore supported under the broad Q019 collective class.

**Fact.** The publication does not assign persistent identifiers to clusters, record cluster birth and death, trace cluster ancestry over repeated cycles, or estimate contributions from earlier clusters after mergers. Its phylogenetic analysis assigns colors to cells in clusters at the final time and traces their cellular ancestry; it demonstrates within-cluster genetic heterogeneity, not a genealogy of collectives. The manuscript's “lineage” normally denotes a cell-genetic lineage or a population represented by its cellular MRCA, not a lineage of parent and offspring clusters.

**Interpretation.** Aggregation does not logically erase genealogy. A later cluster could have one parent, several weighted parents, or only cellular ancestors, depending on its actual split/merge history. The reported single-cell event has a locally identifiable source parent; later incorporation of unrelated cells can create many-parent edges. What is absent is the time-resolved split/merge pedigree needed to decide whether those local edges concatenate into persistent collective lineages. Thus **short-range collective parentage is supported; multigeneration collective genealogy is not established**.

## 5. Single-cell propagule

The single cell is best interpreted as a reproductive stage of the broad collective life cycle, not automatically as the death of one collective followed only by cell reproduction. The interpretation is legitimate when four relations coincide:

1. the propagule has a parent-specific causal origin;
2. it carries material from that parent;
3. inherited organization reconstructs a later member of the same collective class;
4. the cycle closes recurrently.

Fernandes reports all four qualitatively for the illustrated event. The propagule inherits a cell GRN and metabolic material, divides, reactivates adhesion, and reconstructs a migrating cluster. This supplies both material continuity and a plausible organizational continuity through the inherited regulatory program.

It does not follow that a higher-level evolutionary lineage is established. The paper does not show whether the reconstructed cluster's collective traits resemble those of its particular source cluster, whether later aggregation overwhelms that relation, or whether offspring collectives differ predictably among parent collectives. The single-cell stage therefore strengthens genealogy relative to undirected aggregation, but it does not supply collective heritability by itself.

## 6. Collective traits

The paper reports or derives the following traits; their measurement levels must remain explicit.

| Trait | Reported evidence | Actual measured bearer |
|---|---|---|
| **cluster size** | Median and interquartile distributions differ among evolved strategies; high-adhesion regimes form the largest clusters. | Connected clusters sampled from population simulations. |
| **reproductive/dispersal mode** | Strictly unicellular, single-cell propagule, multicellular propagule, and high-adhesion/rare-tearing categories are inferred from state-specific adhesion. | A cell-genetic lineage assayed as a population cloned from its MRCA. |
| **propagule formation** | Direct trajectories show state-regulated detachment of one or several dividing cells. | Local cluster event generated by cellular regulation. |
| **collective chemotaxis** | Large clusters integrate spatial information through pushing and pulling and migrate faster toward distant food. | Cluster-level performance with consequences for constituent cells. |
| **dispersal** | Quadrat-based population spatial dispersion differs among strategies. | Whole cell population, not an individual collective. |
| **adhesion organization** | Migrating and dividing states evolve distinct ligand–receptor profiles; coordination is genetically coupled in propagule formers. | Heritable cellular GRN phenotype expressed relationally in clusters. |
| **division response/timing** | Genetically encoded metabolic-reserve thresholds consistently regulate division within a cell lineage. | Cell trait contributing to the collective cycle. |
| **ecological persistence and invasion** | Propagule-forming populations invade EC1 hosts; many transferred multicellular lineages retain a multicellular phase. | Cell lineage/population success, not descendant-collective output. |

No reported table or figure treats individual parent collectives and their offspring collectives as paired observational units.

## 7. Trait transmission

**Supported at the cellular-program level.** GRN parameters mutate and are inherited at cell division. The authors extract the cellular MRCA of an evolved population, start a mutation-free simulation with 100 clones, and recover its characteristic adhesion and life-cycle behavior. The evolution of propagule formation also produces rapid selective sweeps in the cellular population. These results show that inherited cell programs can reliably reconstruct a collective phenotype.

**Partially supported across one collective event.** In the illustrated single-cell cycle, a propagule from a source cluster produces daughters expressing the inherited regulation required to reconstruct a new cluster.

**Not established across a collective genealogy.** The mutation-free assay begins with 100 cloned cells rather than offspring collectives sampled from identified parents. It therefore tests a genotype-to-population phenotype map, not parent–offspring collective resemblance. Repetition of a phenotype by cells bearing a common program is compatible with collective heredity, but it is not evidence for it until the reconstructed phenotype is indexed to a collective pedigree.

## 8. Collective heritability

Lower-level encoding is not disqualifying. If a propagule inherits a cell genotype that reliably reconstructs a collective trait, and if the propagule links an identified parent collective to an identified offspring collective, that genetic route can realize collective inheritance just as a zygotic genome reconstructs organismal traits.

The converse caution is decisive here. Fernandes establishes cellular heredity and repeatable reconstruction, but does not estimate:

- variation of a declared trait among parent collectives;
- average trait values of each parent's offspring collectives;
- resemblance of offspring to their parent rather than to nonparents;
- the effect of multi-parent aggregation on transmission fidelity;
- collective-trait transmission conditional on cell genotype, composition, and environment.

For additive traits such as a mean cellular adhesion value, a positive collective heritability could simply mirror particle heritability. For nonlinear traits such as coordinated chemotaxis, propagule production, or a composition-dependent life cycle, mixing can make transmission strongly context-dependent. The paper supplies no parent–offspring collective statistic or analogous fidelity analysis. **Collective heritability is therefore NOT ESTABLISHED, not contradicted.**

## 9. Collective reproductive success

Under MLS2, the natural candidate is the number or rate of descendant collectives generated by an identified collective, possibly separated into fecundity, life-cycle completion, and descendant persistence. Which of these is appropriate depends on the declared collective pedigree.

Fernandes does not report such a measure. Its quantity called fitness is steady-state food depletion by a population in mutation-free simulation. Competition assays record whether an invading cell lineage/population displaces a host lineage/population. Cluster size, population dispersal, cell survival, and cell division explain these outcomes, but none counts descendant collectives of a particular parent collective.

The rapid sweep of propagule-forming cellular genotypes shows differential success of inherited strategies. It does not establish that variation among collectives caused differential collective reproductive success. A collective fitness measure is conceptually definable from the reported cycle but empirically absent.

## 10. MLS1 / MLS2

**MLS1-like dynamics are supported.** Collective chemotaxis, cluster size, spatial integrity, and dispersal alter food acquisition, starvation, survival, and division of constituent cells. The group context therefore changes cell-lineage fitness. Propagule-forming regulatory programs sweep because their cells combine collective migration with dispersal.

**MLS2-like dynamics are not established.** No analysis defines a population of collectives, records collective parents and offspring, assigns collective reproductive output, or shows a covariance between collective phenotype and descendant-collective number. This absence is evidential, not conceptual: MLS2 could emerge without an explicit group-fitness function in the simulator if those causal/statistical relations occurred.

The regimes occupy different structural positions without forcing a binary ontological classification. High-adhesion clusters are the clearest MLS1-like case: collective performance benefits cells while collective multiplication is rare. Propagule-forming regimes provide the missing architecture for MLS2—recurrent collective offspring—but the published bookkeeping remains at the cell-lineage/population level.

## 11. Reproductive autonomy

### Supported aspects

- No experimenter supplies group identifiers, group transfers, group generations, or a propagule-production operator.
- A cell's metabolic state activates a heritable regulatory coupling between division and adhesion; the resulting cluster process generates propagules endogenously.
- The source cluster's movement, feeding, local resource exhaustion, cell-state changes, and differential adhesion jointly organize propagule release.
- Propagule-forming lineages can persist or invade outside the environment in which they first evolved; 35 of 49 transferred multicellular lineages retained a multicellular phase in EC1, all while preserving or evolving propagule formation.

### Unsupported or unresolved aspects

- The cross-environment results concern cell-genetic populations, not reproductive output of identified parent collectives.
- Food-patch geometry strongly determines which cycle is expressed and favored; no collective-level causal-stability analysis separates parental organization from background contribution.
- Cells retain independent survival and division, and unrelated cell lineages repeatedly aggregate; the focal whole may therefore be the cellular lineage-plus-environmental interaction rather than a persistent collective individual.
- No intervention asks whether an identified collective continues producing descendant collectives after controlled variation in relevant background features.

**Interpretation.** Fernandes is more autonomous than an externally passaged scaffolded collective because its boundary formation and propagule release are not imposed by a group-level protocol. It nonetheless has only **partially supported reproductive autonomy** at the collective level. Bourrat's framework supplies no justified threshold, and Q020 introduces none.

## 12. Physiological versus evolutionary integration

Wilmsen and Kost separate an integrated physiological component from a joint evolutionary component. Fernandes exhibits a genuine collective function: cells in large clusters combine forces and spatial information to improve chemotaxis. But the cells are not shown to be obligately interdependent, can survive and reproduce independently, and repeatedly mix with distant lineages.

The best-supported classification is therefore **Type IV-like during the collective phase**: a functionally consequential aggregation of lower-level physiological/evolutionary units that retain autonomy. Some coordinated clusters may show Type V-like physiological coherence, but the required degree of interdependence is not established. Type VI's joint evolutionary component is not supported: there is no vertically tracked collective lineage, collective heredity assay, or suppression of cellular evolutionary independence. [Wilmsen & Kost 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.70098)

This classification is structural, contextual, and stage-specific; it is not a scalar maturity score.

## 13. Strongest positive case

**Exactly one case: the multicellular + unicellular-propagule regime illustrated in Figure 3.**

It is strongest because a particular source cluster causally produces an identifiable propagule; inherited regulation coordinates detachment with division; that propagule reconstructs a newborn cluster; the cycle is generated without imposed collective reproduction; and propagule-forming cellular lineages show adaptive persistence and invasion. It therefore supports multiplication, closure, a local collective parent–offspring edge, genetically reconstructible organization, and partial autonomy in one compatible regime.

It stops short of higher-level Darwinian individuality because the study does not follow descendant clusters over successive cycles, measure parent–offspring collective trait resemblance, or count descendant collectives by parental collective.

## 14. Strongest negative case

**Exactly one internal control: the high-adhesion strictly multicellular regime.**

These large, genetically heterogeneous aggregates show stable multicellular structure and collective chemotaxis. Yet they rarely split, have no reported regulated propagule program, and are evaluated through cell-population food acquisition rather than collective offspring. This is the cleanest reported case in which:

\[
\boxed{\text{multicellular organization and collective function}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{collective Darwinian individuality}.}
\]

Rare tearing preserves Q019's broad reproduction classification but does not supply genealogy, heredity, or MLS2 evidence.

| **Darwinian individuality** | No combined evidence for genealogically indexed heritable collective variation and differential collective reproduction. | **NOT ESTABLISHED** | The published model does not yet demonstrate a higher-level Darwinian population. |

`NOT ESTABLISHED` is not `CONTRADICTED`. The paper was designed to explain the emergence and regulation of reproductive strategies, not to measure collective individuality.

### Genealogy-ablation thought experiment

**Thought experiment.** Remove every proposed collective parent–offspring link from the analysis while retaining cell ancestry, cluster snapshots, adhesion states, resource use, cell survival/division, population dispersal, and competition outcomes. The paper's principal reported results remain detectable: life-cycle categories can be inferred from cellular adhesion programs; clusters and collective chemotaxis can be observed; propagule events can be seen; cellular strategies can sweep; and populations can invade or persist.

What disappears is the ability to test whether parent collectives transmit traits and differ in descendant-collective output. This shows that explicit collective lineage tracking is not required for the paper's process and cell-selection conclusions, but is indispensable for the stronger MLS2/Darwinian-individuality claim. It does not show that collective genealogy is absent in the model.

## 16. Minimum missing analysis

**One lineage-resolved collective pedigree analysis of the reported single-cell-propagule regime is the minimum decisive test.**

Track connected collectives through split, propagule, reconstruction, and merger events as a weighted parentage hypergraph; attach one predeclared reported collective trait (propagule-production mode) to each collective; and record descendant-collective number. On that single pedigree, test whether parental trait differences predict both offspring trait values and differential collective output, with cellular genotype/composition recorded and the already reported food backgrounds kept explicit.

This is one analysis, not a new model or experimental program. It simultaneously distinguishes local material descent from a persistent genealogy, repeated cell-program expression from collective transmission, and MLS1 from an MLS2 component. Failure to recover stable parentage or parent-specific predictive transmission would support “collective reproducee process only”; positive transmission plus differential collective output would support a nascent higher-level Darwinian process. Cross-background replication within the same analysis would bound reproductive autonomy without inventing a scalar threshold.

## 17. Central decision

**B — GENEALOGICAL REPRODUCER**

The strongest regime supports a real but short-range collective parent–offspring relation: one source cluster produces a single-cell propagule that reconstructs a newborn collective. Aggregation permits reticulate or many-parent descent and does not invalidate that edge. Published evidence does not show that such edges concatenate into a stable multigeneration pedigree, but the local structure is strong enough to exceed “collective reproducer only.”

The classification stops at B because collective heritability, collective fitness-2, and MLS2-like selection are not established. Endogenous propagule regulation supplies partial reproductive autonomy, but without a collective pedigree it cannot be assigned cleanly to persistent collective individuals. Therefore categories C–E exceed the evidence, while F would discard the directly observed local parentage relation.

This is an empirical classification of the publication, not a new theory and not a mechanism claim.

## 18. Mechanism readiness

**NOT READY**

The published evidence does not yet establish a sufficiently clear higher-level evolutionary process. It establishes collective reproduction, one local genealogical edge, inherited cellular programs that reconstruct collective behavior, and MLS1-like ecological selection. It does not establish genealogically indexed collective heritability or differential collective reproduction, the two observations needed to identify an MLS2-like Darwinian population. Partial autonomy cannot substitute for them.

Consequently, the phenomenon to be explained by a later mechanism pass is still underdetermined. No inference is made that ecological interaction generically creates higher-level individuality.

## 19. Remaining explanatory gap

The narrow gap is:

> Do locally observed cluster → propagule → cluster edges concatenate into a persistent, possibly reticulate collective pedigree in which propagule-production differences are transmitted and predict descendant-collective output?

Everything broader—whether multicellular reproduction occurs, whether group function can affect cell fitness, and whether inherited cell programs can reconstruct aggregate behavior—is already settled for this model.

## 21. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### ESTABLISHED / KNOWN

- Fernandes exhibits collective multiplication and qualitative life-cycle closure.
- The single-cell-propagule regime contains a directly traceable local source-cluster → propagule → newborn-cluster relation.
- Aggregation and multiple parents are compatible with genealogy in principle; they require explicit contribution tracking rather than a tree assumption.
- Inherited cell programs can reconstruct collective phenotypes; lower-level encoding does not disqualify collective heredity.
- The publication measures cell ancestry, cellular mutation/inheritance, population food depletion, and population competition, not a multigeneration collective pedigree or collective fitness-2.
- MLS1-like effects occur because collective organization changes cell survival and division.
- Absence of an explicit group-fitness variable does not preclude emergent MLS2, but no MLS2 analysis is reported.

### PARTIALLY EXPLORED / UNDEREXPLORED

- Collective pedigrees in aggregative systems with repeated splitting, merging, and single-cell reconstruction.
- Parent-specific transmission fidelity for nonlinear, composition-dependent collective traits.
- Reproductive autonomy of endogenously regulated but genetically heterogeneous collective cycles.
- Joint use of weighted genealogy, collective quantitative genetics, and multilevel selection decomposition in level-blind artificial systems.

### POTENTIALLY NOVEL

None. The conceptual distinctions and proposed measurements are established prior art. Their application could yield a new empirical result, but Q020 does not supply it.

### UNKNOWN

- Whether Fernandes clusters form persistent one- or many-parent collective lineages over multiple cycles.
- Whether propagule-production mode or collective chemotaxis is transmitted from parent to offspring collectives.
- Whether collective variants differ in descendant-collective output after split/merge ancestry is resolved.
- Whether collective reproduction is causally stable across relevant backgrounds at the level of identified collectives.
- Whether any MLS2 component coexists with the established MLS1-like process.

## 22. Hypothesis decision

**NO HYPOTHESIS JUSTIFIED**

Categories A–F are empirical classifications, not new dynamical relations. Collective heritability from lower-level inheritance, MLS1/MLS2, reproductive autonomy, and reticulate parentage are prior art. Q020 identifies missing validation and makes no claim that ecological interactions generally cause higher-level individuality. [HYPOTHESES.md](HYPOTHESES.md) remains unchanged.
