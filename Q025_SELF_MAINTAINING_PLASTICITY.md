## 2. Frontier selection

**Selected frontier: F4 — self-maintaining plasticity.**

F4 is the current canonical active frontier in `FRONTIER.md`. This follows transparently from the old ranking: Q022 removed F5, Q024 removed F3, leaving F4 as the highest-ranked unblocked survivor.

It outranks the next two alternatives by expected information gain:

- **F2 — recurrent evolutionary-regime transformation** is more central to OEE, but prior work shows that single transitions, recursive transition types, scaffolding, and historical prerequisites can mimic recurrence. Separating a general recurrence mechanism without worked histories has lower theoretical tractability and a higher eventual empirical burden.
- **Continuity across changing evolutionary units** is tractable conceptually, but Q010 and Q016 already place it mainly in formalization: correspondences are domain-specific and sometimes partial. It is important for valid claims but unlikely to supply an anti-closure process.

F4 has lower empirical dependence and high branch-elimination power: strong developmental and organizational prior art could either absorb it decisively or isolate one interventionally meaningful residue. It is not selected merely because it is adjacent to Q024.

## 3. Current OEE target

The current target distinguishes four increasingly demanding claims:

\[
\text{long evolutionary activity}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{continued adaptive substitutions}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{continued novelty}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{continued generation of new evolutionary possibilities}.
\]

Long activity can be neutral turnover or recurrence inside a fixed effective regime. Continued adaptive substitutions can exhibit diminishing returns inside one supplied problem. Continued novelty can enumerate new members of a fixed phenotype vocabulary. The strongest OEE target is continued production of resource-feasible routes to further adaptive forms or regimes that are not exhausted by the current effective organization.

This target does not require monotonic complexity, monotonic expansion, preparedness for arbitrary futures, or one universal novelty metric. It permits losses, specialization, historical dependence, and only partial comparison across transitions.

## 4. Exact research question

> Is maintaining evolutionary identity while altering the organization that generates heritable variation a mechanism problem beyond established robustness–evolvability, facilitated-variation, plasticity, canalization, and developmental-system-drift theory?

## 5. Strongest prior art

The strongest absorption challenge is not one theory but a division of explanatory labor:

| Claim hidden inside F4 | Strongest prior art | Established causal content |
|---|---|---|
| a living token persists while components turn over | autopoiesis, adaptivity, autonomy, closure of constraints | operational or constraint relations regenerate and regulate conditions of continued viability |
| a homologous trait persists while its genetic/developmental realization changes | developmental-system drift, compensatory evolution, stabilizing selection, GRN evolution | robust neutral change or selected compensation preserves an output while causal underpinnings diverge |
| a phenotype responds flexibly and the response subsequently evolves | phenotypic accommodation, reaction-norm theory, genetic accommodation/assimilation | plastic response exposes or reorganizes phenotypic variation; selection changes its regulation and environmental sensitivity |
| perturbations are buffered while new variants remain reachable | canalization, robustness, degeneracy, neutral-network and evolvability theory | buffering may preserve viable variation or expose cryptic variation, with context-dependent effects on access |
| reproductive/hereditary organization changes while historical continuity remains | major-transition, nascent-life-cycle, scaffolding, endogenization, and multilevel-selection theory | overlapping lower/higher-level reproduction and temporary external structure can stabilize a new hereditary individual |
| the organization producing variants evolves | facilitated variation and evolution of genotype–phenotype maps | weak linkage, modularity, developmental organization, mutation, and inheritance processes can change |

Closure-of-constraints theory characterizes biological stability through mutually maintained constraints at declared timescales. Its companion variation theory goes further than the proposed F4 slogan: constraints may change, disappear, or be incorporated, and an organized object may move from one closed regime to another rather than preserve a fixed set of components. It explicitly states that closure can remain while the constraints realizing it vary. [Montévil & Mossio 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029), [Montévil et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2016.08.005)

Developmental-system drift (DSD) directly realizes another strong reading. Homologous phenotypes can retain their identity while their genetic basis changes by robust neutral movement, mutation, selection on correlated traits followed by compensation, loss or addition of components, and regulatory rewiring. DSD is an outcome class with several mechanisms, not a synonym for genetic drift. [True & Haag 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1525-142x.2001.003002109.x), [McColgan & DiFrisco 2024](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.203054)

Phenotypic and genetic accommodation supply a change-first pathway: a novel perturbation elicits coordinated developmental adjustment; heritable variation in the response is selected; and the response may remain plastic or become assimilated/canalized. This already explains preservation of viable organization during many phenotype and regulation changes without requiring selection for abstract future flexibility. [West-Eberhard 2005](https://doi.org/10.1002/jez.b.21071), [Lande 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2009.01754.x)

Major-transition theory covers changes in the bearer of evolutionary identity. Nascent life cycles, multilevel selection, conflict mediation, bottlenecks, ecological scaffolds, and later endogenization can maintain an overlapping route from lower-level reproduction to a higher-level hereditary individual. These mechanisms need not preserve lower-level autonomy. [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112), [Black, Bourrat & Rainey 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-019-1086-9), [Hammerschmidt et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0390), [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1086/719118)

## 6. What existing theory already explains

**Fact.** Existing theory explains that:

1. organismic persistence can depend on a stable relation among changing material components rather than component identity;
2. homologous phenotypes can remain stable while developmental mechanisms diverge extensively;
3. neutral robustness and compensatory selection provide distinct routes to such developmental change;
4. plasticity can buffer a new perturbation and then evolve toward greater sensitivity, accommodation, or assimilation;
5. robustness and plasticity are not simple opposites and neither has a universal positive effect on evolvability;
6. conserved core processes plus weak regulatory linkage can combine present reliability with regulatory redeployment;
7. new reproductive and hereditary organization can be scaffolded during an interval in which old and new levels overlap;
8. a scaffolded property can become endogenized, permitting persistence after removal of the original scaffold;
9. the genotype–phenotype map and variation-generating organization can themselves change;
10. every identity claim—organism, trait, reproducer, lineage, or evolutionary individual—uses a different continuity relation.

Robustness is particularly destructive of a generic trade-off. It can impede adaptation by hiding phenotypic effects or facilitate it by allowing populations to explore neutral networks; the sign depends on population size, mutation rate, and landscape structure. Degeneracy may combine overlapping functionality with access to alternatives, but it is a proposed architecture within robustness/evolvability theory, not a universal OEE principle. [Draghi et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08694), [Whitacre & Bender 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2009.11.008)

**Interpretation.** F4 cannot retain “systems are both stable and plastic” as a discovery. The scientifically useful question is which identity relation is preserved, which organization changes, and which known causal process connects them.

## 7. Prior-art absorption test

Adversarial claim:

\[
\text{self-maintaining plasticity}
=
\text{organizational maintenance}
+
\text{plasticity/accommodation}
+
\text{robustness/DSD}
+
\text{transition scaffolding}.
\]

The absorption succeeds for every stable reading found:

1. **Same organism, changing state or components.** Autopoiesis, adaptivity, regulation, and closure explain maintenance under perturbation and material turnover.
2. **Same trait, changing developmental basis.** Homology declares trait identity; DSD explains neutral, robust, compensatory, or pleiotropy-driven rewiring.
3. **Same lineage, changing reaction norm.** Quantitative-genetic plasticity and genetic accommodation explain the response and its stabilization or loss.
4. **Changing evolutionary individual.** Genealogy, nascent life cycles, scaffolding, endogenization, and multilevel selection explain continuity during a specified transition.
5. **Changing distribution of heritable variants.** Evolution-of-evolvability and facilitated-variation theory already explain the map change; Q024 excludes target-independent retention.

The phrase “preserve the relations needed for continuity while those very relations change” does not identify a fifth causal process. If the relevant relation is retained at a coarser level while its realizers change, the case is ordinary multiple realization, DSD, closure, or historical continuity. If every relation used to establish identity disappears with no genealogical or causal bridge, calling the result the same evolving system is underdetermined rather than mechanistically unexplained.

The closest organizational literature even formulates the apparent conjunction directly: closure provides relative stability while variation replaces constraints and yields successive organized regimes. That framework is not a predictive population-genetic mechanism for every transition, but it establishes that the conjunction and vocabulary are prior art. Local mechanisms must still be supplied by developmental, physiological, ecological, or transition theory.

**Absorption result:** no independent process \(M\) remains after the claim is disaggregated. F4 is a useful research slogan but not a distinct general mechanism frontier.

## 8. Counterexamples

| Case | Counterexample | What it defeats |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | An autopoietic or constraint-closed system continually replaces components yet has no reproduction or heritable variation. | Self-maintenance plus material plasticity is insufficient for evolution or OEE. |
| **B** | DSD preserves a homologous phenotype while much of its regulatory basis changes under stabilizing selection, robustness, or compensation. | Identity-preserving organizational rewiring is not sufficient for novelty or new evolutionary possibilities. |
| **C** | A plastic response facilitates survival after an extreme change and is later genetically assimilated, reducing the original plasticity. | Transformational persistence need not preserve plasticity, and plasticity need not be self-maintaining. |
| **D** | Mutational robustness facilitates adaptation in one population/landscape regime and impedes it in another. | No universal robustness–plasticity balance maximizes future evolution. |
| **E** | A nascent multicellular life cycle stabilizes higher-level heredity while ratcheting changes erode cellular autonomy and reversibility. | Evolutionary continuity may require loss rather than preservation of old organizational flexibility. |
| **F** | A conserved trait is produced by a replaced or redeployed GRN, while the lineage exhibits no sustained transformational novelty. | Even extensive change of the developmental generator is not OEE. |
| **G** | Homeostasis repeatedly returns a system to one attractor after perturbation. | Dynamical recovery can produce indefinite persistence while actively suppressing consequential change. |
| **H** | High morphological deformability or diversity occurs without a genealogical relation or selectable heritable differences. | Continuous morphology and visible plasticity do not establish evolutionary continuity. |

The strongest positive-looking case is also absorptive. GRN studies describe “repeal, replace, and redeploy,” including putative wholesale substitution of a network while gross developmental output remains conserved. This demonstrates that extensive realization change is compatible with trait continuity; it does not reveal a general self-maintaining-plasticity mechanism, and molecular studies emphasize multiple distinct routes rather than one unifying process. [Halfon 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tig.2017.04.005)

## 9. Surviving distinction

A distinction survives, but it is classificatory rather than mechanistic:

\[
\boxed{
\text{persistence of a material token}
\neq
\text{preservation of an organized function}
\neq
\text{trait homology}
\neq
\text{genealogical continuity}
\neq
\text{continuity of an evolutionary individual}
}
\]

The relevant invariant may be a maintained closure relation, a homologous character, a parent–offspring chain, a life-cycle relation, or a transmission structure. None is universal. Components and lower-level capabilities may be lost while a higher-level relation persists; conversely, morphology may persist while heredity or reproduction changes.

For a declared domain, identity-preserving transformation can be tested. Let \(I\) be an independently justified identity relation, \(\Delta\) a specified organizational change, and \(M\) a candidate bridge such as redundancy, compensation, plastic accommodation, or scaffolding. A local comparison may ask whether

\[
P(I\text{ persists and }\Delta\text{ establishes}\mid \operatorname{do}(M=\text{intact}))
>
P(I\text{ persists and }\Delta\text{ establishes}\mid \operatorname{do}(M=\text{removed})).
\]

This schema can validate a known or case-specific mechanism. It cannot choose \(I\), equate different identity kinds, or create a general process called self-maintaining plasticity.

## 10. Mechanistic interpretation

No general causal mechanism remains unexplained. Several local mechanisms are interventionally meaningful:

- disrupting a mutually maintaining constraint can reduce current viability;
- removing redundancy or compensation can expose a previously conserved phenotype to developmental change;
- blocking a plastic response can reduce survival during an environmental transition;
- removing an ecological scaffold before endogenization can prevent higher-level heredity from establishing;
- changing bottleneck or assortment structure can alter the efficacy of higher-level selection.

These interventions alter different outcomes on different timescales. Their conjunction is not an additional causal variable. “Enough stability plus enough plasticity” is also not a mechanism: without a declared perturbation distribution, identity relation, and transition target, neither quantity has a fixed meaning or causal optimum.

The remaining issue is **definition/formalization**: specify the bearer and continuity relation before asking which local process maintains it. Lack of one universal scalar or invariant does not make the local mechanisms unknown.

## 12. Autopoiesis contribution

\[
\boxed{\textbf{USEFUL}}
\]

Autopoiesis and closure-of-constraints theory help isolate one meaning of maintenance: an organization regenerates the relations or constraints that sustain its present existence despite material turnover. Di Paolo's adaptivity extension adds regulation of tendencies relative to viability rather than mere continued autopoiesis. Organizational variation theory explicitly permits the constraint set to change while closure is re-established. [Varela, Maturana & Uribe 1974](https://doi.org/10.1016/0303-2647(74)90031-8), [Di Paolo 2005](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-005-9002-y), [Montévil et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2016.08.005)

The contribution stops there. Closure neither establishes heredity nor predicts OEE, and moving from one closed regime to another is a description until a domain-specific transition mechanism is supplied.

## 13. Lenia contribution

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NONE}}
\]

Lenia illustrates persistent, deformable patterns in continuous state spaces. Without an independently established reproducer, hereditary lineage, and organizational transition, this adds no theoretical leverage to F4. Continuous morphology does not imply self-maintaining evolutionary identity or OEE.

## 14. Deep-learning contribution

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NONE}}
\]

The stability–plasticity analogy adds no content beyond biological robustness, reaction norms, canalization, developmental-system drift, and facilitated variation. Representation learning would return to the F3 branch closed by Q024. No learning-theoretic formalism changes the F4 result, and no neural mechanism is imported.

## 15. Central decision

\[
\boxed{\textbf{TERMINOLOGICAL REPACKAGING}}
\]

“Self-maintaining plasticity” combines several scientifically meaningful but already theorized processes under one phrase. The shared verbal tension between persistence and change does not identify an independently manipulable mechanism. Where the identity relation is specified, established domain theories provide the causal candidates; where it is not, the residue is definition/formalization.

## 16. Mechanism readiness

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NOT READY}}
\]

The general target is not stable enough to support one intervention or prediction, and its domain-specific versions are substantially covered by prior art. A new mechanism would require one declared identity relation and a causal effect not explained by robustness, compensation, accommodation, closure, scaffolding, or evolution of developmental organization. No such effect survived this pass.

## 17. Remaining explanatory gap

The narrow residue is:

> For any particular claimed transformation, which independently justified relation carries identity across the change, and which known or case-specific bridge makes its continued reproduction or maintenance possible?

This is primarily a domain-specific definition/formalization and validation task. It may reveal an unknown mechanism in a particular system, but no general missing process is established by the abstract tension alone.

## 18. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

- **ESTABLISHED:** physiological organization persists through component turnover; plasticity, canalization, robustness, and genetic accommodation evolve; homologous traits can undergo DSD; GRNs and genotype–phenotype maps can be replaced or rewired; scaffolding and nascent life cycles can bridge specified changes in individuality.
- **PARTIALLY EXPLORED:** the relative roles of neutral robustness, compensatory selection, pleiotropy, and directional selection in DSD; when plasticity is maintained versus assimilated; how external scaffolds become endogenized.
- **UNDEREXPLORED:** comparative, interventionally matched studies of the same declared identity relation across different realization-changing mechanisms. This is not yet a general OEE frontier.
- **POTENTIALLY NOVEL:** none. No new causal relation survives the firewall.
- **UNKNOWN:** whether particular natural histories require bridge mechanisms not represented in current domain theories; whether useful generalizations exist across several identity domains without conflating their bearers.

## 19. Hypothesis decision

“Organization must balance stability and flexibility” is not sufficiently scoped or falsifiable, and every more specific process considered has close prior art. `HYPOTHESES.md` remains unchanged.
