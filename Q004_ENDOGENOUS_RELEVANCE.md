# Q004 — Can evolutionary relevance be endogenous without being fixed?

2026-09-07. Exactly one theoretical research pass, continuing [Q003](Q003_CAPACITY_AND_OBSERVER.md). No implementation, OEE mechanism, or new hypothesis.

**Result / Interpretation:** Partially yes. Organisms can constitute, regulate, and historically change the dependencies that make particular differences consequential for them. Existing theories explicitly address endogenous goals and changing biological norms. But three claims remain distinct: producing one's own conditions of maintenance, changing those conditions, and generating new kinds of evolutionary possibility. None implies the next without additional evidence.

The narrow unresolved issue is not whether dependencies can change. It is how to distinguish a newly enabling function from new dependence within an existing function, while justifying the relevant beneficiary and level of description.

## 1. Problem

Q003 made capacity objective conditional on dynamics, admissible continuations, and outcomes. This left open why those outcomes should matter to the evolving system.

**Interpretation / diagnostic distinctions:** “Endogenous relevance” can refer to different achievements:

| Claim | What would be endogenous? | What does not follow? |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutive relevance | The organization produces conditions on which its continuation depends | That it detects or regulates threats |
| Regulatory relevance | The system responds differently to conditions according to their consequences for its own organization | That the repertoire of concerns evolves |
| Historical relevance | Selection or organizational history establishes functions and dependencies | That every new dependency enables a new function |
| Evolutionary relevance classification | A distinction counts as a new kind of adaptive possibility | A unique classification from the preceding achievements |

These are a synthesis of the accounts below, not a proposed new theory. A norm in this note means a biological standard of success/failure, not a moral obligation or a statistically typical value. Nor must a norm be explicitly represented as a number or goal inside an organism.

The inquiry therefore does not begin with a human-selected target set. It asks how existing approaches obtain their reference for success, and where a reference is still supplied by their definitions.

## 2. Viability-based approaches

**Fact:** Mathematical viability theory derives which initial states admit trajectories respecting given constraints. Its viability kernel is derived; the constraint set and admissible dynamics are inputs. It does not, by itself, identify an organism or derive why those constraints ought to be maintained. [Aubin, *A Survey of Viability Theory*](https://doi.org/10.1137/0328044)

**Fact about an autonomy account:** Di Paolo grounds viability ranges in the organization and current structure of the system. His account explicitly permits a time-varying viability set. Adaptivity adds the capacity to distinguish tendencies toward loss of viability and regulate them before breakdown. Thus variable viability is already allowed; the account does not give a general evolutionary law deriving successive viability criteria. [Di Paolo, *Overcoming Autopoiesis*, discussion of adaptivity](https://ezequieldipaolo.net/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/dipaolo-overcoming-autopoiesis.pdf)

**Interpretation:** Separate three objects that a scalar $V$ can obscure:

- **Actual conditions of continued organization:** dependencies and failure modes produced by physical organization and its environment.
- **The system's regulatory responses:** what it senses, acts on, prioritizes, or fails to detect. These need not perfectly match its actual conditions of survival.
- **A scientist's viability measure:** lifetime, survival probability, maintenance of a particular organization, or another summary. Choosing one imposes a comparison and horizon; the system need not maximize that scalar.

Persistence alone is insufficient. A stable object or sustained dissipative process can persist without producing and regulating an organization of the kind autonomy theories require. Conversely, maintaining the present individual indefinitely is inappropriate for life cycles involving differentiation, reproductive sacrifice, or replacement across generations.

**Answer:** The system helps determine what its present maintenance physically requires. This does not uniquely determine a scalar ordering of all possible trajectories. Survival of an individual, persistence of a lineage, and maintenance of an ecological association can favor different outcomes. These are different beneficiaries and questions, not contradictory physical laws.

Whether “$V$ evolves” therefore needs disambiguation: a modeled survival probability can change because state or environment changes; actual maintenance requirements can change because organization changes; and an analyst can change the measure. Only the second directly concerns the requested endogenous change.

## 3. Autopoiesis / autonomy / closure

**Fact about the framework:** Autopoiesis characterizes a network producing the components that realize the network and its unity. Its reference is continued realization of that organization, rather than a designer's task. Original autopoietic organization does not require that each individual reproduce. [Varela, Maturana & Uribe 1974](https://doi.org/10.1016/0303-2647(74)90031-8)

Di Paolo explicitly argues that autopoiesis alone does not explain sense-making: an all-or-nothing continuation/breakdown distinction is insufficient for graded regulation of favorable and unfavorable tendencies. Adaptivity is an additional requirement, not a synonym for self-production. [Di Paolo 2005](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11097-005-9002-y)

**Fact / formal proposal:** Montévil and Mossio distinguish dissipative processes from constraints approximately conserved over the timescale of their action. Closure requires each constraint to depend directly on another constraint in the set and to help maintain another; strict closure additionally excludes decomposition into two closed sets. This concerns maintained causal dependencies, not isolation from matter/energy or exhaustion of evolutionary possibilities. Their boundary analysis allows partial descriptions, degrees of closure, and hierarchical organizations. [Montévil & Mossio 2015, §§3–6](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2015-mm-organisation-closure-constraints/)

**Interpretation:** This provides a principled alternative to choosing a desirable output first: identify a materially realized organization, then investigate what sustains or disrupts it. It constrains identity and causal contribution. It does not automatically rank every perturbation, identify every future function, or show that all important perturbations are detected. Regulatory structure can supply further distinctions.

**Fact / extension:** Bich's regulated-closure account distinguishes establishing/serving goals from actively pursuing them. Regulatory constraints coordinate activities and priorities according to internal and external conditions. This develops what the system itself does with its maintenance requirements; it is richer than an externally observed feedback equilibrium. [Bich 2024, *Organisational teleology 2.0*, §§4–5](https://leonardobich.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/ratio-2024-bich-organisational-teleology-2-0-grounding-biological-purposiveness-in-regulatory-control.pdf)

**Fact about criticisms:** Cusimano and Sterner argue that constraint counts and dependencies can change under redescription, and use a candle-flame analysis to challenge claimed objectivity. Corti separately disputes the inference from causal conditions of existence to normative “oughts.” These are criticisms of distinct steps, not a settled disproof of organizational explanation. [Cusimano & Sterner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10441-019-09365-9), [Corti 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-023-04255-7)

**Boundary:** A produced membrane supplies physical evidence of individuation, but need not settle every functional boundary. Nested cellular and collective organization is permitted; identifying it requires more than finding an arbitrary cycle. Constraint identification, relevant timescales, sufficient causal detail, and the treatment of interacting organizations remain substantive modeling commitments.

## 4. Biological function theories

**Fact / definitions; Interpretation / limits:**

| Account | What grounds a function? | What it naturalizes | What remains to be identified |
|---|---|---|---|
| Causal-role | Contribution of a component to a capacity of a system | How the capacity is realized | The containing system, capacity, and explanatory decomposition |
| Selected-effect | An effect responsible for the trait's historical selection/retention | Why that effect, rather than an incidental effect, has functional standing | Trait lineage, selection level, effect description, and relevant historical comparison |
| Organizational | Contribution to the differentiated organization that produces/maintains the component | Present reciprocal dependence between contributor and beneficiary | Constraints, organization, regime, and contribution |
| Ecological-organizational | Contribution within an ecologically closed network | Relational roles grounded in mutually maintained conditions | Whether and at what scale the ecological network actually realizes closure |
| Intrinsic fitness | Differential reproductive consequences arising through system interactions | A source of selection without a separately assigned task score | Reproductive entities, comparison context, and classification of adaptive kinds |

Causal-role analysis is explicitly relative to a capacity. Selected-effect accounts instead make history explanatory, allowing proper function to differ from present performance. Neither is simply “anything that causes a difference.” [Cummins 1975](https://joelvelasco.net/teaching/3334/cummins75-functional_analysis.pdf), [Wright 1973](https://mechanism.ucsd.edu/~bill/teaching/w10/wright.functions.%201973.pdf)

Mossio, Saborido, and Moreno require a component to contribute to an organization, be produced/maintained under its constraints, and belong to a differentiated system. They explicitly distinguish being functional in one self-maintenance regime from being indispensable across all regimes. Their account can recognize current contributions without waiting for a selection history, but its regime/class distinction retains a classification problem. [Mossio et al. 2009, §§5–5.1](https://www.ias-research.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Mossio_25592036.pdf)

Ecological functions need not presuppose that an ecosystem has undergone organism-like selection. The organizational proposal grounds them in contributions to collective maintenance. Its application requires demonstrating the relevant organization; a contribution to a scientist's preferred ecosystem output is insufficient. [Nunes-Neto, Moreno & El-Hani 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10539-013-9398-7)

**Interpretation / circularity:** The organizational strategy is a simultaneous characterization: constraints and their dependencies identify a candidate whole, and contributions within that whole identify functions. Mutual causal dependence is not itself a vicious logical circle. The unresolved issue is whether independently justified constraint descriptions select the whole sufficiently well. Defining the organism by whatever one already considers functional would merely relocate the original target choice.

**Intrinsic fitness is insufficient to classify adaptive kinds.** A reproductive consequence supplies selection pressure and evidence of significance; it does not distinguish faster performance of an established activity from a different activity. “Intrinsic” here describes how selection occurs, not a unique novelty ontology. Model-relative exploratory versus expansive novelty remains relevant. [Taylor 2019](https://www.tim-taylor.com/papers/taylor2019evolutionary.preprint.pdf)

## 5. Semantic information

**Fact:** Kolchinsky and Wolpert distinguish correlation from information whose removal by specified interventions changes viability. Their framework chooses a viability function, information measure, intervention class, system/environment decomposition, horizon, and initial distribution. Viability can be a function of a distribution, not just a score on an instantaneous microstate. They also propose optimizing information measures over decompositions, horizons, and initial distributions to reduce discretionary choice; this is a proposed selection strategy, not a proof of a unique biological beneficiary. [Kolchinsky & Wolpert 2018, §§IV–VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.08053)

**Fact:** Sowinski et al.'s forager has physically consequential resource depletion and a specified dead state. They use expected lifetime as viability and find a sensor-information threshold below which survival deteriorates. This is stronger grounding than a freely chosen reward unrelated to persistence. Nevertheless, the modeled agent, death condition, and lifetime statistic remain specified; the study varies sensing/foraging within an established organization. [Sowinski et al. 2023, §§II–III](https://artemyk.github.io/assets/pdf/papers/Sowinski-SemanticInformationInAModelOfResourceGatheringAgents.pdf)

**Interpretation / answers:**

- Causal necessity for a viability measure gives an operational sense of “semantic”; it does not establish every philosophical notion of meaning or normativity.
- An internally grounded failure mode constrains viable interpretations but does not uniquely choose boundary, horizon, or beneficiary.
- A viability benefit, the amount of relevant information, and emergence of a new semantic relation are distinct claims. A scalar increase alone supplies no classification of new functions.
- Semantic significance does not require prior selection for the relation. A newly acquired relation could already contribute to maintaining current viability before any measured increase in reproductive fitness. This is a possibility allowed by the criterion, not an empirical finding of this pass. A merely latent correlation with no relevant causal consequence under the chosen conditions does not qualify on that basis.
- Neither more semantic information nor a new semantic relation proves access to a new kind of evolutionary possibility; §8 supplies a bounded counterexample.

An additional proposal treats target conditions as materially embodied constraints: García-Valdecasas and Deacon describe an autogenic system that reconstructs its organization, interpreting a preserved constraint as a minimal self-representation. This directly addresses target grounding. The illustrative target is organizational continuity; the account does not derive an unlimited succession of new targets. [García-Valdecasas & Deacon 2024, §§5–6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-024-04705-w)

## 6. Endogenous normativity

**Fact:** Endogenous normativity is already an explicit research topic. Barandiaran, Di Paolo, and Rohde propose a generative definition of agency combining individuality, interactional asymmetry, and adaptive regulation. Norms are tied to the self-maintaining network and its environmental dependencies. The definition is an organizational proposal open to empirical revision, not a theorem that arbitrary dynamics generate norms. [Barandiaran et al. 2009, §§2–4](https://xabier.barandiaran.net/wp-content/uploads/2009/07/barandiaran_dipaolo_rohde_-_defining_agency_v_1_0_-_jab_20091.pdf)

**Interpretation / levels of rigor:**

| Existing result | Strength | Missing step for this question |
|---|---|---|
| Viability kernels | Mathematical results given dynamics and constraints | Endogenous selection of the constraints |
| Closure of constraints | Explicit dependence and closure conditions | Unique physical decomposition and normative interpretation |
| Semantic-information measures | Quantitative causal comparisons given an assay | Derivation of the assay and a novelty classification |
| Adaptivity/regulatory agency | Operational organizational conditions | General evolutionary account of how the conditions change |
| Historical accounts of evolving constraints | Explicit conceptual treatment of norm change | General representation-robust criterion for new relevance kinds |

These approaches naturalize different parts of the target problem. None needs a conscious goal-setter. But a system constituting a dependency, detecting its violation, and evaluating violations are different achievements. Likewise, naturalizing an operational sense of benefit/harm is weaker than deriving normativity in every philosophical sense.

In a recent critique, Cuciniello distinguishes a minimal maintenance account from a regulative account and asks how either handles development and changing purposiveness. The paper explicitly raises the problem of separating switching among available functions from developing new functions when availability is only established afterward. This is close prior art for the present unresolved boundary. [Cuciniello 2026, §§4–5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40656-026-00731-8)

## 7. Can norms themselves change?

**Fact about existing theory:** Canguilhem treats health as capacity to establish different physiological norms, rather than conformity to one statistical average. This is direct prior art for norm-generating capacity, principally in physiology and pathology; it is not a formal OEE result. [Canguilhem, “The Normal and the Pathological,” reproduced chapter, pp. 131–133](https://www.ias-research.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Canguilhem_Normal_Pathological.pdf)

Tahar explicitly proposes evolutionary norms as historically produced constraints that in turn shape evolution through organismal interactions. The proposal is conceptual and uses biological cases; it is not a general dynamical algorithm for deriving all future norms. [Tahar 2022](https://philpapers.org/archive/TAHBCA.pdf)

The organizational tradition is not uniformly committed to a fixed organization. Montévil and colleagues distinguish organizational changes, including the integration of new constraints. They separate a new constraint being maintained from its contributing to maintenance; either can precede the other. Their principle of variation is a theoretical postulate, not a proved impossibility of fixed-state mathematical representation. [Montévil et al. 2016, §4](https://montevil.org/publications/articles/2016-mmp-theoretical-principles-variation/)

**Fact / biological evidence:** Ancestral reconstruction of the V-ATPase ring supports a transition from two paralogous components to three through duplication and complementary loss of interfaces. Both daughter components became obligatory, without apparent acquisition of a new function. Thus evolution can change which component losses are consequential even while the machine's overarching task persists. [Finnigan et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3979732/)

The Black Queen account offers an ecological counterpart: adaptive loss of costly, leaky functions can make organisms dependent on other organisms providing those functions. This is an existing explanatory hypothesis and model, not a newly established universal history of microbial dependencies. [Morris, Lenski & Zinser 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3315703/)

Changes of beneficiary also have prior art: multilevel evolutionary models address the emergence of higher-level individuality and reproductive division of labor. They specify tradeoffs and selection conditions, rather than derive a unique norm system from unconstrained physics. [Michod 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1876437/)

**Interpretation / what changes:**

| Change | Assessment |
|---|---|
| New value under one assay | Does not establish a new requirement |
| Changed response or regulatory priority | Can be endogenous but use an existing repertoire |
| New dependency for maintaining an existing function | A real organizational change; can reflect entrenchment or loss of independence |
| New function or interaction used in maintenance | Stronger than a numerical improvement, but requires a justified functional comparison |
| New unit whose continuation is at issue | Changes the beneficiary; not automatically replacement of all lower-level interests |

**Fact / mathematical caution:** The notation $v_t\rightarrow v_{t+1}$ versus $V_t\rightarrow V_{t+1}$ is coherent only after fixing the representation. If $V_\theta(T)=-(T-\theta)^2$, an optimum moving from $37$ to $38$ changes the function of $T$, although it is merely a parameter change in the fixed family $V(T,\theta)$. An explicit change of $V$ therefore does not prove creation of a new kind of norm.

**Interpretation:** A changing dependency network is a defensible object of investigation, but its change need not constitute functional expansion. Maintaining organization remains a fixed higher-level reference in many theories while the concrete conditions realizing it change. Developmental changes of organization also do not automatically establish evolutionary novelty: an inherited life cycle can repeatedly traverse them.

The schematic $(X_t,N_t)\rightarrow(X_{t+1},N_{t+1})$ is therefore not adopted as an additional state ontology. Existing constraint networks, regulatory regimes, and selection histories express more specific claims. If norms are derived from these, adding $N_t$ without a derivation would merely name the unresolved problem. Conversely, encoding their evolution inside a larger fixed dynamical description would not show that local requirements remained unchanged.

## 8. Counterexamples

| Requested case | Strongest evidence or construction | Boundary clarified |
|---|---|---|
| **A: organizational closure without OEE** | **Logical counterexample:** a mutually regenerating organization with a fixed repertoire and no mechanism of heritable innovation satisfies maintenance conditions without an evolutionary novelty process. Autonomy and OEE are separately required in Ruiz-Mirazo et al.'s proposed definition of life. [Ruiz-Mirazo et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15068038/) | Closure of maintenance does not entail OEE. This does not assert that real organisms are evolutionarily frozen. |
| **B: internally grounded viability without changing criteria** | The fixed forager in §5 and the self-reconstituting autogen in §5 provide operational/theoretical maintenance cases. | Grounding what matters now does not demonstrate evolving concerns. Neither is presented here as proof of full biological autonomy. |
| **C: increasing semantic information without new possibilities** | The finite binary-sensor construction below increases the measured amount of semantic information within one fixed survival task. | Information increase is insufficient for creation of new functional kinds. |
| **D: new selected-effect function within a fixed menu** | Avida's evolved EQU behavior acquired a history of selection in an experiment with specified logic-task rewards. [Lenski et al. 2003](https://cse.msu.edu/~ofria/pubs/2003LenskiEtAl.pdf) | Historical grounding of a trait's new function does not require an expanding task vocabulary. The experiment does not prove every future capacity unchanged. |
| **E: apparent autonomy with descriptive dependence** | Constraint redescription and candle-flame objections (§3); Bich also recognizes unresolved symbiosis/environment boundary problems. [Bich 2024, *Biological Organization*, §8](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/biological-organization/8F618A9F6912A03B3A2AC8D6D754D53D) | Physical self-maintenance does not guarantee a unique inferred organization. The criticism remains disputed. |
| **F: multiple endogenous references in the same dynamics** | Nested organizational levels and multilevel selection (§§3, 7) admit different beneficiaries. **Logical illustration:** the same resource competition sustains two organisms whose persistence requirements conflict. | Both references can be physically grounded. This is not two incompatible causal predictions for the same fully specified beneficiary and assay. |

**Constructed counterexample C / deduction, not a published experiment:** Let an equiprobable environmental bit $b$ be sensed as $a$, with accuracy $p>1/2$. A fixed physical rule allows persistence through the episode exactly when $a=b$. Use survival probability as the viability assay and the stored-information intervention that either retains the distinction between the two environmental states or fully scrambles it. Actual viability is $p$; scrambled viability is $1/2$. Preserving actual viability requires retaining the binary distinction, so the semantic amount is

$$
I_{\mathrm{sem}}=I(a;b)=1-H_2(p).
$$

Here $H_2$ is binary entropy. Improving an allowed sensor variant from $p=3/4$ to $p=1$ increases semantic information. The two environmental states, responses, and survival rule remain fixed. A finite hereditary repertoire containing just these variants can end at the better variant with no further variant available. The example uses the conditional semantic assay; it does not derive that assay or claim a new OEE mechanism.

**Further counterexample:** The V-ATPase result (§7) defeats the attractive inference “new necessity implies a new function.” A new requirement can be a consequence of complementary losses. Counting requirements would then count some losses of independence as gains of openness.

**Evidence limits:** No empirical comparison was found establishing two universally valid intrinsic norm systems for one fixed beneficiary under identical assumptions. F supports plural, beneficiary-relative relevance. No reviewed study establishes that changing norms is sufficient or necessary for OEE.

## 9. Remaining explanatory gap

**Interpretation / narrowed result:** The literature gives a partial naturalization of targets:

- Material organization grounds dependencies and failure modes.
- Regulation grounds distinctions that the system acts upon.
- Selection and organizational history explain why some effects acquire or retain significance.
- Evolution can change dependencies and the entities whose maintenance or reproduction matters.

What remains unsettled is the passage from these facts to a reproducible classification of **new evolutionary relevance kinds**. A theory must justify the beneficiary across organizational change, distinguish a new requirement from new means of satisfying an old one, and distinguish new dependence from newly enabled capacity. Current work addresses each issue in part; the combination is not established by the sources examined.

This gap is narrower than “biology has no endogenous norms,” which the search does not support. It is also narrower than demanding a scalar goal uniquely recoverable from all physical dynamics. Relational, system-specific significance can be scientifically constrained without one privileged norm for the entire world.

The answer to the pass is consequently **yes for historically changing, materially grounded relevance; only partially for a general formal account of evolving significance criteria; unresolved for an intrinsic criterion of new evolutionary kinds**. The stronger claim “OEE requires evolving norms” remains unadopted.

## 10. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

**Known / ESTABLISHED within cited scopes**

- Autopoiesis, adaptivity, organizational function, regulatory control, and selected-effect function are distinct accounts, not interchangeable names.
- Published autonomy accounts permit changing viability sets and organizations; they do not all assume fixed concrete requirements.
- Evolutionary norm change has explicit prior art, including Canguilhem's norm-generating physiology, evolutionary-constraint accounts, and organizational variation.
- Empirical ancestral reconstruction demonstrates new obligatory components without apparent new function.
- Semantic measures retain specified viability assumptions; proposed optimization of boundaries does not itself establish a unique beneficiary.

**PARTIALLY EXPLORED**

- Grounding beneficial/harmful distinctions in organization and regulation.
- Extending organization across development, heredity, symbiosis, and higher-level individuality.
- Distinguishing regulation within a repertoire from changing the repertoire itself.

**UNDEREXPLORED:** Not established. Direct prior art and active criticism were found, including a 2026 paper close to the core distinction.

**POTENTIALLY NOVEL:** No claim retained. Neither changing norms nor changing organizational constraints is a new idea.

**UNKNOWN**

- Whether a sufficiently general, representation-robust criterion can distinguish newly enabling relevance from entrenchment while tracking the relevant beneficiary.
- Whether any resulting norm-change criterion is necessary for a specified stronger form of OEE.

No genuinely new falsifiable candidate is justified. [HYPOTHESES.md](HYPOTHESES.md) is unchanged.

## 11. Highest-information next question

**How can causal analysis distinguish an endogenously acquired dependency that entrenches an existing function from one that enables a new heritable functional capacity, without counting every dependency change as innovation?**

This follows from the strongest counterexample, rather than assuming norm evolution or possibility-space expansion is the answer. Stop after this pass.
