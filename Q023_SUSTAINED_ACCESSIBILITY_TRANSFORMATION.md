# Q023 — Sustained accessibility transformation

Theoretical research pass, 2026-09-08. Scope: one synthesis-based frontier selection and prior-art audit of F3. This pass does not implement or simulate anything, does not reopen the Fernandes instrumentation branch, and does not perform Q024.

Epistemic convention: literature-supported statements are **Fact**. Cross-literature boundary judgments are **Interpretation**. The causal comparison below is an **analysis specification**, not a hypothesis.

## 1. Frontier selection

**Selected frontier: F3 — sustained accessibility transformation.**

F5 was excluded as **CLOSED — EXISTING THEORY SUFFICIENT**. F3 is the highest-ranked surviving unblocked mechanism family in SYNTHESIS_002_Q013_Q021.md. It outranks the next two alternatives:

This selection is global rather than a continuation of Q022's ecological topic.

## 2. Research question

> Do established theories of evolvability and evolutionary innovation explain a causal process that repeatedly preserves resource-feasible access to adaptive heritable variation when the organization generating variation changes, or do they explain only bounded, target-relative gains?

## 3. Why it matters for OEE

**Interpretation.** OEE requires more than producing one innovation or increasing evolvability once. If an access-enhancing organization works only for a fixed target family, disappears when immediate selection changes, or becomes an entrenched constraint after one transition, it cannot explain sustained anti-closure.

The relevant implication is one-way:

\[
\text{sustained OEE}
\Longrightarrow
\text{recurrently available viable evolutionary continuation},
\]

not

\[
\text{one evolvability gain}
\Longrightarrow
\text{OEE}.
\]

Taylor explicitly includes commonly available viable mutational pathways and continued evolutionary drive among proposed OEE requirements. Tokyo Type 2 OEE names ongoing evolution of evolvability, but naming the hallmark does not identify the dynamics that maintain it. [Taylor 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07403), [Packard et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00291)

## 4. Strongest prior art

The strongest absorption candidates are:

| Prior art | Strongest relevant claim | Scope limit |
|---|---|---|
| evolution of evolvability | Mutation, recombination, regulatory architecture, development, and genotype–phenotype maps can evolve so that adaptive heritable variation becomes more likely. | Effects are population-, environment-, timescale-, and linkage-dependent. |
| facilitated variation | Conserved core processes, weak regulatory linkage, compartmentation, exploratory processes, and reduced pleiotropy can convert small genetic changes into viable coordinated phenotypic changes. | Demonstrations generalize mainly across challenges sharing a supplied generative structure. |
| modularity and developmental organization | Structured environmental variation or connection costs can select or incidentally produce modular architectures that adapt faster. | Modularity has no universal sign or uniquely adaptive origin, and the modeled task decomposition is declared in advance. |
| robustness and neutral networks | Robust populations can accumulate neutral or cryptic diversity and encounter more phenotypic neighborhoods. | Robustness can facilitate or impede adaptation depending on population size, mutation rate, recombination, and landscape structure. |
| potentiation and cryptic variation | Prior substitutions or hidden diversity can make a later innovation accessible and can open alternative adaptive peaks. | The effect can be innovation-specific and does not establish repeated broadening. |
| evolvability modifiers | Mutators and other modifiers can spread by linkage to the beneficial variants they generate or through direct effects. | Recombination can break the association; deleterious load and direct costs can reverse the advantage. |
| evolving genetic architecture | Pleiotropy, gene duplication/deletion, regulatory topology, and developmental processes can themselves change rather than remain fixed parameters. | This absorbs “the map changes” but does not imply indefinite preservation of useful access. |
| scalable composition | Reuse of already adapted components can prevent search difficulty from rising in recursively compositional tasks. | The canonical result uses a scale-invariant, predefined hierarchical problem family and supplied composition operation. |
| evolved open-endedness / Type 2 OEE | Mechanisms contributing to open-endedness can themselves be evolutionary outcomes; ongoing evolvability is an explicit OEE category. | These frameworks classify a historical pattern or research target; they do not provide a general retention mechanism. |
| evolutionary generalization | Past structured challenges can shape architectures that perform on related unseen challenges. | “Unseen” does not mean outside the declared challenge-generating distribution or representation. |

Wagner and Altenberg made the genotype–phenotype map and modular genetic architecture central to the evolution of evolvability. Hansen later reviewed genetic architecture as a dynamical evolutionary variable. Payne and Wagner's contemporary synthesis identifies diversity-generating mechanisms, robustness, and landscape topology as major causes and emphasizes that evolvability itself can evolve. [Wagner & Altenberg 1996](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1996.tb02339.x), [Hansen 2006](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.37.091305.110224), [Payne & Wagner 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-018-0069-z)

The strongest constructive results go beyond fixed mutation rates. Alternating environments can evolve gene-regulatory networks that preserve robustness while making a small class of mutations switch efficiently between adaptive expression states. Modularly varying goals can evolve weak linkage, reduced pleiotropy, modularity, and generalization to new combinations, and structured variation can generate modular network organization. [Crombach & Hogeweg 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000112), [Parter, Kashtan & Alon 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000206), [Kashtan & Alon 2005](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0503610102)

## 5. What prior art already explains

**Fact.** Existing theory and experiments establish that:

1. evolvability has multiple causal determinants rather than one scalar substrate property;
2. selection and drift can alter those determinants;
3. genetic architecture and developmental maps can evolve, not merely phenotype values;
4. robustness can maintain neutral or cryptic variation that later accelerates adaptation;
5. past environmental regularities can be encoded as modular developmental biases that generalize to related new challenges;
6. mutation-rate and other modifiers can receive indirect selection through future beneficial variants;
7. prior mutations can specifically potentiate a later innovation;
8. reuse and composition can reduce search cost for recursively structured problem families;
9. sustained or ongoing evolution of evolvability is already an explicit OEE research category.

Gerhart and Kirschner identify versatile elements, weak linkage, compartmentation, redundancy, and exploratory behavior as mechanisms that reduce lethal coupling while permitting coordinated variation. Draghi and colleagues show formally that mutational robustness can either impede or facilitate adaptation. Zheng and colleagues experimentally show that cryptic variation can supply otherwise inaccessible stepping stones to multiple adaptive peaks. [Gerhart & Kirschner 1998](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC33871/), [Draghi et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08694), [Zheng, Payne & Wagner 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax1837)

**Interpretation.** Q023 must not redescribe modularity, neutral networks, potentiation, changing genotype–phenotype maps, or evolution of evolvability as a new mechanism.

## 6. Prior-art absorption test

Adversarial claim:

\[
\text{F3 is completely contained in evolution of evolvability}
+\text{ facilitated variation}
+\text{ scalable composition}.
\]

The claim succeeds for three important subcases:

1. **Fixed representation and declared target distribution.** Evolvability, landscape, modifier, neutral-network, and first-passage theory already describe changes in accessible adaptive variation.
2. **One evolving map or developmental architecture.** Evolution of genetic architecture, regulatory-network models, and facilitated variation already explain how the generator of variation changes.
3. **Repeated search over a supplied recursive family.** Scalable-composition models show how adapted components can be reused at successive predefined levels.

It fails only for a stricter conjunction:

1. the variation-generating organization changes more than once;
2. each changed organization remains causally responsible for resource-feasible access to later adaptive variation;
3. the later challenges are not merely new combinations sampled from the same supplied subgoal alphabet;
4. direct costs, specialization, recombination, drift, and entrenchment are allowed to remove the access-enhancing organization;
5. no fixed hierarchy or externally guaranteed sequence of environmental targets supplies recurrence.

**Interpretation.** “Ongoing evolution of evolvability” names this general direction, and “evolved open-endedness” recognizes that enabling mechanisms can be products of evolution. Neither provides a general dynamical explanation for the five-clause conjunction. The frontier is therefore not fully absorbed, but its residual is much narrower than “how does evolvability evolve?”

## 7. Strongest counterexamples

| Case | Counterexample | What it defeats |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Gene-regulatory networks evolve robustness and rapid switching under two or three alternating targets. | Maintained evolvability across many generations does not imply access beyond a fixed target set and encoding. |
| **B** | Modularly varying goals produce generalization to unseen combinations drawn from the same subgoals. | Held-out performance does not establish access after the generative basis itself changes. |
| **C** | Mutational robustness facilitates adaptation in some population/landscape regimes and impedes it in others. | Robustness has no universal positive causal sign. |
| **D** | A mutator hitchhikes with beneficial mutations in an asexual population but is separated by recombination or burdened by deleterious mutations. | Selection for evolvability does not guarantee retention. |
| **E** | The LTEE citrate innovation required historical potentiation specific to citrate use. | A dramatic access transformation can be narrow, contingent, and one-off. |
| **F** | Cryptic genetic variation opens several adaptive peaks for one assayed molecular phenotype. | Multiple accessible outcomes within one assay do not establish long-depth scope. |
| **G** | Recursive symbiotic composition remains scalable on a supplied scale-invariant hierarchical landscape. | Indefinite performance in a predefined hierarchy does not explain endogenous continuation. |
| **H** | Diminishing-returns epistasis reduces the effects of beneficial mutations as adaptation proceeds in a constant environment. | Ordinary adaptation need not preserve its own future rate or benefit distribution. |
| **I** | A system can satisfy permissive formal OEE conditions while producing only simple repetitive or low-complexity novelty. | Indefinite activity or formal openness does not validate sustained adaptive accessibility. |
| **J** | A major transition produces a new unit while reducing lower-level autonomy and exposing new coordination costs. | Organizational transformation has no guaranteed positive effect on later access. |

The direct evidence is correspondingly conditional. The LTEE replay established citrate-specific potentiation, not a general increase in innovability. Microbial evolvability studies emphasize that indirect benefits depend on linkage and environment, while experiments in constant environments show declining adaptability through diminishing-returns epistasis. Watson and Pollack's scale result assumes a supplied hierarchical landscape, and Hintze demonstrates that permissive OEE conditions can be satisfied by an evolutionarily uninteresting system. [Blount, Borland & Lenski 2008](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0803151105), [Díaz Arenas & Cooper 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/1574-6976.12008), [Wünsche et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-016-0061), [Watson & Pollack 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0303-2647(02)00135-1), [Hintze 2019](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00289)

## 8. Surviving conceptual distinction

A useful distinction survives between:

\[
\boxed{\text{bounded evolvability gain}}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\text{long-depth retention of broad-scope evolvability}}.
\]

A bounded gain improves access for a specified population, perturbation operator, environment or target family, timescale, and resource budget. Long-depth retention would require a sequence of locally validated gains after the developmental or hereditary organization defining variation changes.

“Broad-scope” does not mean target-free. It means that a determinant performs across a declared range of held-out challenges whose variation is not exhausted by replaying the selected targets. Riederer and colleagues' distinction between general and specific evolvability, and between broad- and narrow-scope determinants, provides established terminology for this part of the boundary. [Riederer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2022.01.004)

The extra Q023 content is the conjunction of **repetition**, **retention under countervailing forces**, and **change in the organization producing variation**. No new name is introduced for it.

## 9. Formalization status

**Established within a fixed stage.** Given a stochastic evolutionary process, target event \(T\), initial ensemble, and resource functional, access can be represented by a first-passage distribution. Evolvability can also be represented by a distribution of mutational fitness effects or by response to a declared selection gradient. These are conditional scientific objects.

**Partially formalized across stages.** Let \(M_k\) be an access-relevant organization at stage \(k\), \(\mathcal T_k\) a locally declared held-out challenge family, \(B_k\) a resource budget, and \(\tau_{\mathcal T_k}\) first passage to an adaptive member of that family. A local causal audit could ask whether

\[
\Pr\!\left(
\tau_{\mathcal T_k}\le B_k
\mid \operatorname{do}(M_k=\text{intact})
\right)
>
\Pr\!\left(
\tau_{\mathcal T_k}\le B_k
\mid \operatorname{do}(M_k=\text{ablated or ancestral})
\right).
\]

Repeating this comparison for several \(k\) does not require one universal phenotype space. It establishes only a chain of domain-specific causal effects.

**Not formalized universally.** No common target catalogue, evolutionary clock, resource currency, or organizational identity spans arbitrary changes in heredity and individuality. Q010 already established that cross-transition comparison is partial and that unmatched novelty classes must remain undefined. The expression above is an analysis specification, not a universal OEE measure and not a generating mechanism.

## 10. Observer / representation dependence

The residual is conditional on:

- what counts as the evolving entity and its descendants;
- which developmental or hereditary organization is manipulated;
- the mutation, recombination, developmental, and ecological perturbation operators;
- the challenge distribution and the meaning of “held out”;
- viability, adaptation, establishment, and persistence criteria;
- resource budget and time horizon;
- causal correspondence across successive stages.

An invertible recoding should not change a correctly transported first-passage result. A new coarse-graining, target family, or generation clock can. Generalization across new combinations of old subgoals may look broad under one description and narrow under a richer one.

**Interpretation.** Observer dependence prevents a universal scalar ordering, but it does not erase a causal effect after the variables, interventions, and scope are declared. The mechanism frontier is conditional rather than observer-free.

## 11. Mechanistic status

**A distinct but conditional mechanism gap survives.**

Established mechanisms explain how access can increase:

- variation supply changes;
- robustness permits neutral exploration;
- modularity reduces pleiotropic interference;
- developmental organization biases viable variation;
- environmental regularities select generalizable organization;
- prior variants potentiate later innovations;
- composition reuses adapted components.

They do not establish a general process that repeatedly retains such organizations when:

1. their benefits are delayed and indirect;
2. immediate selection favors specialization or lower cost;
3. linkage to the beneficial descendants can be broken;
4. the relevant developmental or hereditary organization changes;
5. the next challenge is not guaranteed to reuse the old decomposition.

This is not a claim that biology needs one universal evolvability mechanism. It is the narrower absence of an established causal account connecting successive local access-preserving episodes without presupposing their target sequence. Candidate ingredients exist; a sufficient dynamical relation among them does not.

The result is not **EMPIRICALLY BLOCKED** at this stage: prior-art review can establish the mechanism boundary. Testing any proposed mechanism would later require data, but no specific empirical case is made the decisive Q023 question.

\[
\boxed{\textbf{NONE}}

Deep learning contribution: none.

Learning-theoretic language usefully distinguishes memorization of past targets from generalization to a challenge distribution. Parter and colleagues already operationalized that abstraction in evolutionary models. Frank's contemporary proposal that overparameterized regulation may increase biological generalization is a testable analogy, but it presupposes a challenge distribution and does not explain repeated retention after that distribution or the developmental organization changes. It adds no necessary abstraction to Q023. [Parter, Kashtan & Alon 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000206), [Frank 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/evolut/qpag111)

## 13. Central decision

\[
\boxed{\textbf{DISTINCT MECHANISM GAP}}
\]

The generic parts of F3 are established evolvability theory. The surviving gap is not “evolution can improve evolvability,” “variation can be modular,” or “viable paths matter.” It is the absence of an established process that makes broad-scope access-enhancing organization repeatedly retained and causally effective through successive changes in the organization of variation, without a supplied target hierarchy.

## 14. Mechanism readiness

\[
\boxed{\textbf{CONDITIONALLY READY}}
\]

The target is scientifically meaningful only under locally declared entities, interventions, challenge families, and resource budgets. Serial local causal comparisons can avoid demanding a universal cross-ontology metric. Readiness is not **READY** because no invariant scope criterion, general retention dynamics, or specific causal structure has survived strongly enough to support a hypothesis.

## 15. Remaining explanatory gap

The narrow gap is:

> What evolutionary dynamics keep a costly or fragile, broad-scope determinant of evolvability associated with the descendants that benefit from it, and re-establish that association after the developmental or hereditary organization generating variation changes?

Modifier theory explains linkage and indirect selection within a declared regime. Facilitated variation explains generalization under structured environmental recurrence. Composition explains scaling under a supplied recursive decomposition. The unexplained conjunction is retention plus renewed causal efficacy across changes of regime without those structures being guaranteed in advance.

## 16. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

- **ESTABLISHED:** evolvability has multiple determinants; genetic architecture and developmental maps evolve; robustness, modularity, cryptic variation, potentiation, modifiers, and composition can improve access under specified conditions; Type 2 OEE names ongoing evolution of evolvability.
- **PARTIALLY EXPLORED:** selection for broad- versus narrow-scope evolvability; evolutionary generalization beyond experienced environments; how modifier effects depend on linkage, recombination, population size, environmental change, and direct costs.
- **UNDEREXPLORED:** causal retention of broad-scope evolvability determinants across successive changes in developmental or hereditary organization, assessed through a chain of local resource-bounded comparisons rather than one universal metric.
- **POTENTIALLY NOVEL:** no specific causal relation is justified. The exact conjunction above may be underexplored, but its components and several close models are established.
- **UNKNOWN:** whether any domain exhibits repeated endogenous retention under the stated controls; whether one general process exists; whether heterogeneous domain-specific mechanisms are sufficient for natural OEE.

## 17. Hypothesis decision

\boxed{\textbf{NO HYPOTHESIS JUSTIFIED}}
\]

No specific structure–effect relation passes the prior-art, distinctness, and counterexample tests. The surviving gap is mechanistic but currently identifies a missing explanandum, not a candidate explanation. HYPOTHESES.md remains unchanged.
