# Q011 — Evolutionary Regime Bridging

One theoretical research pass, 2026-09-07. Scope: continuity of evolution while heredity, reproduction, selection, and individuality change. No implementation and no complete OEE theory.

## 1. Problem statement

Q010 found that accessibility is only partially comparable across changes in evolutionary ontology. That measurement result leaves a mechanistic question: how can such a change occur without evolution stopping while the old entities cease to be the primary evolutionary units and the new entities are not yet full Darwinian individuals?

The apparent circularity is real but not fatal. A nascent collective need not begin with autonomous reproduction, a distinct inheritance substrate, and conflict-free individuality. It needs some process that generates variation among collectives, differential persistence or reproduction, and sufficient parent–offspring resemblance for collective-level selection to have an effect. Those minimal properties can initially be consequences of lower-level reproduction, a simple life cycle, spatial ecology, or an externally imposed grouping/dispersal regime. Selection at the old and new levels can then overlap while the collective acquires more reliable development and reproduction.

**Interpretation:** continuity is ordinarily supplied by temporal and causal overlap, not by an instantaneous switch between complete evolutionary regimes. This answers the one-transition bootstrap problem only conditionally: it does not establish one universal transition mechanism or an ability to repeat transitions indefinitely.

## 2. Major transitions prior art

**Fact:** major-transition and evolutionary-transition-in-individuality (ETI) theories already study the formation of new units of reproduction and selection from previously independent entities. The literature distinguishes fraternal transitions among similar units from egalitarian transitions among unlike units, and changes in individuality from changes in hereditary information systems. It also recognizes origin, maintenance, and transformation as distinguishable phases. [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112), [West et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4547252/)

The transition paradox is resolved in several established ways:

1. **Ecological individuation:** spatial or demographic structure supplies collective discreteness, reproduction, and heredity before these are internally produced.
2. **Life-cycle individuation:** attachment, incomplete separation, propagules, fission, or bottlenecks make collectives reproduce before they are highly integrated organisms.
3. **Multilevel overlap:** lower-level reproduction produces the material on which between-collective selection acts; collective adaptations can then modify lower-level reproduction and development.
4. **Conflict mediation and fate alignment:** bottlenecks, clonality, policing, fair transmission, division of labour, and developmental control can reduce disruptive within-collective selection. These are alternative or complementary mechanisms, not a universal ordered checklist. [Michod 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2540.2001.00808.x), [Ratcliff et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5665808/)

MLS1 and MLS2 sharpen the diachronic change. In MLS1, collective context changes the fitness of lower-level particles and collective fitness is commonly an aggregate of particle output; the collective need not itself reproduce. In MLS2, collectives have differential survival or reproduction and function as evolutionary units. ETIs can be studied as movement toward MLS2 while MLS1 and lower-level selection remain present. This is a transition in the organization of selection, not selection suddenly appearing from nothing. [Okasha 2006](https://academic.oup.com/book/3059/chapter-abstract/143818664), [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112)

**Known boundary:** cooperation, high relatedness, division of labour, conflict suppression, and fitness alignment are recurrent ingredients, but their necessity depends on the transition. Germ–soma differentiation is not required at the origin of simple multicellularity; egalitarian symbioses face transmission and coordination problems different from clonal multicellularity; and “fitness transfer” is a disputed metaphor rather than a conserved quantity. Trade-off breaking and explicit multilevel covariance are more precise in particular models. [Bourrat et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.73715)

## 3. Ecological scaffolding

**Fact:** ecological scaffolding is a direct solution to the bootstrap problem. Black, Bourrat, and Rainey's model uses bounded resource patches, within-patch growth, patch extinction, and dispersal to impose collective-like birth, death, variation, and heredity on cell populations. Lower-level traits thereby affect differential patch reproduction even though the cells have not already evolved an autonomous collective life cycle. [Black, Bourrat & Rainey 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-019-1086-9)

What this explains is precise: an environment can supply enough Darwinian structure for selection on collective traits to begin. It does not by itself establish a new organism. In the original model the collective properties are ecological consequences; remove the patch regime and the imposed collective birth/death process disappears. The same limitation applies to laboratory protocols that manually alternate group and propagule phases: they can demonstrate adaptation under a collective life cycle without showing that the evolved collective reconstructs that life cycle unaided.

Ecological scaffolding is therefore not synonymous with an ETI. It is an initiation mechanism whose outcome may remain scaffold-dependent, collapse, or become endogenized.

## 4. Endogenization

**Fact:** “endogenization” is already the appropriate term for evolved retention of properties first supplied by an ecological scaffold. Bourrat's extension of the patch model allows an evolved growth–dispersal trade-off to make collective traits resilient when the scaffold is lifted. Doulcier and colleagues formalize this as evolutionary hysteresis: after sufficient evolution under a scaffold, changing or removing it leaves the system in the basin of a stable collective-trait state that the ancestral population could not maintain. [Bourrat 2025](https://doi.org/10.1086/719118), [Doulcier et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50625-1)

Persistence after removal of the *initiating* scaffold is consequently a strong test within this research program. It separates temporary externally supplied Darwinian properties from evolved internal organization. It is not a universal requirement that the new entity reproduce without any environment: every reproducer depends on environmental resources and conditions. The relevant counterfactual is whether the particular grouping, sorting, or dispersal operation that constituted higher-level selection must still be supplied from outside.

Endogenization can involve evolved propagule production, a developmental switch, reconstruction of group composition, stable boundaries, or a trade-off that sustains a collective phenotype. It need not imply all of the following at once:

* material independence from lower-level heredity;
* elimination of lower-level selection;
* a germ–soma distinction;
* organism-like autonomy in every sense;
* increased capacity for subsequent novelty.

Snowflake yeast retained multicellular clustering through 35 transfers without gravitational settling selection, providing limited empirical evidence of trait persistence after the initiating selection was removed. It does not by itself demonstrate a fully autonomous new evolutionary regime. [Ratcliff et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1115323109)

**Interpretation:** scaffold removal is a valuable completion assay for scaffold dependence, not an accepted necessary-and-sufficient criterion for every major transition.

## 5. Emergence of new heredity

A distinct higher-level inheritance *system* need not begin as a new molecular inheritance medium. Three cases must be separated.

1. **Correlated collectives from lower-level heredity.** Clonal growth and a single-cell bottleneck transmit cell genotypes and can immediately generate parent–offspring resemblance in collective traits. Models show that collective-trait heritability can appear without prior selection for a separate group genome. [Black et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10454505/)
2. **Collective developmental heredity.** A reproducible life cycle can reconstruct composition, shape, or division of labour from a propagule. Here what is inherited is a developmental capacity or attractor, even though its material carriers remain lower-level genes and cells.
3. **Compositional or community heredity.** In multispecies droplets, ecological interactions and developmental correction can evolve to make offspring communities resemble parents. This is collective-level heredity causally organized through lower-level reproduction, not mere sampling correlation. [Doulcier et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.53433)

Higher-level heredity becomes scientifically distinct when parent collectives generate offspring collectives whose relevant traits recur with sufficient fidelity and whose between-collective differences can respond to selection. This is a causal and dynamical distinction, not a demand for a physically separate code. Bottlenecks, fair representation of symbionts, policing, and developmental correction are different solutions to the fidelity problem. Epigenetic inheritance systems can also help stabilize developmental organization, but they are not a universal bridge. [Jablonka & Lamb 1998](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1420-9101.1998.11020159.x)

**Conclusion:** inheritance-system transformation is one family of bridge mechanisms. It does not supply a general sequence from every old ontology to every new one.

## 6. Emergence of new reproduction and individuality

**Fact:** reproduction can precede full individuality. “Stay-together” growth, incomplete cell separation, fragmentation, fission, and bottlenecked propagules generate nascent collective life cycles before strong integration or reproductive division of labour. A life cycle creates recurring collective parent–offspring relations and exposes collective traits to selection; conflict control and development can then evolve. [Ratcliff et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5665808/)

The reverse ordering is also possible in a weaker sense. Persistent, functionally integrated associations can exist before they possess high-fidelity collective reproduction or a distinct collective heredity. Aggregative “come-together” groups and egalitarian symbioses often require later evolution of transmission, partner assortment, or reproductive coordination. Experimentally imposed two-phase life cycles can produce collective adaptation and a developmental switch while still depending on the experimenter to execute group reproduction. [Hammerschmidt et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13884)

There is therefore no supported universal chain such as

\[
\text{collectivity}\rightarrow\text{reproduction}\rightarrow
\text{individuality}\rightarrow\text{inheritance}.
\]

Which property appears first depends on whether groups form clonally or aggregatively, whether constituents are similar or dissimilar, how dispersal occurs, and what the ecology supplies. “Reproduction scaffolds individuality” is established for life-cycle-first routes, but is not a universal prerequisite ordering.

## 7. Multilevel overlap during transition

The most general continuity supplied by ETI theory is coexistence of processes at multiple levels:

\[
S_{\mathrm{low}}
\longrightarrow
(S_{\mathrm{low}},S_{\mathrm{collective}})
\longrightarrow
\text{stronger }S_{\mathrm{collective}},
\]

not literal disappearance of lower-level selection. During the overlap, particle births create collective growth, collective dispersal or extinction changes particle lineage success, and evolved collective development feeds back on particle division. Multilevel Price-equation or contextual analyses partition these effects; they do not require a metaphysical instant at which the level switches.

Several mechanisms can stabilize the overlap:

* clonality and bottlenecks align reproductive fates and reduce within-group variance;
* policing, apoptosis, fair meiosis, or partner control suppress lower-level cheaters;
* developmental control reconstructs the collective phenotype;
* propagule formation makes collective reproduction repeatable;
* division of labour can increase interdependence after a collective life cycle exists.

None is individually universal. Some conflict mediation or reliable transmission is usually required for a complex transition to persist, but clonality can reduce conflict without policing, simple multicellularity need not have germ and soma, and lower-level selfish variants can remain as cancer, meiotic drive, or symbiont conflict.

The changing-clock problem from Q010 also has a mechanistic answer without a universal clock. Cell divisions and collective life cycles coexist as nested event series. Early in a transition, collective “generations” may be environmentally imposed episodes; later, propagule production or fission defines an endogenous collective cycle. Evolutionary continuity is tracked through their coupling, not by declaring a cell generation and a collective generation numerically equivalent.

## 8. Loss and irreversibility

**Fact:** a major transition is a reorganization, not necessarily monotonic inclusion of possibilities. Formerly independent entities can lose independent reproduction, genes, metabolic capacities, or access to external environments while the new collective gains development, division of labour, and higher-level reproduction. Endosymbiotic organelles underwent extensive genome reduction and became dependent on nuclear gene products and protein import; somatic specialization can remove a cell lineage's contribution to organismal descendants. [Griesemer et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4547265/)

Thus the default abstraction should not be

\[
\mathcal O_t\subseteq\mathcal O_{t+1}.
\]

A transition can open some trajectories, close others, and redefine the entities to which reproduction and heredity apply. Claims of “expanded possibility” must state whose possibilities and which level are being compared.

Irreversibility is also contingent rather than definitional. Dependency, genome loss, division of labour, and developmental integration can make reversal increasingly difficult, but experimentally evolved multicellularity has been rapidly reversed under sufficiently strong selection. [Rebolleda-Gómez 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.13654)

**Workspace correction:** across major transitions, “endogenous transformation” or “reorganization with gains and losses” is more defensible than set-theoretic monotonic expansion.

## 9. Repeated transitions

Repeated and recursive transitions are prior art. Szathmáry explicitly discusses recurrent origins of multicellularity, plastid transitions, and recursive multicellularity, and calls for a theory covering origin, maintenance, and transformation. Eukaryotic conflict-mediation machinery has also been proposed as a precondition that made later independent origins of complex multicellularity more accessible. [Szathmáry 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421398112), [Blackstone 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3685466/)

That literature does not establish a generic recursion

\[
E_0\rightarrow E_1\rightarrow E_2\rightarrow\cdots
\]

in which completion of each transition reliably creates the initiation conditions for the next. Known transitions are explained using heterogeneous mechanisms: compartmentation and fair transmission, chromosome linkage, endosymbiosis, clonal life cycles, kin structure, policing, developmental regulation, or cultural inheritance. Some earlier transitions supply components later co-opted, but this is evidence for historical facilitation, not a general transition-producing operator.

**Interpretation:** “single-transition theory only” is too strong because repeated transitions and their historical dependencies are actively studied. Yet one successfully endogenized transition neither predicts another nor establishes OEE.

## 10. Endogenous scaffold generation

The distinction between exploiting a supplied scaffold and generating scaffolding conditions is real, but not terminologically new.

**Established adjacent theory:** niche construction and ecological inheritance describe organisms modifying environments and thereby altering selection for descendants. Social niche construction makes population structure and assortment themselves products of social traits. A recent “constructionist ecological scaffolding” path explicitly proposes that lower-level entities create environmental structure—such as persistent material or biofilm boundaries—that later functions as a scaffold for collective individuality. [Laland, Odling-Smee & Feldman 1996](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1420-9101.1996.9030293.x), [Ryan, Powers & Watson 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4686542/), [Bourrat et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.70661)

Consequently, the provisional loop

\[
O_t\rightarrow E_{t+1}\rightarrow S_{t+1}\rightarrow O_{t+1}
\]

is substantially niche construction plus ecological scaffolding and should not be renamed an “evolutionary bridge.” What remains thin is not the conceptual possibility but the demonstrated causal sequence: a prior evolutionary organization generates a scaffold, the scaffold initiates a genuinely new level of heredity/reproduction, and that level endogenizes the relevant properties after the initiating condition is removed or altered.

No reviewed general framework shows that this sequence repeats, that it increases overall evolvability, or that it is required for all transition types. Endogenous scaffold generation is therefore prior art with an underdeveloped repeated-transition application, not a justified new OEE mechanism.

## 11. Counterexamples

| Case | Strongest case found | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A — scaffolded Darwinian appearance without transition | In the original ecological-scaffolding model, patch birth/death and dispersal create collective selection, but collective properties disappear with the scaffold. | Higher-level selection-like dynamics do not establish autonomous individuality. |
| B — persistent external dependence | The experimentally imposed two-phase *Pseudomonas* life cycle enables collective adaptation and developmental switching, but the apparatus still performs collective reproduction. | Adaptation at a collective level need not endogenize its life cycle. |
| C — endogenization without further novelty | Hysteresis models can settle into a stable post-scaffold collective-trait equilibrium; snowflake-yeast persistence establishes retention, not continuing innovation. | Transition completion is not OEE. |
| D — individuality with reduced evolvability | Organelle integration can increase eukaryotic capacities while genome reduction destroys the endosymbiont's independent evolvability. | There is no observer-independent monotonic “overall evolvability” conclusion. |
| E — capacities open and close together | Germ–soma specialization opens organism-level functions while many somatic cells lose lineage-level reproduction; organelles lose autonomous capacities. | New ontologies are not supersets of old ones. |
| F — transitions not explained by ecological patch scaffolding | Genetic-code/chromosome transitions and egalitarian endosymbioses are treated using linkage, information transmission, partner control, and conflict mediation rather than the Black-style patch scaffold. | Ecological scaffolding is not a universal ETI mechanism; stretching “scaffold” to every enabling condition removes explanatory content. |
| G — prior evolution constructs later conditions | Social niche construction and constructionist ecological scaffolding provide explicit models; eukaryotic conflict mediation plausibly facilitated repeated multicellularity. | The route exists in prior art, but evidence for a complete, repeated generate–initiate–endogenize sequence remains limited. |

These counterexamples jointly reject three tempting inferences:

\[
\text{scaffolded selection}\not\Rightarrow\text{completed ETI},
\]

\[
\text{completed ETI}\not\Rightarrow\text{greater evolvability at every level},
\]

and

\[
\text{one endogenized transition}\not\Rightarrow\text{further transitions}.
\]

## 12. Central decision

**Decision: C — Fragmented bridge mechanisms.**

Existing ETI theory gives several coherent ways to maintain evolutionary continuity: externally supplied ecological scaffolds, nascent life cycles, clonal bottlenecks, multilevel selection, developmental reconstruction, conflict mediation, and evolved transmission. Scaffolding plus endogenization gives a particularly explicit account for one class of transitions, but it is not demonstrably general across fraternal and egalitarian transitions, changes in informational inheritance, or recurrent transitions.

Decision A is too strong because no sufficiently general bridge formalism was found. B is too strong because repeated and recursive transitions are established subjects. D overstates novelty because niche construction, social niche construction, and constructionist ecological scaffolding already address endogenous production of enabling conditions. E is false as a blanket claim: the bootstrap problem is substantive and the cited mechanisms explicitly solve it in bounded domains.

The term “evolutionary bridge” adds no established explanatory content beyond scaffolding, endogenization, nascent life cycles, and multilevel overlap, so it is not retained.

## 13. Remaining explanatory gap

The narrow gap is not how *any one* higher-level individual can originate. It is whether, and under what causal conditions, an endogenized evolutionary organization can itself produce the discreteness, transmission, life-cycle, or ecological structure that initiates a *subsequent* change in evolutionary individuality or inheritance.

Existing theory supplies components and historical examples, but no reviewed result demonstrates a general or repeatable generate–initiate–endogenize sequence. A useful test must distinguish:

* an external condition merely exploited by the system;
* ordinary niche construction that changes selection without creating a new evolutionary unit;
* a self-produced scaffold that causally initiates new-level heredity or reproduction;
* persistence of that new organization after the initiating scaffold changes;
* genuine further transition from mere adaptation within the first new regime.

This is a **theoretical and empirical integration gap**, not yet a new hypothesis and not evidence that one universal mechanism exists.

## 14. Known / Underexplored / Potentially Novel / Unknown

### Known / ESTABLISHED

* ETIs can proceed through overlapping lower- and higher-level selection rather than a discontinuous replacement.
* Ecological scaffolds and nascent life cycles can supply minimal collective reproduction and heredity before full individuality.
* Endogenization and post-scaffold evolutionary hysteresis are explicit prior art.
* Collective heritability can arise through lower-level inheritance, bottlenecks, and developmental reconstruction without a new molecular code.
* Transition orderings vary across clonal, aggregative, fraternal, and egalitarian cases.
* Major transitions reorganize possibilities and can eliminate lower-level autonomy; they are not necessarily monotonic expansions.
* Niche construction and social niche construction already cover organism-generated changes to future selective and population structure.

### Underexplored

* Complete causal demonstrations in which evolved organization constructs a scaffold, the scaffold initiates a new evolutionary level, and the new level then endogenizes its reproduction or heredity.
* Whether mechanisms that complete one transition systematically alter the probability of another, rather than merely being historically reusable components.
* How to compare alternative bridge mechanisms without presuming a universal order of Darwinian properties.

### Potentially Novel

No distinct mechanism or dynamical law passed the prior-art firewall. The generate–initiate–endogenize sequence is a useful synthesis of existing questions, but synthesis alone does not justify novelty.

### Unknown

* Whether any generic mechanism can produce repeated transitions across different kinds of inheritance and individuality.
* Whether self-generated scaffolding is necessary, sufficient, or neither for sustained transformational OEE.
* Whether repeated transitions tend to preserve, increase, or reduce long-run evolutionary accessibility.

**NO HYPOTHESIS JUSTIFIED**

The pass establishes a fragmented-theory boundary and corrects a monotonic-expansion abstraction. It does not establish a new falsifiable dynamical claim. `HYPOTHESES.md` therefore remains unchanged.
